## When the House Is Cold and the Furnace Isn't Working

The thermostat is calling for heat and nothing happens. Or the furnace clicks, runs for a minute and shuts off. Or the blower runs and the air coming out is cold.

It feels like the whole system has died, but a furnace has a short list of parts that cause most no-heat calls.

The ignitor lights the burner, and ignitors crack with age.

The flame sensor tells the furnace the burner is lit, and when it gets coated with soot, the furnace shuts the gas off as a safety step. A worn blower motor or a clogged filter keeps the heat from reaching the house, and can make the furnace overheat and shut itself down.

Each of those is a repair, and most of them are small ones.

## Check These Before You Call

Two minutes can save you a trip.

- Make sure the thermostat is set to heat and the temperature is above the room temperature. Replace the batteries if it has them.
- Check the furnace's power switch, which looks like a light switch on or near the furnace, and the breaker.
- Replace a clogged filter. A furnace that can't pull in air will overheat and shut down.
- Make sure the vents in the house are open and nothing is blocking them.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas company from outside. Don't try to relight anything.

If none of it helps, call us.
