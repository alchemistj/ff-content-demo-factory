## Look First, Then Tell You Straight

A heating and cooling system rarely fails all at once. Most calls start with one worn part: a capacitor, a relay, a fan motor, a sensor.

That matters, because a small part is a small repair. Before anyone talks about replacing a system, we find out what actually failed.

Then we tell you what it will cost to fix it and, if the system is old enough that replacing it is worth a look, what that would cost too. You decide with both numbers in front of you.

You pay for the work that was done.

## AC Repair

The outside unit is running, but the house won't cool. Or it cools for an hour and quits. Or the fan has stopped and the air coming out of the vents is warm.

In Wichita summers, the AC runs most of the day for months. Capacitors, contactors and fan motors wear out under that load, and a part that costs little can take the whole system down.

We find the part that failed, replace it and get the system running again. If the real trouble is bigger, we say so before we do anything.

## Furnace Repair

A cold house in January is not something to wait on. A furnace that won't light or won't stay on is usually a failed ignitor, a dirty flame sensor, a worn blower motor or a clogged filter.

Many of those are quick fixes, and we'll show you what we found so you know what you're paying for.

A furnace that's failing because of its age is a different conversation, and we'll have that one with you honestly too.

## Seasonal Checkups

Most people don't think about their system until it fails, usually on the hottest or coldest day of the year.
