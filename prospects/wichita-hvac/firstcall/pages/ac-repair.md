## When the AC Runs but the House Won't Cool

You walk by the outside unit and it's humming, or the fan isn't spinning at all. Inside, the air from the vents is warm, or it's cool for a while and then it isn't.

Those are the usual signs of a failed part. Wichita summers keep an AC running hard for months, and the parts that start and run the motors wear out first.

The capacitor gives the compressor and fan motors the jolt they need to start. A relay or contactor switches them on. When either one fails, the unit may hum without starting, or not run at all, even though the rest of the system is fine.

Fan motors seize. Drains clog and shut the system down. Dirty coils and filters choke the airflow until the evaporator freezes into a block of ice.

Most of these are repairs, not replacements.

## Repair or Replace: What We'll Tell You

Age matters more than anything else. An AC usually lasts 12 to 15 years, and one that's older than that, and breaking down every summer, may not be worth another repair.

Refrigerant matters too. Systems built to run R-22 refrigerant, which is no longer made, get more expensive to service every year, so a major repair on one is a harder choice.

If the repair is small and the system is sound, we repair it. If a replacement is coming, we tell you roughly when and what the options are, so you can plan for it instead of being surprised on the hottest day.

## Check These Before You Call

A few things take two minutes and sometimes solve it.

- Check that the thermostat is set to cool and the temperature is below the room temperature.
- Check that the breaker for the AC hasn't tripped.
- Replace a clogged filter. A filter that's full of dust can freeze the coil.
- Make sure the outside unit isn't buried in leaves, grass clippings or a fence's worth of weeds.

If the unit is covered in ice, turn the AC off and let it thaw before you call, so the ice doesn't keep damaging the system.

If none of it helps, call us.
