## The House Won't Warm Up

It might be a furnace that never starts, one that blows cold air, or one that runs a few minutes and shuts off. Or the house is warm in one room and cold in the next.

Each is a symptom, and each points to a short list of causes. Knowing the cause is the difference between a quick repair and a repeat visit.

## What Usually Goes Wrong With a Furnace

Most furnace calls come down to a few parts:

- **Dirty or clogged filter.** Restricted airflow makes the furnace overheat and shut itself off. It is the easiest fix and the most common cause.
- **Igniter or flame sensor.** If the burner lights and then goes out, a dirty flame sensor is a frequent cause. A cracked igniter means the burner never lights at all.
- **Blower motor.** The motor that pushes warm air through your ducts wears out. When it fails you get heat in the furnace and none in the house.
- **Thermostat.** A dead battery or bad setting can look like a failed furnace.
- **Pilot or gas supply.** On older furnaces, a pilot light that won't stay lit or a closed gas valve stops the heat before it starts.

A furnace repair should end with you knowing which part failed and why. That is how we work.

Tell us the symptom when you call, such as the burner clicking without lighting or the blower running with no warm air. It helps us bring the right parts to your door.

## Safety Comes First

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas company or 911 from outside. Don't flip switches or use the phone inside.

If a carbon monoxide alarm goes off, get everyone out and call 911. Furnaces with a cracked heat exchanger can leak carbon monoxide, which is why an odd smell, soot or a yellow flame is worth a call.

Never run a furnace you suspect is unsafe. Call us once everyone is safe.

## Repair or Replace?

A furnace with one failed part, like an igniter or a blower motor, is usually worth repairing. It's a small cost against a whole system.

Replacement becomes the better choice when the furnace is old, has repeated failures, or heats unevenly even when it's running properly. We'll tell you where yours stands.
