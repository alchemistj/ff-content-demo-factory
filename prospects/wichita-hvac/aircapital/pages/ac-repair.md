## Warm Air, Silent Unit: What Usually Fails

The thermostat says 74, the house says 85, and the vents blow warm. Heat is when an air conditioner works hardest, so heat is when parts quit. Most of the time it's one failed part, not a dead system.

Here is what we usually find, and what we do about it.

**The unit hums but the fan won't start** A weak capacitor, the small part that gives the motors their starting jolt. We replace it, and it is one of the cheapest and most common fixes.

**The fan or compressor stalls** Hours of Kansas heat wear a motor's bearings and windings. We confirm the motor is the cause, then replace it.

**Cool air that isn't cold** A system doesn't use up refrigerant, so low levels mean a leak. We find the leak instead of just topping off.

**The system shuts off and water sits by the unit** Algae has clogged the condensate drain and a safety switch tripped. We clear the line and check why it clogged.

**Ice on the coil** Restricted airflow, usually a dirty filter, lets the coil freeze over. Turn the system off to thaw it, and we find why the air was restricted.

**The AC won't turn on at all** A tripped breaker is the first thing to rule out, and a failed contactor or a dead control board is next. We test each one, show you the part that failed, and replace only that.

We'd rather find the real cause and fix it once than guess and have to come back.

## Repair or Replace?

Repair when the system is fairly young and one part has failed. A new capacitor or motor on a system that otherwise runs well is money well spent.

Replace when most of these are true: the system is 10 to 15 years old or more, it has broken down repeatedly, it runs on the older R-22 refrigerant, or it can't keep the house cool even with every part working.

We give you the reasoning and the numbers on both, and you choose. If it does come to replacement, here is [what a new system involves](/ac-replacement-wichita-ks/).

## Not Sure It's the AC?

A quick check can save you a visit:

- Confirm the thermostat is set to cool, below the room temperature, with working batteries.
- Check that the breaker for the outdoor unit and the indoor air handler hasn't tripped.
- Look at your air filter. If it's packed with dust, replace it.
- Check for ice on the refrigerant lines or the indoor coil. If you see ice, turn the system off and let it thaw before calling.

If those don't fix it, call and tell us what you see and hear. That helps us arrive with the right parts.
