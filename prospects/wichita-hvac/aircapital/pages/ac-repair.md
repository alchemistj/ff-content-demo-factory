## What Usually Fails, and Why

Air conditioners fail in heat because heat is when they work hardest. Here is what fails most often:

- **Capacitors.** A small part that gives the motors the jolt to start. It weakens with heat and age, then the fan or compressor won't start. It is one of the cheapest and most common failures.
- **Fan and compressor motors.** Hours of Kansas heat wear bearings and windings until the motor stalls or quits.
- **Low refrigerant.** A system doesn't use up refrigerant, so low levels mean a leak somewhere that needs finding, not just topping off.
- **Clogged drain line.** Algae blocks the condensate drain and a safety switch shuts the system down.
- **Frozen coil.** Restricted airflow, usually from a dirty filter, lets the coil ice over and stops cooling.

A technician who finds the real cause fixes it once. One who guesses sends you a second bill.

## Repair or Replace?

A repair is usually the right call when the system is fairly young and one part has failed. A new capacitor or motor on a system that otherwise runs well is money well spent.

Replacement starts to make sense when the system is old, keeps breaking, or can't keep the house cool even when every part works. Spending repeatedly on a system near the end of its life rarely pays.

We give you the numbers on both and let you choose. If you go with a new system, financing is available through Enhancify.

## Not Sure It's the AC?

Before you call, a quick check can save you a visit:

- Confirm the thermostat is set to cool, below the room temperature, with working batteries.
- Check that the breaker for the outdoor unit and the indoor air handler hasn't tripped.
- Look at your air filter. If it's packed with dust, replace it.
- Check for ice on the refrigerant lines or the indoor coil. If you see ice, turn the system off and let it thaw before calling.

If those don't fix it, give us a call. Tell us what you see and hear, and that helps us arrive with the right parts.
