## Furnace or Water Heater

Both are "heaters," and both are in our line of work.

**A furnace** warms the air that moves through your house. Gas furnaces carry a burner, an igniter, a blower and a heat exchanger. When the heat exchanger cracks, replacing the furnace is usually the answer, because that part can't be fixed safely.

**A water heater** keeps a tank of hot water ready, or heats it on demand. Tanks rust from the inside and collect sediment, especially in hard water, and a tank that has started to leak can't be repaired.

If you're not sure which one you need, call and tell us what's happening. Cold rooms point to the furnace, and cold showers point to the water heater.

## Why the Install Matters as Much as the Unit

A good heater installed badly is still a bad heater. A furnace that's too big short-cycles and wears out early. One that's too small never catches up on a January night.

Venting is the part people don't see. A gas furnace or water heater has to send combustion gases outside, and a poor connection can put them in the house. A water heater that isn't connected and drained correctly can leak into a floor.

This is why installation shouldn't be a rush job. The unit has to match the house, and the work around it has to be done correctly.
