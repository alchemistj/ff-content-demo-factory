## What You Notice, and What It Means

Wichita houses run a heater hard from November to March, and a cold snap is when a worn one finally quits. Before you call, check the filter and the thermostat, and look at the base of the water heater for water.

**Cold rooms and uneven heat** A blower that's wearing out, a clogged filter or leaking ducts can cause it, and so can a furnace that can't keep up any more. We look at the blower, the filter and the ducts first, and tell you which one it is.

**A furnace that starts and stops** It's short-cycling: often too big for the house, so it heats fast and shuts off, or it's overheating from a dirty filter or a failing part. We find which, and replace the part or size a new furnace to the house.

**A cold shower halfway through** Tanks collect sediment, especially in hard water, and it covers the bottom of the tank and the heating element or burner. We tell you whether it's the element, the burner or the tank itself.

**Water around the base of the tank** The tank is leaking from the inside, and a leaking tank can't be repaired. We install a new water heater and take the old one out.

If you're not sure which unit is the problem, call and tell us what's happening.

## Repair or Replace

Replace when the heat exchanger has cracked, because that part can't be fixed safely. Replace when a water heater tank is leaking.

Replace when the unit is old and the repairs are stacking up. A rule of thumb: if a repair costs a big share of a new unit, the money is better spent on the new one.

Repair when one part has failed and the rest of the unit is in decent shape. An igniter, a blower motor or a thermostat can all be swapped without replacing the furnace.

Nate will tell you which one yours is. We also repair what's in your house, so a repair is an answer you can hear from us.

## Why the Install Matters

A good heater installed badly is still a bad heater. A furnace that's too big short-cycles and wears out early. One that's too small never catches up on a January night.

Sizing comes from the house: its square footage, its insulation, its windows and its ducts. The old unit's size is only a starting point, because the house may have changed since it went in.

Venting is the part people don't see. A gas furnace or water heater has to send combustion gases outside, and a poor connection can put them in the house.

That's why a gas appliance is worth getting installed by someone you can call back. If something smells or sounds wrong afterward, you want to reach the person who set it up.
