## What You Hear, and Which Part It Is

Moving parts wear out first, and the ones that work hardest wear fastest. In Wichita that's a hot summer on the condenser fan and a long winter on the furnace blower.

**The unit hums but won't start** A weak capacitor or contactor, which give a motor its start-up jolt. The motor itself may be fine, so we swap the small part first.

**The outdoor fan or the blower stalls** The motor bearings have worn, and dust and heat speed that up. We replace the motor with one that matches the unit.

**The furnace clicks and quits** An igniter or a sensor, most often. The igniter cracks after many heat cycles, and a dirty sensor can't prove the flame, so we replace or clean the one that failed.

**Cooling slowly fades** A leak in a refrigerant line or a coil lets refrigerant out, and ice on the lines or a hissing sound is a clue. We find the leak and repair it, not just top off the system.

The fix is usually to swap the failed part and test the system through a full cycle. A capacitor or contactor is a quick swap. A motor takes longer, because it has to match the unit.

Why replace a motor rather than the unit? A motor is one part in a system built from many. If the compressor, coils and controls are sound, the system has plenty of life left.

## A Leak Takes More Than One Visit

Topping off refrigerant without finding the leak just starts the clock again. The system runs low, cools badly and strains the compressor, which is the most expensive part to lose.

A slow leak can take more than one visit to chase down. Coils corrode over time, and fittings and joints can weep a little at a time.

Sometimes the part has to be ordered. We explain what failed and what's needed, and we do what we can to keep the system running in the meantime.

## Fix the Part or Replace the System

A part is the right answer when the rest of the system is in decent shape. It's the wrong answer when the unit is old and failing in several places, or when the compressor or heat exchanger has gone.

Check how many times it's been fixed lately. One part in a long-running system is a repair. A second or third failure in a short span means the system as a whole is wearing out.

## Maintenance That Catches It Early

Most parts don't fail without warning. Filters clog, bearings get noisy, coils collect dirt, and the system works harder until something gives.

Cottonwood fluff and yard dust can coat the outdoor coil by midsummer, and a dirty coil makes the system run longer and hotter. A clean filter, a clear space around the outdoor unit and an ear for new noises catch most trouble early.

We do maintenance on heating and cooling equipment, so small problems are found before they become a dead system on the hottest or coldest day of the year.

In winter, the same goes for the furnace: a clean filter, clear vents and a look at the flame. A steady blue flame is what you want to see. Yellow or flickering is worth a call.
