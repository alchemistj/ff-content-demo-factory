## What Usually Goes Wrong

Furnace problems come down to a short list.

- **Igniter or flame sensor.** The furnace tries to start, clicks or clicks off and never stays lit. Both parts wear out, and a dirty sensor can shut the burners off early.
- **Blower motor.** The fan that pushes warm air through the ducts. If it fails, the furnace may run but little heat reaches the rooms.
- **Control board.** The circuit board that runs the startup sequence. When it fails, the furnace may not respond at all.
- **Dirty filter.** A clogged filter restricts airflow, makes the furnace overheat and shut down, and then it restarts and cycles.
- **Thermostat.** Sometimes the furnace is fine and the control isn't.
- **Heat exchanger.** The metal that heats the air without mixing it with exhaust. Cracks and rust here are a safety problem, and they're why an inspection is worth it.

Most of these are single parts, not a new furnace, even on an older unit.

## If You Smell Gas

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas company or 911 from outside. Don't flip switches or run the furnace.

For any other failure, call us. If the furnace is blowing cold air or won't light, turn the thermostat down, check the filter and call.

## We Find the Cause Before We Replace Anything

Replacing parts until the problem goes away costs you money. We find the cause first, then fix it.

A furnace that cycles on and off may be overheating behind a clogged filter or a closed-off return. Or a dirty flame sensor can't confirm the flame, so the furnace shuts the burners off a few seconds after they light.

Each one looks the same from the hallway: the heat starts, then stops. That's why the diagnosis comes first.
