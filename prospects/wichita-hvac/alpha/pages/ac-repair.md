## What Usually Goes Wrong

Most AC failures come down to a short list.

- **Capacitor.** A small part that gives the motors their starting push. The unit hums, the fan doesn't spin and nothing cools.
- **Blower motor.** The fan inside that moves cool air through the house. When it fails, the outdoor unit may run but little air reaches the rooms.
- **Refrigerant leak.** Low refrigerant means weak cooling and, eventually, ice on the lines or the indoor coil.
- **Frozen coil.** Ice builds when airflow is restricted or refrigerant is low. A clogged filter is a common cause.
- **Thermostat.** Sometimes the system is fine and the control that talks to it isn't.
- **Condenser.** The outdoor unit takes the most abuse, from heat, dirt and Kansas weather.

Many of these are a single part, not a new air conditioner.

## What to Check Before You Call

A few checks cost nothing and sometimes solve the problem.

- **Check the thermostat.** Make sure it's set to cool and set below the room temperature.
- **Check the filter.** A clogged filter chokes airflow, and that can freeze the coil.
- **Check the breaker.** A tripped breaker can shut off the outdoor unit.
- **Look at the outdoor unit.** Clear leaves and debris from around it.

If the AC is still warm after that, turn it off and call. A system that keeps running hot, frozen or short of refrigerant strains the compressor, the most expensive part in it. A repair that's a capacitor today can be a compressor next week.
