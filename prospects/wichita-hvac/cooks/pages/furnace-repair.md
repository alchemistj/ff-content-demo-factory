## What Your Furnace Is Telling You

A furnace runs a sequence: the thermostat calls for heat, the ignitor lights the burners, the flame sensor confirms the flame, and the blower moves warm air through the house. If any step fails, the sequence stops.

That's why symptoms are so specific.

A furnace that clicks and never lights often has a worn ignitor. One that lights and shuts off a few minutes later may have a dirty flame sensor or poor airflow. A blower that won't start can be wiring or a failed part.

Telling these apart is the job. It's the difference between a quick repair and a guess, and we'll walk you through what we found.

## Why a Furnace Blows Cold Air

Cold air from the vents doesn't always mean the furnace is dead. The blower runs on its own, so it can keep pushing air after the burners have shut off.

The cause is usually upstream: a flame that won't stay lit, a safety switch that tripped on overheating, or a filter so clogged the furnace can't breathe. Each has a different fix, and some of them are cheap.

## Carbon Monoxide

A furnace that's burning gas poorly can put carbon monoxide into the house. You can't see or smell it.

We do carbon monoxide detection as part of our heating work. If you have a gas furnace and no working detector, ask us about it.

If you smell gas, don't stop to check the furnace. Leave the house and call the gas company from outside.

## Questions Before You Call

**What can I check before I call?**
Make sure the thermostat is set to heat and the batteries are fresh, and look at your air filter. A clogged filter restricts airflow and can shut a furnace down. If it still won't run, call us.

**Do you work on my brand?**
Yes. We service all brands.

**Is a burning smell normal?**
A dusty burning smell the first time you turn the heat on in the fall is dust on the burners, and it fades within the hour. A smell that keeps coming back, or smells electrical, is not normal. Turn the furnace off and call us.
