## Signs Your Furnace Needs a Technician

The thermostat is calling for heat and nothing happens. You hear clicking but the burners never light. The furnace starts, runs a couple of minutes and shuts off, over and over.

Other signs are quieter. Some rooms stay cold, the blower screams or rattles, or the air coming out isn't warm. Any of these is worth a call before the coldest week of the year.

## What Usually Stops the Heat

A furnace has several steps to go through before it heats your house, and a failure at any one stops the whole thing. That's why most no-heat calls turn out to be a small part and not a new furnace.

**Igniter.** The part that lights the burner wears out with use. A cracked or weak igniter means the furnace tries to start and can't.

**Flame sensor.** This small rod tells the furnace that the burner lit. When it gets coated with residue, the furnace shuts down after a few seconds as a safety measure. Cleaning or replacing it is a simple repair.

**Filter and airflow.** A clogged filter makes the furnace overheat and shut itself off. It's the cheapest fix there is, and the reason some furnaces cycle on and off.

**Blower motor.** The blower moves the warm air through the house. When it fails, the furnace can run and heat and you still get little or no warmth in the rooms.

## The Problem That Isn't Small

A cracked heat exchanger is different. It's the metal chamber that keeps burner exhaust apart from the air you breathe, and a crack in it is a safety problem, not an inconvenience.

If a technician finds one, you'll hear it from us straight, with what it means and what your options are. Whatever your furnace is doing, a working carbon monoxide detector on every floor is worth having.

## Repair or New Furnace?

Repair is the right answer most of the time. An igniter, a sensor or a motor costs far less than a furnace, and the furnace goes on heating your house.

A new furnace starts to make sense when the furnace is old, the repairs keep coming, or the heat exchanger has failed. Spending a lot on an old furnace is often money better put toward a new one.

## Keeping a Furnace Healthy

Furnaces fail hardest in the first cold snap, after sitting idle all summer. Dust builds up, parts stiffen and a weak igniter finally gives out.

A check before heating season catches those things while it's still warm outside. We offer maintenance plans, so ask about one when you call. Changing the filter on a schedule helps too.

## Before You Call

If you can do it safely, check a few things first. Make sure the thermostat is set to heat and the batteries are fresh. Check that the furnace switch is on, and that the filter isn't packed with dust.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas company before anything else. Otherwise, if the furnace still won't run, call us.
