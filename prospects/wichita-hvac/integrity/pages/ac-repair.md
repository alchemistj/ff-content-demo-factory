## What Usually Goes Wrong

The house won't cool below 80, the vent air is lukewarm, the outdoor fan isn't spinning, or the breaker trips every time it starts. Most of the time it's one of a short list of parts.

Ice on the copper line, water around the indoor unit and a system that cycles on and off every few minutes mean turn it off and call. Running a struggling air conditioner makes a small repair bigger.

**Capacitor** Gives the compressor and fan motor the jolt they need to start. When it weakens, the unit hums and won't run. It's one of the most common and cheapest repairs.

**Dirty coil or clogged filter** Dust and a choked filter cut airflow until the coil freezes into a block of ice. The fix is cleaning, and finding out why it got that way.

**Low refrigerant** An air conditioner doesn't use it up, so low means a leak. Adding more without fixing the leak is money that drains away again.

**Fan or compressor motor** A noisy or slow-spinning motor is a warning. Replacing it early is a far smaller job than waiting for it to fail.

## Repair or Replace?

Here's a rule you can check. If it's a capacitor, a motor or a cleaned coil, repair is the answer, for a fraction of the cost of a new system.

If the unit is old, repairs keep piling up, or the compressor has failed, that's when to price a new one. A big repair bill on an old unit is often money better put toward replacement.

## Keeping Your AC Running

A few habits stretch a system's life. Change the filter on a schedule, keep the outdoor unit clear of grass, leaves and anything stacked against it, and don't ignore a new noise.

A spring check before the first hot week catches weak parts before they fail on you, when every company in town is booked.
