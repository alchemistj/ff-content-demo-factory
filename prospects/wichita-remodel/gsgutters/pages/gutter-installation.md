## When New Gutters Are the Answer

Sometimes you can tell from the driveway. A run of gutter has pulled away from the house, a seam drips every time it rains, or the whole thing sags in the middle.

Other times it's what's under the gutter: streaked siding, soft wood along the roof edge, a puddle at the foundation where the downspout should have sent the water.

Gutters take a beating in Kansas. Hail dents them and splits seams, wind loosens the fasteners, and leaves and shingle grit pack the troughs. After enough of that, patching one section only moves the problem down the line.

When the whole run is tired, replacing it is cheaper than coming back to it every spring.

## Why Gutters Matter to the Rest of the House

A roof sheds a surprising amount of water in a hard rain. Gutters catch it at the edge and send it away from the walls and the foundation.

Without them, that water lands next to the house, splashes mud up the siding and soaks into the ground by the foundation. A good run keeps the wall dry from the top.

## Where the Downspouts Go

A gutter is only as good as its downspouts. Too few, and the trough fills faster than it can drain and spills over the front. The same goes for a downspout that drops water right at the foundation.

How many you need depends on how long the run is and how much roof drains into it. We look at your house and tell you.

If the plan changes once we're up on the ladder, we'll tell you and work it in.
