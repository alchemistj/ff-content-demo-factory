## Why a Frameless Door Beats a Curtain or a Framed Slider

A shower curtain clings, grows mildew along the hem and lets water out when the fan pulls on it. A framed door has a bottom track that fills with soap scum and a roller that wears until the door scrapes.

A frameless door has no track and no metal frame around the glass. Thick tempered glass hangs from a few hinges, so there's less to clean and nothing for scum to hide in.

It also lets you see the tile. A small bathroom looks bigger when the shower isn't boxed in by a frame.

## It Has to Be Measured in Your Bathroom

Frameless glass carries its own weight, so a quarter inch matters. Bathroom walls lean, tubs sit a little off level and tile adds thickness no drawing shows.

Glass can't be trimmed once it's made, and tempered glass can't be cut at all. If the door is made to a wrong number, it doesn't get fixed with a shim. It gets remade.

So we come to your bathroom and measure the real shower, not the one on the plans. Then we cut the glass to those numbers.

## A Door or a Walk-In Panel

There are two ways to go, and the bathroom usually points to one.

**A hinged door** closes the shower in. It holds the heat and keeps the spray in a tub-shower combination or a small stall.

**Walk-in shower glass** is a fixed panel with an open entry and no door to swing. It suits a larger shower with room to step in, and it leaves the tile in view.

With a walk-in, the panel has to stand where the spray from the shower head would otherwise hit the floor. That's why we look at where the head points before we decide how wide the glass should be.

Not sure which way to go? Call the office and talk it through before the visit.

## What Decides the Look and the Price

A shower door is mostly glass, so size drives the price first. After that come the thickness of the glass, the hinges and handle you pick and the finish on them.

Clear glass is the usual choice, and it's what lets the bathroom keep its light.

Hardware is also what wears. Hinges carry the full weight of the door, so they're worth choosing on purpose and not by the cheapest option.

Once we've measured and talked through the choices, you get a quote for your shower.
