## When It's the Glass That Failed

A cracked pane is obvious. Others are quieter: a haze between the two panes of a double-pane window that won't wipe off, or a window that has always felt cold to stand next to.

Fog between the panes means the seal around the sealed glass unit has failed and moisture got in. Cleaning won't reach it, because it's inside. The glass unit is what gets replaced.

Cracks come from a hit, from the house settling or from heat building up unevenly across the pane. Either way, the frame is often fine and the glass is the part that needs to go.

## Glass or the Whole Window

Older single-pane windows fail by breaking. Newer double-pane windows fail by fogging, and nothing is cracked.

Either way, the question is the same: what's wrong with it, the glass or the frame?

Replacing only the glass is usually the faster, smaller job when the frame is straight, solid and still opens and closes well.

A whole window is the better call when the frame itself is the problem: rot, warping, a sash that doesn't close or a frame that has pulled out of square.

We look at the frame when we come out to measure, so you hear which one makes sense before you spend anything. And if the answer is new windows, we supply those too.

## Why Every Opening Gets Measured

Windows that look identical rarely are. Houses settle, frames get replaced one at a time and old openings drift out of square.

Glass can't be trimmed once it's made, so the number has to be right before the glass is cut. We measure the opening itself instead of assuming the neighbor window is the same.

Safety matters on some windows, too. Glass in a door, beside a door or low to the floor is usually required to be tempered safety glass, and tempered glass can't be cut after it's made. That's one more reason to measure first.
