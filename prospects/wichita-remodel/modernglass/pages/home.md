## Glass Is Cut for One Opening

A sheet of glass can't be trimmed to fit once it's made. Shower glass and most door glass is tempered, and tempered glass can't be cut at all after it's tempered.

So the measurement is the job. A bathroom wall that leans a quarter inch or a window frame that has shifted over the years is the difference between glass that drops in and glass that goes back.

That's why we come out and measure the opening ourselves instead of quoting from a guess. Then we cut the glass to that measurement and install it.

## Frameless Shower Doors

If your shower has a curtain, or a framed door with a track that sticks and collects scum, a frameless door is the change you notice every morning.

There's no metal frame around the glass. The weight is carried by the hinges and the glass itself, so the fit has to be exact.

We measure in your bathroom, fit the door to the shower you have and install it. Frameless shower doors are the work we do most.

## Walk-In Shower Glass

A walk-in shower trades the door for a fixed panel of glass and an open entry. It suits a bathroom where you want the tile to show and nothing to step around.

The panel has to sit where it will stop the spray from the shower head, so we measure with the head in mind, not just the width of the opening.

We install the glass and leave the bathroom clean.

## What Moves the Price of Glass

We don't put prices on a page, because glass isn't priced off a list. It's priced off your opening.

Size is the biggest factor, since more glass costs more. After that come the thickness of the glass, the hardware on a shower door, whether the edges are polished and whether the glass has to be tempered.

That's another reason we measure first. Once we've seen the opening and talked through what you want, the quote is for your job and not a range.
