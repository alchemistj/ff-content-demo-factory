## Business in one line
Fluid Frame Marketing is Josh Lenz's Springfield MO agency for local service businesses: websites, Google (search, map, Local Service Ads), ads and follow-up, with client-site reporting that ties calls and bookings to marketing source (FF Reports).

## Core customer insight (three lines, written first)
- **What the buyer doesn't know:** how many of the "leads" in the agency report were real people. On client sites, 16 of 18 SRA and 26 of 35 Luecke's submissions in 30 days looked like spam or pitches (Central Forms).
- **What most companies do:** report traffic, impressions and conversions as the agency's own tools count them. Three client Google Ads setups we audited counted any form submit, a form interaction or a phone tap, or sent conversions to a different account (FF Reports).
- **What Fluid Frame does instead:** counts real visitors, real leads and booked jobs, filters the junk before it reaches the owner, and shows the owner the same number it sees.

## Two-sided moat line
"We count leads like your office manager does, and we build sites like a marketing agency." Beats the **ad-and-report shops** on honest counting and beats the **CRM or call-tracking vendors** on building the site, Google profile and ads that make the calls. (Use as a method-page line only; it rests on the SRA ServiceTitan tie.)

## Three USP statements
- **A, pain point:** For Springfield service-business owners who can't tell how many of the agency's leads were real, Fluid Frame counts real leads and booked jobs, not traffic and spam, and shows you the same number we see.
- **B, method:** Unlike agencies that report traffic and let your Ads count every form tap as a win, Fluid Frame filters the junk before it reaches you, ties calls to the source and the booked job, and tests changes on your own site, keeping what wins with real leads.
- **C, emotional outcome:** You open the report and don't have to wonder. The number is real, and the person who made it is a phone call away.

**Pick: A.** It names fear #1 and #2 (paying for numbers you can't trust) in the moment the owner feels it, and its proof is a set of facts no competitor homepage states: the CRM tie (SRA), the filtered count (418 raw, about 233 real) and the spam hold. B's testing and tracking-audit detail stay in the method section, where they support the lead; the audit stays in-house. C is the closing line, not the lead, because nothing in the pull proves a client felt it.

- **The lead rests on SRA (CRM tie) and the Nov 10 spam hold.** One client with a CRM; three days of hold data (18 submissions, 16 held, 2 real delivered, 0 wrongly held so far). Ask Josh which other clients could get the CRM tie, and watch false holds over a few weeks. Say "so far," never "never."
- **Exclusivity is a homepage scan.** "Not stated" is not "absent." Competitors may do this on calls. Check their case studies and a sales call before claiming no one else does it.
- **Named-person and small-team claims are capped at E 3** and support the method section only.
- **Lock-in, contracts and ownership of logins.** Nothing in INPUTS. This is the #3 fear, and FF has no proven answer yet. Ask Josh what a client owns if they leave.
- **No proven split-test winner.** Safe line: "We test changes on your own site and keep what wins with real leads." Not safe: "our tests show X converts better."
- **Flat or down.** Window Dudes, HealthRev, A New You and Luecke's are flat or down in Search Console for 2026 so far. Don't use SRA's 1 to 60 clicks as a general claim, and don't say "we grow every client."
