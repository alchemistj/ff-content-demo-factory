About New Haven, IN
Henry Burgess laid out New Haven, which was founded as a town under Indiana law in 1866. In 1963, it was constituted as a city. Several Burgess-built homes still stand in New Haven. The oldest brick structure in Jefferson Township is a Burgess residence on Summit Street. On the hill above what is now Central Lutheran School, Henry Burgess’ son-in-law, EW. Green, built a huge frame Greek Revival mansion. Another Burgess structure still stands at the intersection of Summit and Eben Streets.
As a small, “front porch” village in Allen County, New Haven offers the best of both worlds. It shares a border with Fort Wayne, Indiana’s second largest city. Because of its proximity to Fort Wayne, New Haven provides access to all of the attractions of a big city while keeping the low cost of living and good quality of life that only a small town can provide.
New Haven nurtures its citizens by providing a range of chances for a place to grow, worship, and feel protected, with 11 neighborhood parks, dedicated emergency service agencies, and other amenities that enhance the quality of life. New Haven is a wonderful place to live, and for a tiny city with so much to offer, it’s easy to see why so many people are happy to call this distinctive community home.
