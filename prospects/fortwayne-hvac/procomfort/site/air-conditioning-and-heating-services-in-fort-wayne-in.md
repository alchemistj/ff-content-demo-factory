About Fort Wayne, IN
Because of its important location at the confluence of three rivers – the St. Mary’s, the St. Joseph, and the Maumee – early settlers and Native Americans referred to Fort Wayne as a crossroads. Fort Wayne is named after General “Mad” Anthony Wayne, a daring military leader who built the first American fort at the confluence of these three rivers.
Fort Wayne thrived as the 1900s rolled into American history. Even throughout the Great Depression, Fort Wayne’s economy thrived. Fort Wayne was home to companies such as Lincoln National Life Insurance Corporation, Farnsworth Television, Zollner Piston, Central Soya, and the Holsum Bakery. During this period, notable Fort Wayne landmarks such as the Embassy Theatre, the Scottish Rite Auditorium, and the Lincoln Tower were built.
Fort Wayne is now not only the junction of three rivers, but also a major metropolitan region and Indiana’s second largest city. Fort Wayne continues to grow as organizations and individuals coexist to live, thrive, and expand. In fact, Fort Wayne was named an All-American City and a Most Livable City in the 1980s and 1990s. The compliments keep pouring in. As the twenty-first century progresses, Fort Wayne looks forward to a time of creativity, innovation, and growth.
