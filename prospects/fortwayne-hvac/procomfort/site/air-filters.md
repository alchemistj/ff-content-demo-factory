Why You Should Obtain Air Filters for Your Home
If you want to make sure that the air in your home is free from contaminants, it’s essential that you select an efficient filter with a MERV rating of at least eight. In the event that your air conditioning unit is powerful enough, you might be able to use a MERV filter with a 13 rating, which will remove nearly all contaminants that are present in the air.
Keep in mind, however, that systems with less powerful blowers might be unable to use a filter with a high efficiency rating. In this scenario, the system’s airflow could become obstructed, which will lower the system’s efficiency and could damage the condenser. You can strengthen the system’s efficiency by replacing the filter on a regular basis. When your air filter is clogged, air flow is restricted.
It’s highly recommended that you have your air filter replaced every three months if you live in a home with no pets. If you believe that there are too many contaminants in your indoor air, you might need to replace the filter sooner. The type of air filter you have can also dictate when it needs to be replaced with a new one.
In the event that you have a single pet in your home, make sure to change your filter every 60 days. If anyone in your home has allergies or you have multiple pets, the filter may need to be changed every 20-45 days. If you own a vacation home that isn’t used that often, you could wait anywhere from 6-12 months to have your air filters replaced. We can provide you with a personalized recommendation based on your living situation and the type of HVAC unit you have.
Health Issues Caused by Poor Air Quality
Bad indoor air quality can cause a wide range of negative health effects, which is why your air filter should be replaced regularly. The many types of indoor air pollutants that can create issues with air quality include mold, second hand smoke, carbon monoxide, pests, dust mites, lead, radon, and pet dander. An increase in precipitation and humidity can also worsen indoor air pollution when mold grows in damp areas.
Nearly everyone is at risk of developing health problems because of indoor air pollution. However, people with preexisting health conditions are more at risk. Some of the health effects can occur almost immediately after being exposed to pollutants. These effects include eye and throat irritation, fatigue, dizziness, and headaches. In most cases, the more immediate effects are short-term and can be easily treated. Getting rid of the pollutant might be enough to curb any health issues.
