When heavy rain or fast snowmelt hits Nebraska, your sump pump becomes your basement's first line of defense. It's easy to forget about until the day it fails, and that's usually the day you need it most.
In this video, ACTION explains what a sump pump does and how it works. Located in a pit at the lowest point of your home, the pump collects groundwater and sends it away from your foundation, helping prevent flooding, water damage, and mold. We show you why it's worth knowing what your system looks like and where it's located.
You'll also learn how to check yours. Testing is simple: pour water into the pit and make sure the pump turns on, removes the water, and shuts off properly. We'll also share signs of trouble to watch for, such as strange noises, constant running, or a pump that never seems to turn on. Because most pumps rely on electricity, a power outage during a storm can leave you exposed, so a battery backup system is something worth considering.
Sump pumps don't last forever, and regular checks help you catch issues early. Our Preferred and Total Home Protection Plans include a sump pump operational check each year.
