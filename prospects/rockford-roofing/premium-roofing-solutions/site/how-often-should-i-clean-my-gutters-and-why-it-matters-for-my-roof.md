How Often Should I Clean My Gutters? (And Why It Matters for My Roof)
Prevent roof leaks, rot, and ice dams with regular gutter maintenance
Learn how clogged gutters can silently destroy your roof and foundation
Discover the smart, seasonal schedule most Wisconsin homeowners follow
You probably don’t think about your gutters much.
They’re up there, doing their job — until they aren’t.
And by the time you notice water spilling over the sides or a sagging section…
The damage has already started.
At Premium Roofing Solutions, we’ve seen firsthand what clogged, neglected gutters can do — to roofs, siding, driveways, and even basements. The fix?
Routine gutter cleaning. Let’s break down how often you should do it, why it matters, and how to stay ahead of problems that cost you money.
Why Gutters Matter More Than You Think
Gutters are the silent MVPs of your roofing system.
They catch rainwater and snowmelt, direct it away from your house, and prevent damage to:
Roof decking and shingles
Fascia boards and soffits
Foundation walls and landscaping
When they’re clean, your gutters protect the entire structure of your home.
When they’re clogged? That water has to go somewhere — and it usually finds its way into places you don’t want it.
What Happens When You Skip Gutter Cleaning
Here’s what we see all the time when gutters are ignored:
Roof leaks caused by trapped water backing under the shingles
Rotten fascia boards that eventually pull the gutters loose
Mold and mildew forming in attic insulation and ceiling corners
Ice dams in winter, leading to costly interior water damage
Cracked foundations from pooling water around the base of the home
These are not small issues. And the worst part?
They’re almost always preventable.
So, How Often Should You Clean Your Gutters?
Here’s our recommended schedule for homes in Wisconsin and the Midwest:
Spring (April/May): Clear out winter debris and prep for rain season
Fall (October/November): Remove leaves before snow and ice hit
Optional Summer Cleaning: If you have heavy tree cover or pine needles
If you have overhanging trees, lots of debris, or past gutter issues, you may need to check them quarterly.
A small step — but one that saves you thousands later.
Bonus Tip: Gutter Guards Help, But They’re Not Set-and-Forget
We install gutter guards for many homeowners — and they do reduce cleaning frequency.
But they don’t eliminate it. Seeds, pollen, and fine debris still accumulate over time.
We recommend inspecting even protected gutters once per year to be safe.
