Top 5 Roofing Materials for Midwest Homes: Pros and Cons
Your home is more than just a shelter—it’s a reflection of your values, your priorities, and your investment in the future. And nothing protects that investment quite like the right roof. In the Midwest, where homes face freezing winters, spring hailstorms, summer heat, and high winds, choosing the right roofing material is not just about aesthetics—it’s about resilience.
At Premium Roofing Solutions, we’ve helped homeowners navigate this decision. Whether you’re in Beloit, Madison, Lake Geneva, Rockford, Schaumburg, Belvidere, or the surrounding areas, this guide breaks down the top 5 roofing materials for Midwest homes—along with their pros and cons—to help you make an informed decision.
Let’s climb up and look at your options from a bird’s-eye view.
1. Asphalt Shingles
Asphalt shingles are the most common roofing material in the United States—and for good reason. They offer a solid balance of cost, durability, and versatility.
Pros:
Affordable: Great for budget-conscious homeowners.
Easy to install: Quick turnaround for most projects.
Wide variety: Available in many colors and styles to match your home’s look.
Decent lifespan: Typically lasts 20–30 years depending on brand and installation quality.
Cons:
Vulnerable to high winds: May be prone to lifting or damage during storms.
Shorter lifespan than premium options.
Not as energy-efficient as other materials like metal or tile.
Best For: Homeowners looking for a reliable, cost-effective roofing option that’s easy to maintain.
2. Architectural Shingles
Think of architectural shingles as the premium version of asphalt. Thicker, heavier, and more dimensional in appearance, they’re designed for performance and style.
Pros:
Improved durability: Can withstand winds up to 130 mph.
Aesthetic appeal: 3D design mimics cedar shake or slate.
Longer warranty: Many come with a 30–50 year limited warranty.
Better weather resistance than standard asphalt shingles.
Cons:
More expensive than traditional asphalt shingles.
Heavier: May require reinforced decking in older homes.
Best For: Homeowners who want to elevate curb appeal without committing to the cost of luxury materials.
3. Metal Roofing
Metal roofing has taken off in the Midwest—and for good reason. With extreme temperature swings, ice dams, and snow loads, metal stands up where others wear down.
Pros:
Long lifespan: 40–70 years with proper maintenance.
Energy-efficient: Reflects sunlight and reduces cooling costs.
Low maintenance: Resistant to cracking, shrinking, and eroding.
Excellent for shedding snow and ice.
Cons:
Higher upfront cost than asphalt or architectural shingles.
Noise: Rain and hail may be louder unless soundproofed.
Not ideal for flat roofs or certain architectural styles.
Best For: Homeowners who plan to stay long-term and want a low-maintenance, high-performance roof.
4. Cedar Shake Roofing
Cedar shake offers a rustic, natural beauty that complements historic homes and custom designs. While it may not be for everyone, it’s a strong option for the right house and budget.
Pros:
Natural insulation: Helps regulate attic temperatures year-round.
Beautiful appearance: Each shake is unique.
Environmentally friendly: Biodegradable and sustainably sourced.
Cons:
High maintenance: Requires regular treatment to prevent mold and insects.
Expensive: One of the priciest materials.
Shorter lifespan in humid or snowy climates (20–30 years unless properly maintained).
Best For: Homeowners seeking premium aesthetics and natural materials, and who are willing to commit to maintenance.
5. Synthetic Roofing (Composite Slate or Shake)
Advancements in roofing technology have brought synthetic options that look like slate or shake but perform better under harsh conditions.
Pros:
Lightweight: Easier on your home’s structure.
Durable: Often comes with 50+ year warranties.
Low maintenance: Resistant to mold, pests, and fire.
Weather-resistant: Great for fluctuating temperatures and heavy snow loads.
Cons:
Higher cost upfront compared to asphalt.
Limited manufacturer availability in some areas.
Still a newer option, so long-term performance may vary by brand.
Best For: Homeowners who want the look of luxury materials without the price tag or maintenance headache.
