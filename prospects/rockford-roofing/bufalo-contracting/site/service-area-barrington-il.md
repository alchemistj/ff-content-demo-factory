Barrington IL Roofing for Complex Homes and Demanding Conditions
What does proper roofing for a multi-plane Barrington roof actually require?
When dealing with the roofing demands of Barrington's established residential landscape, the complexity goes beyond shingle selection. Barrington and the surrounding area feature a high concentration of homes with multi-plane rooflines, steep pitches, dormers, and masonry chimneys — each element multiplying the number of flashing points that must be executed correctly to prevent long-term water intrusion. Bufalo Contracting approaches Barrington projects with the additional planning time these architecturally detailed homes require, because rushing through flashing installation on a complex roof creates failure points that standard inspections miss until water has already reached interior spaces.
Lake County's proximity and the moisture-laden weather systems that move through the Barrington area from the northeast make proper underlayment and ventilation particularly important here. Attics in larger homes with complex rooflines are especially prone to inadequate airflow when ridge-to-soffit ratios aren't calculated for the actual footprint — a common problem in homes where additions were built without recalculating the full ventilation system. Our inspections address these dynamics directly, not just the visible shingle surface.

How We Handle Barrington's Architectural Complexity
Roofing a detailed Barrington home requires methodical attention at every transition point. Our process on complex multi-plane homes follows a sequence that ensures each element is addressed correctly before the next layer goes on.
Valley flashing installed with woven or open-valley metal systems appropriate to the pitch and traffic load of each valley — not just rolled over with modified bitumen and called complete
Chimney flashing rebuilt with step flashing integrated into mortar joints and counter-flashing locked into the masonry — the standard for lasting performance on Barrington stone and brick chimneys
Skylight and dormer flashing inspected and replaced as needed, since these high-failure zones on complex rooflines account for a disproportionate share of interior leak calls
Ventilation recalculation when additions or converted spaces have altered the original attic configuration — common in Barrington's evolved home inventory
Material selection for high-visibility steep-pitch sections where shingle alignment and shadow line consistency are visible from street level and affect curb presentation
