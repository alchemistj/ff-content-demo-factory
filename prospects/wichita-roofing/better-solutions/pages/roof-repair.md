## Water Through the Ceiling

A stain on the ceiling is rarely under the hole in the roof. Water gets in at one spot, runs along the decking or a rafter, and drips out somewhere else.

That's why patching above the stain so often fails. The usual culprits are flashing around chimneys, walls and vents, cracked pipe boots, and valleys where two slopes meet.

A bead of caulk can hide a bad flashing job for a season. It doesn't fix it. We start at the stain, work upward and outward until we find where the water gets in, and repair that.

## Vents and Flashing

The small parts of a roof do a lot of the work. Vents let attic air out, flashing seals the joints, and rubber boots wrap the pipes that come through.

Those parts wear out before the shingles do. When one fails, water finds it first, so replacing a worn vent or resetting flashing is often the whole repair.

## Why Kansas Roofs Need Repairs

Wichita roofs take a lot. Spring storms bring hail and straight-line wind, summer sun bakes the shingles, and a freeze and thaw in winter works water into every small gap.

A roof that's a few years old can lose shingles in one gust, and the damage rarely stays small if it's left. A repair done early costs a fraction of what the same leak does after a season of rain.

## Repair or Replace?

Repair makes sense when the damage is in one place and the rest of the roof is sound. Replacement makes sense when the trouble is everywhere: shingles curling across several slopes, granules piling up in the gutters, leaks in more than one spot.

## Questions About Roof Repair

**How soon should I fix missing shingles?**
As soon as you reasonably can. Exposed underlayment lets water in, and the next wind or rain makes it worse.

**Why is the ceiling stained when the roof looks fine?**
Water travels from where it gets in. Flashing, vent boots and valleys are the first places to check.

**Will new shingles match my old ones?**
Close, usually, but not perfectly. Sun fades shingles, so new ones can look a shade different at first.
