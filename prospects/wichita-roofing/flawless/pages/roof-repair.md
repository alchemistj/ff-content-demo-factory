## Where Roofs Leak

The stain on your ceiling is almost never right under the hole. Water runs along the decking and the rafters before it shows, so the leak can be several feet from the stain.

Most leaks start in the same handful of places:

- **Flashing.** The metal around chimneys, walls and skylights gets bent and pulls loose, and water goes in behind it.
- **Pipe boots.** The rubber collar around a vent pipe cracks in the sun long before the shingles wear out.
- **Missing or lifted shingles.** Wind breaks the seal and lifts them, and the next rain gets under.
- **Hail bruises.** A shingle hit by hail loses granules and cracks, and it leaks months later.
- **Valleys.** Where two slopes meet, a lot of water runs through a small seam.

That's why we get on the roof. A leak you can only see from inside the house has to be traced from outside.

## Claim It or Just Fix It

After a storm, the question isn't only what's broken. It's whether the damage is worth an insurance claim at all.

A claim pays for storm damage above your deductible. If a few shingles blew off and the repair costs less than your deductible, filing gets you nothing back, and paying for the repair yourself is simpler.

If the hail or wind damage runs across the roof, the math changes. That's when a claim can cover far more than a patch, and often a whole new roof.

We'll tell you which side of that line your roof is on after the inspection, with the repair price in hand, so you decide with real numbers. If you file, we file it with you and work with the adjuster.

Have the damage looked at soon either way. Damage written down right after the storm is better evidence than a memory of a noise in the night.

## Repair or Replace

This is the question most homeowners have. We'll answer it on the roof, with you.

A repair makes sense when the damage is in one place and the rest of the roof has years left. A flashing, a pipe boot or a handful of shingles can be fixed without touching anything else.

Replacement makes sense when the damage is spread across the roof, the shingles are old and brittle, or the same roof keeps leaking in different places. At that point you'd be paying for repair after repair on a roof that's finished.
