## Shingles Blown Off in the Wind

You come home after a windy night and there are shingles in the yard, or you spot a bare patch from the street. A missing shingle exposes the layer under it, and that layer was never made to sit in the sun and rain.

Wind gets under the edge of a shingle whose seal has aged, and lifts it. Once one goes, the shingles next to it are easier to lift.

We replace what blew off, look over the shingles around it and make sure the new ones seal down. That's usually a small job, and it's best done before the next storm.

## A Leak Isn't Always Where the Stain Is

Water doesn't drop straight down. It enters the roof in one place, runs along the deck or a rafter, and shows up as a stain a few feet away.

That's why patching right above the stain so often fails. The leak is still there, and the stain comes back.

The usual entry points are flashing, the metal around chimneys, walls and vents, along with pipe boots and valleys. A bead of caulk can hide a bad flashing job for a season, but it doesn't fix one.

I start at the stain and work up and out until I find where the water gets in. Then I fix that.

## Skylights, Vents and Other Places Roofs Leak

Anything that goes through a roof is a place for water to get in. That's skylights, vent pipes, chimneys and the joints where one roof meets a wall.

Skylights age out. The seal around the frame dries, cracks or pulls loose, and the leak looks like a roof problem when it isn't.

When one of these is the cause, that's where the fix goes, not in the shingles around it.

A repair makes sense when the damage is in one place and the rest of the roof is sound. Replacement makes sense when the trouble is everywhere: shingles curling across several slopes, granules piling up in the gutters, leaks in more than one spot.

## Questions About Repairs

**How soon should I fix missing shingles?**
As soon as you reasonably can. The layer underneath isn't made to face the weather, and the next wind or rain makes it worse.

**Why is my ceiling stained when the roof looks fine?**
Water often travels from where it enters. Flashing, vent boots, skylights and valleys are the first places I check.

**Will a repair match my old shingles?**
Close, usually, but not perfectly. Sun fades shingles, so new ones can look a shade different at first.

**Do you repair gutters too?**
Yes. Gutters are part of the roof edge, and I'll look at them while I'm there.
