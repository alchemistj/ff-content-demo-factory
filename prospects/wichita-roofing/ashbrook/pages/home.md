## When Insurance Is Part of the Job

A storm goes through. Maybe there's a stain on the ceiling, maybe the roof looks fine from the driveway, and you've never filed a claim in your life.

An adjuster walks your roof for a short visit, and what goes in the report shapes the whole job. The photos, the notes and the list of what's damaged all matter.

That's the part I do with you. I look at the roof, document what I find, and explain it in plain words. When your adjuster comes out, I'll be there if you want me there.

If the first answer from the insurance company looks short, I'll help you ask for a second look. Your policy and your decisions stay yours, and no roofer can tell you what an insurance company will pay.

Water rarely comes through the roof right above the stain. It runs along the deck and drops somewhere else, so I look for where it gets in, not just where you see it.

## Questions Before You Call

**Do you help with insurance claims?**
Yes. I document the roof, come out when the adjuster is there, and help you ask for a second look if the first answer looks short. What the insurance company pays is between you and them.

**Will insurance pay for a new roof?**
It depends on your policy and on what the adjuster finds. I can't promise an outcome, and anyone who does is guessing.

**How long does a roof take?**
Many of our roofs go on in a single day. Weather, size and any rotted decking can change that, and I'll tell you what to expect once I've seen the roof.

**What will it cost?**
It depends on the size and pitch of the roof, the layers on it, the condition of the deck and what you want on it. I give you a price after I've looked, not before.

**Do you do repairs or only whole roofs?**
Both. A few shingles or a leak is a repair job, and I'll say so.

**What if I'm out of town?**
I've handled roofs for owners who weren't home, including the insurance side. Call and we'll work out how.

**Do you do more than roofs?**
Yes: gutters, siding, soffit and fascia, windows, doors, and some interior remodeling.
