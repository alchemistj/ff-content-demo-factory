## What Hail Actually Does to a Shingle

Most hail damage can't be seen from the ground. Hail bruises a shingle: it knocks the protective granules loose and cracks the asphalt mat underneath.

The shingle still looks like a shingle. But the bare spot ages fast in the sun, and the crack opens up over the next few winters. That's how a roof that "looked fine" after the storm leaks two years later.

## What You're Seeing, and What It Usually Means

Most roof calls start with something a homeowner noticed from the yard or the hallway. Here's what usually causes each one, and what we do about it.

**Shingles in the yard after high wind** Wind got under shingles whose seal strip had aged and tore them loose, and the ones beside them are easier to lift now. We replace what blew off, check the shingles around it and make sure the new ones seal down.

**Dented gutters and granules piled at the downspouts** Hail hit hard enough to dent metal, so it almost certainly bruised the shingles too, even if the roof looks fine from the driveway. We inspect for free and photograph the hits so you can see them yourself.

**Neighbors getting new roofs, and yours looks fine** Hail damage is easy to miss from the ground, and the same storm usually hits the whole block. A free inspection tells you whether yours took damage; if it didn't, we say so and you owe nothing.

**A water stain on the ceiling** Water enters at one spot and runs along the decking before it drips, so the stain is rarely under the leak. We start at the stain and work up the roof to the flashing, pipe boot or valley that's letting it in.

**A leak that came back after a repair** The first repair fixed something that wasn't the problem, often because flashing from the original roof was set wrong. We go back, look again and fix the cause instead of caulking over it.

**Shingles curling or cracking on every slope** That's age and sun, and patching one spot won't stop the next. A full replacement is the honest fix, and we'll tell you if it's still a few years off.

## Questions Before You Call

**Is the inspection really free?**

Yes. We get on the roof or put a drone over it, photograph what's there and walk you through it. If what you thought was damage is just normal wear, we'll tell you that and you owe nothing.

**How can I tell if hail damaged my roof?**

Look at the soft metal first: dents in gutters, downspouts, vents and flashing mean hail hit hard enough to bruise shingles. Granules piled at the bottom of your downspouts are another sign. Then have someone get on the roof, because the shingle damage itself rarely shows from the ground.

**Do I have to file an insurance claim?**

No. It's your policy and your decision. We'll show you what we found, and if the damage doesn't justify a claim, we'll say so.

**Will insurance pay for a whole new roof?**

It depends on your policy and on how much damage the adjuster agrees is there. You can check the offer yourself: the scope lists which slopes and how many squares it covers, so compare that to the slopes that took hail. If it's short, we document what's missing and push back.

**What's my share of the cost?**

Your deductible. Many policies set a separate hail and wind deductible, often a percentage of your home's insured value rather than a flat amount. Find that number on your declarations page before the adjuster visits.

**How long do I have to file after a storm?**

It depends on your policy, and the time limit is written in it, usually in the section on what to do after a loss. Don't wait to find out: hail damage is easiest to prove soon after the storm.

**How long does a roof replacement take?**

A lot of our roofs are done in a single day. A big or steep roof, or one with a lot of decking to replace, takes longer, and we tell you before we start.

**Do you do commercial roofs?**

Yes. We maintain, repair and replace roofs on commercial buildings as well as homes.
