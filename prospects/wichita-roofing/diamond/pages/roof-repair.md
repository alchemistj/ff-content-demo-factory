## Shingles Missing After the Wind

You come home after a windy night and there are shingles in the yard, or a bare patch shows from the street. A missing shingle exposes the layer underneath, and that layer was never meant to face rain and sun.

Wind gets under the edge of a shingle whose seal has aged and lifts it. Once one goes, the ones beside it are easier to lift.

A repair here is usually small, and it's worth doing before the next storm. We replace what blew off and check the shingles around it.

## A Leak Isn't Always Where the Stain Is

Water doesn't drip where it gets in. It enters at one spot, runs along the decking or a rafter, and shows up on your ceiling six feet away.

That's why a patch above the stain so often fails. The usual culprits are flashing around chimneys, vents and walls, cracked pipe boots, and valleys where two slopes meet.

A bead of caulk can hide bad flashing for a season. It doesn't fix it. A real repair finds where the water gets in and fixes that.

## Repair or Replace?

A repair makes sense when the damage is in one place and the rest of the roof is sound.

Replacement makes sense when the trouble is everywhere: shingles curling across several slopes, grit in the gutters, leaks in more than one spot, or a roof that has been patched and keeps leaking.

We'll tell you which one you have. If it's a replacement, we'll say so plainly and explain why, and you can read how that job goes on our [roof replacement](/roof-replacement-wichita-ks/) page.

## What to Do While You Wait

Until someone can get to the roof, a few steps limit the damage:

- Put a bucket under the drip and move furniture and rugs out of the way.
- Take photos of the ceiling, the shingles in the yard, and any damage you can see from the ground. They help if there's a claim.
- Stay off a wet roof. A fall is a bigger problem than a leak.
