## Is It Time for a New Roof?

A roof wears out a little at a time. By the time it leaks, the damage underneath has usually been going on for a while.

The signs are on the surface. Shingles curl at the edges or crack, bare patches show where the granules have worn off, and the gutters fill with grit from the shingles. Leaks that were one spot become two.

A roof that has been patched several times, or that took hail or wind damage across more than one slope, is usually past repairing. Patching a worn roof only buys you a season.

Not every leak means a new roof, though. If the damage is in one place and the rest is sound, we'll say so and point you to [roof repair](/roof-repair-wichita-ks/).

## Choosing the Brand and the Color

The shingle you pick decides how the house looks for the next couple of decades, and it's easy to get lost between brands, lines and colors.

A few things narrow it down. Darker colors show off a roofline but run hotter in a Kansas summer.

Heavier architectural shingles cost more than basic ones and tend to wear better. Your neighbors' roofs and your siding matter too.

Stacia walks you through the options and helps you land on one that fits your house and your budget, so you aren't choosing from a sample board alone.

## Weather and Your Schedule

Roofing depends on the sky. Rain and high wind stop the work, and a roof that's torn off has to be dried in before the weather turns.

Size, pitch and what the crew finds under the old shingles also change how long a roof takes. A typical shingle roof is a matter of a day or a few, not weeks.

Our job is to keep you informed. When the schedule moves, you hear it from us. And when you're up against a deadline, tell us on the first call.
