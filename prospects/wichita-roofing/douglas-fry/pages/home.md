## Tile Roofs, Concrete and Clay

Most tile roof leaks start with a brown stain on the ceiling and a roof that looks fine from the street. Every tile is still there and none are broken.

That's because tile sheds water but doesn't stop it. The layer under the tile, the underlayment, is what keeps your house dry, and it wears out long before the tile does.

Leaks also start where the roof changes direction: valleys, flashing around chimneys and skylights, and the spots where a cracked tile lets water in.

We find where the water is really getting in, and fix that. If the underlayment has failed, we lift the tile, put down new underlayment and lay your existing tile back. It's slow, hard work, and it is how a tile roof gets another few decades.

## Why Matching Tile Matters

A storm breaks part of your tile roof. The easy fix is to replace the broken tiles with whatever is close.

But tile comes in different shapes, profiles and colors, and a tile that has weathered for twenty years never matches a new one. A near match looks like a patch, and if the shape is a little off, it doesn't sit or seal like the original.

So we order matching tile, and Jason takes it up with your insurer when a storm did the damage.

## Questions Before You Call

**How long does a tile roof last?**
The tile itself lasts decades. The underlayment beneath it usually gives out first, and that is why most tile roof repairs involve lifting tile and putting it back.

**Can my tile roof be repaired, or do I need a new one?**
If the tile is sound and the leak is at a valley, flashing or underlayment, it can usually be repaired. If hail or wind has broken tile across the roof, a replacement may make more sense.
