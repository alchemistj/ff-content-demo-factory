## What Usually Needs Repairing

Most roof leaks are not a worn-out roof. We trace most of them to a short list of small places:

- **Flashing.** The metal around chimneys, walls and skylights that bends, rusts or pulls loose.
- **Vent boots.** The rubber collars around plumbing pipes crack in the sun years before the shingles do.
- **Chimney chases and caps.** The framed box around a chimney leaks at the joints and the siding where it meets the roof.
- **Missing or lifted shingles.** Wind breaks the seal on a shingle and then rips it off the next time it blows.
- **Valleys.** Where two slopes meet, all the water runs through one narrow channel.

Each of these costs far less to fix than a roof. The trick is finding which one it is.

## Wind and Storm Damage

Wind in Kansas doesn't just take shingles off, it creases them. A shingle can look fine from the yard and still have a broken seal, ready to go the next time the wind comes up.

Hail leaves bruises and bare spots that you can't see from the ground, and they leak a year later. After a bad storm, it's worth having someone on the roof even if nothing looks wrong.

We repair storm-damaged roofs, from a few shingles to the larger jobs. If the damage is spread across much of the roof, we'll say so plainly, because that's a different decision.

## How We Find the Leak

Your own clues help: whether the stain grows in every rain or only when wind drives it sideways, and whether it sits near a chimney, a bathroom fan or a plumbing vent. They narrow it down, but they don't settle it, because water rarely drips straight down from the opening.

So we start in the attic, where a leak leaves dark streaks, wet insulation and damp wood. Then we go up on the roof and follow the trail to the opening.

Sometimes the trail ends somewhere other than the shingles. That's the whole point of looking: you pay to fix what's actually letting water in. You get a detailed estimate for the repair that stops it.

## Repair or Replace?

We'd rather fix a roof that can be fixed. If most of the roof is sound and the damage is in one place, repair is the right answer.

Here's when we'll tell you it's time to stop repairing:

- Shingles are curling, cracking or bare across the whole slope.
- The roof has been repaired several times and still leaks.
- The decking is soft or sagging in more than a small spot.
- Granules are collecting in your gutters.

In that case the next step is a [roof replacement](/roof-replacement-wichita-ks/), and we'll show you why. If it's a repair, we'll say that too.

## What Affects the Price of a Repair

Repair prices vary because repairs vary. Resealing a vent boot is a short job. Rebuilding a chimney chase or replacing a section of rotted decking is not.

Roof pitch, height and access change the time it takes. Wet weather can delay work. The kind of shingle matters, since older styles can be hard to match.

We give you a detailed estimate once we've found the problem, so you know what you're paying for before we start.

## Storm Damage and Your Insurance

If wind or hail did the damage, your homeowner's policy may cover some or all of the repair. That depends on your policy, your deductible and what the adjuster decides.

Take photos of the damage and the date of the storm, and keep any receipts for temporary fixes. We can't promise how your insurer will respond, but we can tell you exactly what's wrong with the roof.
