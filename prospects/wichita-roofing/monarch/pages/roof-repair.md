## Shingles Blown Off in the Wind

You find them in the grass after a windy night, or you see a bare strip on the roof from the street.

Shingles hold down with a sealant strip and a few nails. When the strip dries out and lets go, the wind gets under the edge and pulls the shingle up. The next gust takes the one beside it.

A repair puts new shingles in and seals the ones around them. It's worth doing quickly, because bare felt and exposed nails let water into the deck.

## A Leak Isn't Always Where the Stain Is

Water doesn't drip straight down. It runs along the roof deck and the rafters until it finds a gap in the ceiling, so the stain can be many feet from the hole.

That's why guessing at a leak gets expensive. Patch the wrong spot and the stain comes back the next time it rains.

Cesar looks for where the water gets in, not where it shows up. That means checking the roof surface, the flashing and the attic side when it matters.

## Where Roofs Usually Leak

Most leaks aren't in the middle of a shingle field. They start at the places where the roof changes direction or something goes through it.

- Flashing around chimneys, skylights and the walls where a lower roof meets a higher one.
- Plumbing vents and roof vents, where the rubber collar cracks in the sun.
- Valleys, where two slopes meet and carry the most water.
- Nail heads that have worked loose and lifted a shingle.
- Hail or wind damage that has opened the shingle surface.

## Why Repairs Fail

Most repairs that come back were fixing the stain. A dab of sealant on the wrong spot hides the problem until the next rain.

A repair that lasts replaces the failed part: the flashing, the collar or the damaged shingles, not the symptom. If you've patched the same spot twice, the real problem is somewhere else.

## Repair or Replace? We'll Tell You Which

Repair makes sense when the damage is limited and the rest of the roof is sound. A few missing shingles, one bad flashing and one failed vent are all repairs.

Replacement makes sense when shingles are cracking across the whole roof, granules are gone from every slope, or the same leak keeps coming back.

## Repairs After a Storm

If hail or wind caused the damage, take photos before anyone patches it. The adjuster needs to see what happened, and a patched roof can hide it.

A small repair and a hail claim are two different jobs. A few lifted shingles is a repair. Hail damage across the roof can be a replacement, and that decision is worth making before you spend money on patches.
