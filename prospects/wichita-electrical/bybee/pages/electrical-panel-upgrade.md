## When an Old Panel Is a Safety Problem

A panel that works isn't always a panel that's safe. These are the signs we look for.

- **A fuse box.** Fuses are older technology and often mean a small service and old wiring.
- **A brand of breaker known to fail to trip.** Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco panels are the two best known. A breaker that doesn't trip can't protect the wire behind it.
- **Rust, scorch marks or a burning smell** at the panel.
- **Two wires under one breaker.** Most breakers are built for one.
- **No open slots.** If every space is full, the next circuit gets squeezed in somewhere it shouldn't.
- **Too small for the house.** A 60 or 100 amp service was fine once. Add central air, an electric range, a hot tub or an EV charger and it can't keep up.

If none of these describes your panel, it may be fine as it is.

## What a Panel Upgrade Involves

Most people want to know what's going to happen to their day. Here it is.

1. **A permit and an inspection.** Panel work is permitted, and the city inspects it. It's part of the job, and we handle it.
2. **The power company.** Evergy disconnects your service so the panel can be swapped, then reconnects it.
3. **The swap.** The old panel comes out, the new one goes in, and every circuit is connected and tested. A new panel is often 200 amps.
4. **Sometimes more.** An old meter base or service mast may need replacing at the same time, and we'll tell you at the estimate if yours does.
5. **Labeling.** Every circuit is labeled, so you know what each breaker controls.

It usually takes a day, and the power is off for part of it. We tell you how long before we start, so you can plan around it.
