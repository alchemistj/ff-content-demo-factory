## Electrical Repair

You flip a switch and nothing happens. An outlet in the kitchen has been dead since you moved in. A breaker trips every time the microwave and the toaster run together.

Each one has a cause.

A breaker trips because a circuit is overloaded, shorted or leaking current to ground, and a breaker that trips again right after you reset it is telling you something.

Lights that flicker in one room are usually the fixture or bulb. Flicker across the house, or when a big appliance starts, points to a loose connection or an overloaded service.

A warm outlet, a discolored switch plate, a buzzing or a burning smell is different. That's a loose or failing connection, and it should be looked at the same day, not next month.

We trace the problem to its source, fix that, and test it before we leave. When we find a second problem while we're there, we show it to you and let you decide.

We repair outlets, light switches, circuit breakers and wiring, and we fix the lights and fans that stopped working.

## Electrical Panel Upgrade

The panel is the box in your garage, basement or utility room where every circuit starts. Most people never think about it until the breaker won't reset or an electrician says it's time.

An old panel becomes a safety problem for a few reasons.

It may be a fuse box.

It may hold a brand of breaker known to fail to trip when it should. You may see rust or scorch marks, two wires under one breaker, or no open slot for the circuit you need.

Or it may simply be too small, a 60 or 100 amp service in a house now running central air, a hot tub or an EV charger.

An upgrade means a permit and a city inspection, the power company disconnecting and reconnecting your service, and a new panel, often 200 amps. The power is off for part of the day, so ask for the length of the outage when you get the quote.

A finished panel has every circuit labeled, so the next person who opens the door knows what they're looking at.

**My breaker keeps tripping. What should I do?**

Unplug what was running on that circuit, reset the breaker once and see what happens.

If it trips again right away, or it feels warm, or it won't reset, stop and call us. Don't keep resetting it.

**An outlet feels warm. Is that normal?**

No. Stop using it.

A warm or discolored outlet, a buzzing or a burning smell means a loose or failing connection. Call us the same day.
