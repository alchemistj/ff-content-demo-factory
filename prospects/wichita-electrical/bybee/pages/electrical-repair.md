## Why Things Stop Working

Electrical problems feel random. They aren't. Every dead outlet and tripping breaker has a cause, and the cause is usually one of a short list.

A connection has worked loose. A device has worn out. A circuit is carrying more than it was built for. Or a wire was run wrong years ago and the house has been getting by.

The fix depends on which one it is, and guessing is how people end up replacing parts that were never the problem. We start by finding the cause.

## Breakers That Trip

A breaker is a safety device. It trips to stop a circuit from overheating.

It trips for one of three reasons: the circuit is overloaded, there's a short, or current is leaking to ground. An overload is the common one, a space heater and a hair dryer on the same circuit. If you unplug things, reset it once and it holds, you've probably found your overload.

If it trips again right away, it's telling you something is wrong with the wiring or with what's plugged in. If the breaker feels warm, or won't reset at all, stop and call us. Don't keep flipping it.

We test the circuit, find out which of the three you have, and fix it. Sometimes that's a breaker that's worn out and needs replacing. Sometimes it's a circuit that needs to be split so it isn't carrying the whole kitchen.

## Outlets and Switches That Don't Work

An outlet can go dead because the device inside has worn out, a wire behind it has come loose, or a ground-fault outlet elsewhere has tripped and cut power to everything downstream. Bathroom, garage and outdoor outlets are the usual places to look.

Switches fail the same way. The contacts inside wear out, and eventually the light works when it wants to.

Sometimes the problem is stranger than that. A house that's been remodeled a few times can have circuits that make no sense, with one breaker feeding outlets upstairs and down, inside and out.

Tracing that takes patience. We follow the circuit until we know what's on it, so the fix stays fixed.

## Warm Outlets, Buzzing and Burning Smells

These are different from a dead outlet, because something is still carrying power and it's getting hot.

A warm outlet or switch plate, a brown or discolored cover, a buzzing sound or a burning smell almost always means a loose or failing connection. Loose connections heat up, and heat is how electrical fires start.

Stop using that outlet or switch and call us the same day. If you smell burning or see smoke, turn off the breaker, and if there's any sign of fire, get everyone out and call 911.

## Flickering and Dimming Lights

Where the flicker happens tells you what it is.

One fixture that flickers is usually the bulb or the fixture. Lights that flicker or dim all over the house, or whenever the air conditioner or dryer starts, point to a loose connection or a service that's carrying too much.
