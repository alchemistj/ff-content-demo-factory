## When an Old Panel Becomes a Safety Problem

The panel is where power enters your house and gets split into circuits. It works quietly for decades, which is why people forget it.

Age alone isn't the problem. These are the signs that a panel needs attention, and any one of them is worth a call:

- A fuse box instead of breakers
- Brands known for breakers that fail to trip, including Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco
- Rust, moisture or scorch marks inside or around the panel
- Two wires under one breaker, called double-tapping
- No open slots for a new circuit
- 60- or 100-amp service in a house that now runs central air, an EV charger or a hot tub

A breaker is supposed to cut power when a circuit is overloaded. A panel whose breakers don't do that is a fire risk, whatever it looks like from the outside.

## Is It Time, or Do You Have Years Left?

You may not need a new panel at all. A breaker that trips now and then, or a panel that's simply old, can be fine.

We'll look at yours and tell you straight which it is. If it's sound, we'll say so. If it's a risk, we'll show you why.

Bring up what you plan to add in the next few years, too. A panel that is fine today can be too small once you add a charger or an addition, and it's cheaper to size it right once.

## Adding Circuits and Loads

Sometimes the panel is fine and the load is the problem. A kitchen with too many appliances on one circuit trips the breaker over and over.

We add circuits to spread the load, and we've done it by swapping standard breakers for GFCI breakers and adding GFCI outlets where the code and the room call for them.

New appliance circuits, air conditioner and furnace circuits, hot tub wiring, EV charger installation and backup generators all start at the panel. If yours can't carry them, that's when an upgrade makes sense.

## What a Panel Upgrade Involves

Here's the process in general terms, so you know what you're agreeing to.

1. **A look and a plan.** We check the panel, the service coming into the house and what you want to run. You get options and a price.
2. **A permit and an inspection.** Panel work is permitted and inspected by the city or county.
3. **The power company.** The utility disconnects service at the start and reconnects it when the work passes.
4. **The swap.** We install a new panel, often 200 amps. Sometimes the meter base or the service mast has to be replaced too.
5. **Labels.** Every circuit is labeled, so the next person knows which breaker is which.

Plan on the power being off for part of the day. It's usually one day of work.

## Surge Protection While the Panel Is Open

A whole house surge protector connects at the panel. If we're already replacing it, that's the easy time to add one, so a lightning strike or a utility surge doesn't reach what's plugged in.

Ask about it when you call.

The same goes for any circuit you know you want next, such as a dedicated line for an EV charger or a hot tub. Adding it while the panel is open is simpler than coming back for it.
