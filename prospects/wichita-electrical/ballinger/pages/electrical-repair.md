## Signs It's Time to Call

You don't have to wait for the lights to go out. These are worth a phone call:

- A breaker that trips again soon after you reset it
- A breaker that is warm, or won't reset at all
- An outlet or switch plate that's warm, discolored or scorched
- A buzzing sound, or a burning smell, from a panel, outlet or switch
- Lights that flicker in several rooms, or dim when a big appliance starts
- Outlets or whole circuits that quit working
- A GFCI that keeps tripping, or an outlet that no longer holds a plug

If anything is hot or smells burnt, stop using it and call us.

## Why Breakers Trip

A breaker trips to stop a circuit from carrying more than its wires can handle. There are three usual reasons.

An **overload** means too much is plugged into one circuit, like a space heater and a microwave on the same line. A **short** is a hot wire touching a neutral or another hot, and it trips the breaker instantly. A **ground fault** is current finding another way to ground, often through moisture in an outdoor or garage outlet.

One trip after you ran everything at once is normal. A breaker that trips again right after you reset it has a problem that resetting won't solve.

## Warm Outlets and Flickering Lights

An outlet or switch that's warm to the touch usually has a loose connection. A loose wire makes heat where the electricity has to jump, and over time that heat can melt the device or start a fire. We take warm outlets seriously, and so should you.

Flicker has more than one cause. If one lamp flickers, it's often the bulb, the fixture or the dimmer. If lights across the house flicker or dim when the AC or the dryer kicks on, it can mean a loose connection in the panel or a service that's carrying more than it should.
