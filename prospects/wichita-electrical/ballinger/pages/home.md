## Electrical Repair

A breaker that trips is protecting you from something: too much on one circuit, a short, or a ground fault. If it trips again right after you reset it, the circuit is telling you something. If it's warm or won't reset, call an electrician.

Warm outlets, a switch plate that's discolored, a buzz, a burning smell: those usually mean a loose connection, and loose connections get hot. Stop using that outlet and call us.

Lights that flicker in one fixture are often just the bulb. Flicker in several rooms, or when a big appliance starts, points to a loose connection or a circuit that's overloaded.

We find the cause, and often fix it on the same visit.

## Panel Upgrades

A breaker panel is the heart of the house, and an old one can be a safety problem. Fuse boxes, rust or scorch marks, double-tapped breakers, and brands with a record of breakers that fail to trip (Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco) are all reasons to get it looked at.

So is a panel with no room left for new circuits, or a 60- or 100-amp service in a house that now runs central air, an EV charger or a hot tub.

An upgrade takes a permit and a city inspection, and the power company disconnects and reconnects your service. Plan on the power being off for part of the day. The new panel is often 200 amps, and every circuit gets labeled.
