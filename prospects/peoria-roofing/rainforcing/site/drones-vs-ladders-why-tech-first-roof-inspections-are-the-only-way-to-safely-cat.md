The Problem With Traditional Ladder Inspections
A ladder-and-clipboard inspection has been the industry standard for decades, but it comes with real limitations:
Safety risk — Climbing onto a steep or wet roof after a storm is one of the leading causes of injury in the roofing trade.
Foot-traffic damage — Every step on an asphalt shingle roof can dislodge protective granules, especially on a roof that’s already stressed from hail impact. Some homeowners have unknowingly ended up with new damage caused by the inspection itself.
Missed micro-fissures — A visual, ground-level or walk-on inspection can catch obvious bruising but frequently misses the fine cracks in the shingle mat that don’t show up until the roof starts leaking a year or two later.
Subjectivity — Without high-resolution, geotagged imagery, an inspection report often comes down to one person’s word — which is exactly what makes insurance adjusters skeptical.
The “storm chaser” reputation problem — Risk-averse homeowners have every reason to distrust a stranger who wants to get on their roof with no documentation and no explanation.
How Drone-Assisted Roof Inspections Work
Instead of sending a technician up a ladder, we launch a drone equipped with a high-resolution camera to systematically photograph and map every plane of your roof. This gives us:
100% coverage of the roof surface, including areas a ladder inspection would normally skip for safety reasons (steep slopes, high peaks, complex rooflines)
High-resolution, time-stamped, geotagged imagery that becomes part of your permanent project file
A visual record you can see for yourself — not just a verbal summary of what someone claims they found
Because the drone never touches the shingles, there is no risk of the inspection itself causing damage — which matters both for your roof’s integrity and for the credibility of the report if your claim is ever questioned.
Thermal Imaging + AI Measurement: Building a Report Adjusters Can’t Easily Dispute
A photo alone tells you what the surface looks like. It doesn’t tell you what’s happening underneath. That’s where thermal imaging and AI-powered measurement tools come in.
Thermal imaging detects temperature differences in the roof deck that indicate trapped moisture — a strong sign of a compromised shingle mat, even when the surface still looks intact.
AI-powered measurement software calculates precise hail spacing, impact size, and slope-by-slope damage density, generating data that mirrors the same measurement standards insurance carriers already use internally.
Combined documentation — photos, thermal data, and measurement reports — turns “trust us” into a technical file that stands up to adjuster scrutiny.
This is a core part of how we support homeowners through the insurance claims process: the stronger the documentation, the less room there is for a carrier to approve a partial or non-compliant repair.

Zero Foot Traffic Means Zero Risk to Your Shingles
Asphalt shingles are more vulnerable right after a hailstorm than at almost any other time — the mat has already absorbed impact stress, and extra weight or friction can knock loose granules that were barely hanging on.
A digital-first inspection means nobody walks your roof to find out if it’s damaged. You get a full report without adding a single new stress point to a roof that’s already been through enough.
