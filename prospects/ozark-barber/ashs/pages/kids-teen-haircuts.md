## Why Kids Fight the Chair

A haircut is odd for a child. Someone they don't know stands behind them with buzzing clippers near their ears and tells them not to move.

Add a quick cut that skips what they asked for and you get a kid who dreads next time. It isn't stubbornness. It's a reasonable reaction to a bad few minutes.

The fix is a barber who slows down, talks to the kid instead of over them and works with the head they actually have.

## What We Do Differently

We ask your child what they want. A five-year-old can tell us "short on the sides" or "like my dad's," and a teen can show us a photo. Then we cut that.

We stay engaged with the kid through the whole cut. Talking while we work keeps small kids calm, and it lets us check that the haircut is going the way they pictured it.

The result is a child who sits in the chair because they know what's coming and they like how they look when it's done.

## Cowlicks, Part Lines and Hair That Won't Behave

Cowlicks are patches of hair that grow in a different direction from the rest. Kids have them at the crown, at the front hairline and behind the ears, and they decide how a cut lies.

Cut against a cowlick without noticing and the hair sticks up all week. A barber who sees it first leaves the hair longer there, or cuts with the direction it grows, so the style sits flat.

A hard part is a shaved line that separates the top from the side. On a child with several cowlicks it takes patience to get straight.

## Toddlers and First Haircuts

Toddlers don't know what a haircut is. They know the chair is high, the cape is strange and the clippers are loud.

We cut toddlers' hair, and a first haircut is a normal visit for us. Tell us it's a first when you book or when you sit down so we can go at the pace your child sets.

A few things help any small child.

Tell them ahead of time that they'll sit in a big chair and the barber will trim their hair, and that it doesn't hurt. Bring them when they're rested and fed. Stay where they can see you.

## Teen Cuts: Fades, Tapers and Photos

Teens have opinions about their hair, and they should. Let them pick the cut. A teenager who chose the style wears it better than one who got whatever a parent picked.

Most teen styles are a taper or a fade.

A taper gets gradually shorter toward the neck and around the ears. A fade blends the sides from longer down to very short, and a skin fade takes the bottom down to the skin. The top can stay longer and textured so it holds a style.

If your teen doesn't know what the cut is called, a photo works. We turn it into the right guard lengths and the right blend, and finish with a clean neckline.
