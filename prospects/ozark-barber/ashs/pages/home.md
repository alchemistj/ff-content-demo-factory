## A Cut That Gets Better Every Visit

Most barbers aim to repeat the last cut. That's the floor here, not the goal.

A haircut wears differently than it looks in the chair. It sits one way with a collared shirt and another with a hoodie. It feels different at work, on a weekend and after a week of Missouri humidity.

So each visit starts with how the last one wore.

What did you like by day ten? What bugged you? Did the top fall the way you wanted with what you actually wear?

Ash takes that in and adjusts. A little more off the crown, a softer line at the neck, a fade that sits lower. Visit by visit, the cut moves toward the one that's really yours.

You get the cut you ask for every time. You also get a barber who keeps working on it with you.

## Haircuts and Fades

The grown-out fade is the haircut moment everyone knows. A week after the cut, the sides have lost their shape and you're looking at the calendar.

A fade is a blend. The hair goes from short at the bottom to longer on top with no line where one length stops and the next begins.

That is why a bad fade is so obvious. A rushed blend shows a step, and as it grows out the shape falls apart faster than it should. A clean one grows out evenly, and you can keep the look for weeks.

We cut classic barber haircuts, tapers, and fades low, mid and high. Skin fades and clean necklines are part of the same work. Show us the photo and we'll match the guard lengths and the line.

## Beard Trim, Cut and Shaping

A beard grows out of shape slowly, so you don't notice until the cheek line has drifted, the neck has gone fuzzy and one side is longer than the other.

Trimming, cutting and shaping are three different jobs.

A trim keeps your length and tidies it.

A cut takes the beard shorter. Shaping sets the cheek line and neckline so the edges look deliberate. We do all three, and we can do them in the same visit as your haircut so the lines on your head and your face match.
