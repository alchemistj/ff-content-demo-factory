What Is a BTU?
One of the most confusing terms homeowners hear is BTU.
BTU stands for British Thermal Unit.
Simply put, a BTU measures how much heat a furnace can produce or how much heat an air conditioner can remove.
Think of BTUs as horsepower for your HVAC system.
The higher the BTUs, the greater the heating or cooling capacity.
However, bigger isn't always better.
An oversized furnace can cycle on and off too frequently, wasting energy and creating uneven temperatures.
An undersized system may run continuously without ever making your home comfortable.
That's why proper sizing is just as important as buying a quality unit.
What Does "Tons" Mean on an Air Conditioner?
Air conditioners are measured in tons, not because of weight, but because of cooling capacity.
One ton of air conditioning equals 12,000 BTUs of cooling per hour.
For example:
1.5 Ton AC = 18,000 BTUs
2 Ton AC = 24,000 BTUs
2.5 Ton AC = 30,000 BTUs
3 Ton AC = 36,000 BTUs
4 Ton AC = 48,000 BTUs
5 Ton AC = 60,000 BTUs
The right size depends on factors like:
Home square footage
Ceiling height
Insulation
Window size and orientation
Air leakage
Climate
Sun exposure
Duct design
Choosing an AC based only on square footage can lead to poor performance if these other factors aren't considered.

Your HVAC System Is Only as Good as Your Ductwork
One of the biggest problems we find during HVAC inspections isn't the furnace at all.
It's the ductwork.
Your ducts are responsible for delivering conditioned air throughout your home.
If they're leaking, undersized, disconnected, or poorly designed, your HVAC system has to work much harder to keep your home comfortable.
Common duct issues include:
Air leaks
Poor airflow
Restricted returns
Improper sizing
Damaged insulation
Dust buildup
Loose connections
That's why we also offer:
Duct inspections
Duct repairs
Airflow evaluations
Duct cleaning recommendations
Improving airflow can often make your home noticeably more comfortable without immediately replacing your HVAC equipment.
Sometimes Your HVAC Isn't the Problem
Many homeowners assume high utility bills mean they need a new furnace or air conditioner.
In reality, your HVAC system may be working perfectly.
The problem could be that your home is losing the conditioned air you're paying for.
We inspect the entire building envelope to identify where energy is escaping.
Some of the biggest areas include:
Windows and Doors
Older windows and doors can account for a significant portion of your home's heating and cooling loss through air leaks and poor insulation.
Siding
Properly installed siding and house wrap help reduce air infiltration and improve overall energy efficiency.
Attic Insulation and Roofing
Heat naturally rises.
Without proper attic insulation and ventilation, your HVAC system works harder to compensate for energy loss through the roof.
