Top 3 Signs Of Siding Damage
Siding Is Warped or Pulling Away

When siding is pulling away from the wall or starting to warp, water could be getting behind it. Left unchecked, this can lead to serious structural damage.

Rising Energy Costs

If your heating/cooling costs are rising for no clear reason, your siding might have lost its insulating power. Properly functioning siding helps maintain your home's temperature, so rising bills often signal it's time for replacement.

Water Stains On Interior Ceiling or Walls

While some color change is normal over time, significant discoloration, fading, or damp spots indoors, could be a sign that your siding is no longer keeping moisture out.


Interior Warning Signs

Peeling wallpaper or paint and damp spots on your interior walls are red flags that your siding is letting water penetrate your home's structure. These issues rarely get better on their own and usually indicate serious siding issues.

Cracks, Holes, or Missing Sections

Even minor cracks can let moisture seep behind the siding, leading to mold and rot inside your walls. If you see visible damage, it’s time to take action.

Sneaky Dry Rot

If you find soft spots or fungus growth on your siding, there's a good chance the problem goes deeper than what you can see. Dry rot can spread quickly through your home's structure if not addressed promptly.

Siding Repair Vs. Replacement
Consider Siding Replacement If:
Large sections of siding show visible damage like warping, buckling, or rotting
Your energy bills have consistently increased over time
You notice drafts or temperature inconsistencies in your home
The siding is over 20-30 years old and shows multiple signs of aging
Consider Siding Repair If:
The damage is confined to small, specific areas of your home.
Your siding is less than 10 years old and the damage can be traced to a specific event rather than just wear and tear
The underlying structure of your home is in good condition with no moisture damage
Repair is cosmetic or limited to the surface layer of the siding material

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I just replace sections of damaged siding, or do I need to do the whole house?

Sometimes a partial replacement makes perfect sense, especially if you've got newer siding with just a few damaged spots. The trick is matching the color and style of your existing siding. Even if we can get the exact same product, sun exposure can cause slight fading that might make new sections stand out. We'll give you an honest assessment of whether a partial replacement will look right or if you'd be better off updating the whole wall or house.

Do I need to replace my insulation when installing new siding?

Siding replacement gives us the perfect opportunity to check out your home's insulation situation. Once we remove the old siding, we can see exactly what's going on under there. Sometimes the existing insulation is fine, but often we find spots that are compressed, water-damaged, or just inadequate by today's standards. Many homeowners choose to upgrade their insulation while we're already in there, since it can make a big difference in your comfort and energy bills.

How long does siding installation take?

For most homes, we can complete a full siding replacement in about 1-2 weeks, though this can vary depending on your home's size and style. We work efficiently but never rush. Each day our crew shows up early, works hard, and cleans up thoroughly before leaving. Weather can sometimes affect our timeline, but we'll always keep you updated on our progress and make sure your home is protected even if we need to pause for rain.
