Signs Of Gutter Damage
Gutters Sagging or Pulling Away
When gutters start to droop or separate from your house, they can't properly channel water away from your home. This means fasteners have failed or the gutters are clogged.
Water Stains or Rotting Fascia
Even small cracks in your gutters can lead to big problems. These breaks will eventually grow larger, leading to leaks that can damage your home's foundation and landscaping.
Cracks or Splits in Gutters
Dark streaks on your siding or rotting fascia boards are clear signs your gutters aren't doing their job, letting water seep where it shouldn't.
Basement Water Issues
Damp basement walls or water seeping into your basement might mean your gutters aren't directing water far enough away from your home's foundation.
Pooling Water or Eroded Soil
If you notice puddles around your foundation or dirt washing away from your landscaping, your gutters might be overflowing or misdirecting water.
Peeling Paint or Rust
When you spot peeling paint/rust spots on your gutters, it's a sign that water is consistently sitting where it shouldn't be. This kind of damage only gets worse over time.

Frequently Asked Questions
What Gutter Material Is Best For My Home?
Aluminum is the most common option due to its durability, rust resistance, and reasonable cost. Vinyl is another popular choice because of its lower price but keep in mind that it's less durable. As an experienced gutter installation company, we'll help you choose the best option for your home and budget.
What are the signs that I need new gutters rather than just repairs?
Major signs include multiple leaks, frequent clogs, visible sagging or pulling away from the house, widespread rust or cracks, and water damage to your home's foundation or landscaping. If your gutters are over 20 years old or showing several of these issues, replacement is usually more cost-effective than repairs.
Should I replace my gutters and downspouts at the same time?
Yes, it's highly recommended to replace both simultaneously. New gutters with old downspouts often lead to drainage issues, and your new gutters may not match the old downspouts in size, material, or color.
