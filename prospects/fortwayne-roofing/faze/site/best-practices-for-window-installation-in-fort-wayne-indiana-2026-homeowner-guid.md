How Replacement Windows Should Actually Be Installed (Before You Buy New Windows)
Best Practices for Window Installation in Fort Wayne, Indiana (2026 Homeowner Guide)
A Window Is Only as Good as the Installation
Many homeowners spend hours comparing window brands but overlook the single biggest factor that determines how well their new windows will perform: the quality of the installation.
Even the highest-quality replacement window can leak air, allow water intrusion, develop condensation issues, or fail prematurely if it isn't installed correctly.
At Faze Construction, we've found that proper installation is what separates a window that lasts 30 years from one that creates expensive problems after just a few seasons. That's one of the reasons we're proud to be a Certified ProVia Installer. ProVia not only manufactures exceptional windows—they also provide a clear installation system designed to maximize long-term performance.
Here's what every homeowner should expect during a professional window installation.
Step 1: Evaluate the Existing Window Opening
Before a new window ever goes into your home, the existing window must be carefully removed and inspected.
A professional installer should determine:
Does the siding need to be removed?
Are exterior trim or metal wraps reusable?
Is there hidden wood rot?
Is there mold or water damage?
Is the framing structurally sound?
Is the opening square and level?
Skipping this inspection often leads to installing a brand-new window into a damaged opening—which simply hides the problem until it becomes much more expensive.
Step 2: Prepare the Opening
Once the old window has been removed, the opening should be prepared before the replacement window is installed.
This includes inspecting the framing and installing proper moisture-management materials.
At Faze Construction, we install flashing tape around the window opening to help direct water away from vulnerable areas. Flashing extends into the opening and several inches onto the surrounding wall to help create a continuous moisture barrier.
This step helps prevent water intrusion long before it has a chance to damage your home.
Step 3: Install the Replacement Window Correctly
Most replacement projects today use what's known as a pocket window.
A pocket window fits inside the existing window frame instead of requiring complete structural reconstruction.
Once positioned, the installer should:
Level the window
Square the frame
Shim where necessary
Secure the window using manufacturer-approved fasteners
If the window isn't perfectly square, homeowners often experience:
Difficult operation
Drafts
Premature seal failure
Uneven gaps
Reduced energy efficiency
Precision during installation is critical.
Step 4: Seal the Window
After the window is secured, every gap between the new window and the framing should be properly insulated.
Professional installers typically use low-expansion foam designed specifically for windows and doors.
This helps:
Reduce air infiltration
Improve energy efficiency
Eliminate drafts
Prevent moisture intrusion
Once insulated, high-quality exterior sealants are applied around the perimeter where required to provide another layer of weather protection.
Step 5: Install Exterior Flashing & Trim
The exterior finishing work is just as important as installing the window itself.
This includes:
Exterior trim
Metal wrapping where applicable
Flashing details
Drip cap installation
One of the most overlooked components is the drip cap.
A properly installed drip cap should lap beneath the house wrap (such as Tyvek®) so water naturally sheds over the flashing—not behind it.
Good installation follows one simple principle:
Every layer should overlap the layer below it so water always drains away from the home.
Step 6: Quality Control
Before the project is complete, every window should be inspected.
Professional installers should verify:
Smooth operation
Proper locking
Weather seal alignment
Interior and exterior caulking
Exterior flashing
Finish work
Overall appearance
A window shouldn't simply look good—it should perform exactly as the manufacturer intended.

The Bottom Line
The window itself is only half of the investment.
The installation determines how that investment performs for decades.
That's why homeowners shouldn't just compare brands—they should compare installation methods, waterproofing practices, warranties, and the contractor's experience.
When installed correctly, a quality replacement window can improve comfort, increase energy efficiency, reduce outside noise, and add lasting value to your home.
