8 Signs It's Time for a New Roof in Fort Wayne
8 Signs It's Time for a New Roof in Fort Wayne
By Affordable Roofing · Published Oct 26, 2026
The short answer: A roof usually tells you it's wearing out before it fails. The signs to watch for are shingles that curl or cup, bald spots and heavy granules in the gutters, missing or lifted shingles after a windstorm, hail dents, ice dams in winter, stains in the attic or on the ceiling, a deck that sags or feels soft, and simple age. One sign on its own doesn't always mean a new roof. Two or three together usually do, and that's the time to get a professional look and a written quote.
The short version:
Shingles curling, cupping or going bald are classic signs of an aging roof. Owens Corning calls curling "a common sign that your roof may need to be replaced."
Granules piling up in gutters or near downspouts mean the shingles are losing their protective top layer.
Wind and hail can take off shingles or dent them. Check from the ground after big storms.
Ice dams, attic stains and a sagging deck point to problems under the shingles, often ventilation or wet decking.
Know your roof's age. If you don't know it, or the houses on your street are getting new roofs, it's time to look closer.

1. You don't know how old your roof is, or it's getting up there
Age is the first thing to check. Owens Corning's own homeowner checklist starts with "Check for warning signs" and "Know your roof's age" (Owens Corning Roofing 101 Guide).
How long a shingle roof lasts depends on the shingle, how well it was installed, how well the attic breathes, and how much sun, wind and ice it takes. So there's no single birthday when a roof has to be replaced. But if your roof was already on the house when you bought it and you've never seen paperwork, it's worth finding out. Look for:
Closing papers or a home inspection report that mentions the roof
A permit record for a past roof replacement
A receipt, warranty card or sticker from the last roofer
The same Owens Corning checklist also lists "Neighbors replacing roofs?" as a sign to pay attention to. Homes on the same street were often built and roofed around the same time, so if three houses on your block got new roofs this year, yours may not be far behind.
2. Shingles are curling or cupping
Walk across the street and look at your roof. Healthy shingles lie flat. Aging shingles start to change shape:
Curling: the edges or corners of the shingles turn up.
Cupping: the middle of a shingle tab sinks while the edges rise, like a shallow bowl.
Owens Corning lists curled edges and cupped tabs as warning signs and says that when you see them, "It's time to get a professional opinion." Curled shingles catch wind more easily and don't shed water the way they were designed to. Curling across a whole slope, not just one or two shingles, usually means the roof as a whole is near the end.

3. Bald spots on shingles and granules in the gutters
The colored granules on top of an asphalt shingle are what protect the asphalt underneath from the sun. As shingles age, they shed those granules. Owens Corning's guide lists "bald spots on shingles or large amounts of granules in gutters or near downspouts" as a warning sign and calls it "a sign of an aging roof."
Easy places to check:
The bottom of your downspouts, where a sandy, gritty pile builds up
Your gutters, the next time you clean them
The south- and west-facing slopes, which take the most afternoon sun
If you can see dark, smooth spots where the color is gone, the shingle is wearing through.
4. Missing, lifted or creased shingles after wind
Northeast Indiana gets strong wind with spring and summer storms. After a big one, look for:
Shingles lying in the yard or driveway
Bare strips or a different color where shingles used to be
Shingles that are lifted or creased across the top, which can mean the wind folded them back
Owens Corning's guide says to "look for missing shingles after high winds." One or two missing shingles on a young roof is a different situation than shingles blowing off an old roof every storm season. When the seal strips across the whole roof have weakened with age, wind keeps finding the weak spots. That's when replacement is the long-term answer.
5. Dents or bruising from hail
Hail can dent shingles, knock granules loose and bruise the mat underneath. From the ground, you often can't see it on the shingles themselves. Instead, check the things around them:
Dents in metal gutters, downspouts, roof vents and the air-conditioner cover
Fresh granules washed into the gutters right after a storm
Dings on painted window trim or the siding
Owens Corning's guide also lists "dents from hail storms" as a warning sign. If you see hail marks on the metal around your home, have someone look at the roof. Please don't climb up yourself. Wet or damaged shingles are slippery, and a fall isn't worth it.

6. Ice dams in the winter
An ice dam is a ridge of ice that forms along the bottom edge of the roof. Snow melts higher up, runs down, and refreezes at the cold eaves. Owens Corning explains that ice damming "can be a sign of poor attic ventilation" and can "cause water to back up into your attic, which can cause damage to walls and ceilings."
Big icicles along the gutters every winter are a hint. Ice dams don't always mean the shingles are worn out. But they tell you the roof system, including the attic ventilation and the ice and water protection at the eaves, isn't working the way it should. When we replace a roof, ventilation is part of the job, and the new roof goes on with the underlayment and edge protection the shingle manufacturer calls for.
7. Stains in the attic or on the ceiling, or daylight through the boards
Some of the clearest signs are inside the house. Take a flashlight into the attic on a bright day and look for:
Dark stains or water marks on the underside of the roof boards or on the rafters
Damp or matted insulation
Daylight showing through the roof boards
Rusty nail tips, which can mean moisture is condensing up there
Inside the living space, brown rings or bubbling paint on the ceiling, especially on the top floor, are worth taking seriously. Water that's getting into the attic now is soaking into the wood deck that your shingles are nailed to.

8. A sagging roofline or soft spots in the deck
Look at the ridge (the top line of the roof) and the slopes from the street. They should look straight and flat. A dip, a wave, or a sag between the rafters can mean the deck underneath has gotten wet and weak.
Those deck problems only show up for certain once the old shingles come off. That's why a good quote explains how decking is handled. Signs that don't mean you need a new roof on their own
Not everything that looks bad means replacement:
Dark streaks. Owens Corning says airborne algae can cause dark streaks on a roof and that they're "not necessarily harmful," though they may not look good. When it's time for a new roof, shingles with algae protection help.
Moss. Owens Corning notes moss "holds moisture and in freezing climates can cause damage to the granules." A little moss isn't an emergency, but if it keeps coming back, get advice.
A single damaged shingle on a young roof. One problem spot on a roof that's otherwise in good shape is a different conversation from a whole roof that's wearing out.

Frequently asked questions
How do I know if I really need a new roof?
Look for more than one sign at once. Curling or cupped shingles, bald spots, heavy granules in the gutters, shingles blowing off after wind, ice dams, attic stains and a sagging deck all point to a roof that's wearing out. Add the roof's age, and if two or three of these line up, it's time for a professional look and a written replacement quote.
Are granules in the gutters a sign I need a new roof?
Large amounts of granules in the gutters or at the bottom of downspouts, especially with bald spots on the shingles, are a sign of an aging roof, according to Owens Corning. The granules protect the asphalt from the sun, so once they're gone, the shingles wear faster. Check the sunniest slopes first.
Do curling shingles mean I need a new roof?
Usually, if the curling is spread across the roof. Owens Corning calls curled edges and cupped tabs a common sign that the roof may need to be replaced. Curled shingles catch wind and don't shed water the way flat shingles do. A free estimate will tell you whether it's a few shingles or the whole roof.
Can a new roof go over my old shingles?
On our full replacements, we tear the old roof off down to the deck. That lets us see and replace bad decking, and the new Owens Corning system goes on a clean base. We've torn off plenty of Fort Wayne roofs with two old layers, and removing them is part of the job.
How long does a roof replacement take?
About 95% of our roof replacements are torn off and installed in one day. Bigger or more complex roofs, or ones that need a lot of new decking, can take longer. Your quote will tell you what to expect for your home.
