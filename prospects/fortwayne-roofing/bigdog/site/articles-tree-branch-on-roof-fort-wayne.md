Tree Branch on Your Roof? Fort Wayne Recovery Steps
By Big Dog Roofing · Published September 27, 2026
If a tree branch lands on your Fort Wayne roof, keep people away from the impact area and arrange a professional assessment before anyone moves the limb. Stay off the roof. If there are damaged power lines, signs of collapse, fire, or an immediate threat to people, move to a safe location and call 911. Contact the utility about electrical hazards.
Once the area is safe, the work usually needs a clear sequence: assess the tree hazard, coordinate removal and temporary roof protection, inspect the exposed roof, and document the permanent repair. A branch resting on shingles does not automatically mean the whole roof needs replacement. It also does not establish that the roof is undamaged just because no water is dripping indoors.
Start with safety, not cleanup
A fallen limb can remain caught in another tree or supported by the house. Cutting or pulling it can change how its weight is supported. Leave that assessment to a qualified tree professional. Do not stand beneath hanging branches, drag a limb off the roof with a rope, or reach it from a ladder.
Keep children, pets, and visitors out of the affected area. If damage may involve the structure, stay out of the rooms beneath it and seek emergency guidance. Do not enter an attic under the impact point to look for a hole.
The National Weather Service’s post-storm safety guidance warns against touching downed wires or anything contacting them. Report the hazard rather than trying to decide whether a wire is energized. Photographs and property protection can wait until they can be handled safely.
Give the tree company and roofer the same information
Tree removal and roof repair are different jobs. When arranging an assessment, explain that the limb is on a building and describe any visible contact with wiring, gutters, a chimney, or an adjoining roof. Send photographs taken from a safe location if they are available.
For a Fort Wayne or Allen County property, useful access details include which side of the house is affected, whether a gate limits entry, and whether the damaged section is above an occupied room, porch, or garage. Describe what you can see without guessing the branch’s weight or the extent of hidden damage.
Ask who will coordinate the transition between removal and roof protection. Before work starts, clarify:
Who evaluates remaining tree hazards and controls access to the work area?
Who documents the roof before and after the branch is removed?
Who assesses any newly exposed opening and arranges temporary protection?
Who contacts you if removal reveals damage beyond the original estimate?
Which work, debris handling, and follow-up visits are included in each written scope?
Do not assume the tree company’s invoice includes roofing, or that a roofing appointment includes tree removal. Confirm the responsibilities directly with each contractor.
Document the scene without delaying necessary protection
From a safe position, capture a wide view showing the affected side of the house and a closer view using your camera’s zoom. Note when you discovered the damage, where the branch landed, and whether you observed any interior water. Keep the original image files.
Ask the contractors to supply photographs of areas you cannot safely see. A useful record distinguishes the branch in place, the condition immediately after removal, temporary measures, and completed repairs. For more detail on organizing those images, see our roof storm-damage photo guide.
If you are pursuing an insurance claim, contact your insurer promptly for policy-specific instructions. The Indiana Department of Insurance’s claim tips recommend documenting damage, retaining receipts, and distinguishing temporary protection from permanent repairs. Ask how inspection and emergency work should be coordinated. Do not postpone necessary safety measures to obtain a better photograph.
Save itemized invoices separately for tree work, temporary roof protection, inspection, and permanent construction. Keeping those categories clear helps explain what happened; it does not guarantee reimbursement. The insurer determines coverage under the policy.
Inspect the roof after the branch is removed
The initial view may be blocked by the limb itself. Request a roofing assessment once the tree hazard is cleared and safe access is established. Ask for the findings to identify the exact roof section and show the affected materials.
The review should address the contact area and nearby components that may have been struck or displaced:
Roof covering: Identify punctured, torn, displaced, or otherwise damaged shingles or metal components.
Underlying materials: Explain whether the inspection found evidence requiring a closer look at underlayment or roof decking.
Transitions: Check affected flashing near walls, valleys, vents, and chimneys.
Roof edges: Document damage to gutters, fascia, soffits, or the connection between them.
Structural concerns: Identify findings that require evaluation beyond a surface roofing repair.
If something remains concealed or inaccessible, the report should say so. “No damage visible from the yard” and “the affected assembly was inspected” are different findings. An inspection should also separate impact-related observations from older wear without guessing when an undocumented condition began.
Compare the repair scope with the documented damage
A localized repair may be appropriate when the affected materials can be restored and connected to the surrounding roof reliably. Broader work needs an explanation tied to the roof’s actual condition, material compatibility, or construction requirements. Neither the size of the tree nor a single photograph settles that decision.
Ask for a written description of the repair area, materials, flashing work, and any decking allowance. If opening the roof may reveal additional work, establish how it will be photographed, priced, and authorized. Our roofing change-order guide explains what to clarify before those decisions arise.
Keep construction recommendations separate from insurance decisions. Big Dog Roofing can provide roof findings and a contractor’s scope; those documents do not promise a particular claim outcome. You should understand the proposed work and authorize it on its own terms.
Close the job with a usable record
Before considering the roof work complete, request the final photographs and a description of any remaining limitations or recommended follow-up. Confirm whether temporary materials have been removed or whether another visit is still needed. Keep the tree-removal and roofing records together.
Have a tree professional assess any remaining tree concerns separately. Removing the limb that landed on the house does not answer every question about the rest of the tree. For future maintenance planning, keep that assessment alongside the roof report rather than assuming either contractor evaluated the other’s specialty.
Frequently asked questions
Can I pull a small branch off the roof myself?
Do not climb onto the roof or pull a lodged branch from below. Its support, nearby wires, and the roof condition may be unclear. Describe the situation to an appropriate professional and keep people clear while it is assessed.
Should I call a tree company or a roofer first?
Immediate threats go to emergency services or the utility first. Once those hazards are addressed, tree and roofing professionals may need to coordinate. Tell both that the branch is on the building and clarify who handles removal, roof assessment, and temporary protection.
Does a branch strike mean I need a whole new roof?
No. The recommendation should follow the documented damage, the surrounding roof’s condition, and whether a durable repair is practical. Ask why a localized repair, a larger section, or full replacement is being proposed.
Will insurance pay for tree removal and roof repairs?
Ask your insurer to explain coverage for your specific loss, including applicable deductibles, exclusions, and limits. Retain photographs and itemized invoices. A contractor’s estimate documents proposed work but cannot establish coverage or guarantee payment.
