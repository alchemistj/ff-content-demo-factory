Solar Panel Removal and Reinstallation During Roof Replacement
The roofing crew should not improvise around an energized array. Solar removal, roof work, new attachment flashing, and system restart need one coordinated schedule.

If solar panels cover the roof area being replaced, plan a professional detach and reset before tear-off. The solar or qualified electrical team safely shuts down, maps, removes, and stores the array. The roofer replaces the roof and coordinates the new attachment locations and flashing. The solar team returns, reinstalls, tests, and documents the system.
Do not let the schedule start with a roofing crew waiting under panels that nobody is authorized to remove. Get roles, dates, storage, roof scope, attachment details, and restart responsibility in writing. Our broader solar and roof planning guide covers what to settle before the original solar installation.
Stage	Primary responsibility	What to document
Pre-job survey	Solar provider and roofer together	Array map, roof condition, equipment labels, attachment locations, access, and schedule
Shutdown and removal	Qualified solar or electrical team	Safe isolation, panel condition, wiring, hardware, and storage inventory
Roof replacement	Roofing contractor	Deck repairs, underlayment, flashing plan, ventilation, and protected attachment zones
Reinstallation	Qualified solar team	New mounts and flashing, wiring, torque or fastening requirements, panel map, and weatherproofing
Commissioning	Solar or electrical team	Testing, monitoring, inspection or utility steps when required, and handoff records
Choose Who Owns the Detach and Reset
Start with the solar contract and current provider. Ask whether it requires an approved service company, whether equipment coverage changes if another party works on the system, and which permits or inspections may apply. If the original company is unavailable, hire a qualified team that can identify the equipment and assume written responsibility for removal and restart.
The roofer should not disconnect electrical equipment outside its scope. The solar company should not cut or patch roofing without coordinating the roof system. One party needs to manage the calendar, but each trade keeps responsibility for its own work.
Inventory and Store the Equipment
Before removal, photograph the array, panel positions, labels, conduit, wire paths, optimizers or electronics, mounts, and existing damage. Number components if the solar team intends to return them to the same layout. Record cracked glass, loose frames, damaged wiring, and failed monitoring before work begins.
Panels need a secure storage location protected from impact, theft, weather, and construction debris. Old mounts, lag screws, flashing, and sealants may not be reusable. The contract should identify what will be replaced and who supplies it.
Build the New Roof for the New Attachments
Tear-off is the opportunity to inspect deck areas hidden below the array. Photograph old penetrations, stains, soft decking, and attachment damage. Unused holes must be repaired as part of the roof assembly, not left under underlayment. The solar team and roofer should agree on approved attachment zones before shingles or metal details are finalized.
New mounts need flashing compatible with the roofing material and the solar attachment system. Surface sealant alone is not a complete flashing plan. Keep attachments away from weak deck edges and roof transitions where the system instructions require clearance.
Schedule the Reset and Verify Operation
The roof should be complete and ready for attachments before the solar team returns. Delays can leave a working roof but an idle array, so include realistic weather and inspection contingencies. Do not pressure either crew to work on an unsafe wet or icy roof just to recover production for one day.
After reinstallation, verify system monitoring, panel layout, visible wiring, conduit support, attachments, and roof penetrations. Save commissioning records and completion photos. Ask who handles a leak near a mount and how the two contractors coordinate if a problem appears later.
How to Coordinate a Solar Detach and Reset
Review the solar contract Identify approved service requirements, equipment terms, permits, inspections, and responsible contacts.
Survey and inventory the array Map panels, equipment, wiring, mounts, roof conditions, and pre-existing damage.
Shut down and remove safely Use the qualified solar or electrical team and secure every component for storage.
Replace and document the roof Repair old penetrations and decking, complete the roof system, and mark approved attachment zones.
Install new compatible attachments Use approved mounts and flashing coordinated with the new roofing material.
Test and hand off the system Verify monitoring, electrical operation, roof penetrations, inspections, and completion records.
Questions to Settle Before Signing
Who is authorized to remove the system? Get the company, contact, license or qualification, and scope in writing.
What equipment will be replaced? Separate reusable panels from new mounts, flashing, wiring, or damaged components.
Where will everything be stored? Assign a secure location and responsibility for damage or theft.
How do the schedules connect? Include shutdown, removal, roof work, weather delay, reset, testing, and inspection time.
Who responds to a later problem? Define the first call for a roof leak, solar fault, monitoring issue, or damaged panel.
What Changes in Fort Wayne and Northeast Indiana
Fort Wayne weather makes coordination important. A hail or wind claim can create high demand for both trades. Winter work adds safe-access and material concerns. Book the solar team early and build weather flexibility into the roofing calendar instead of removing panels long before the roof crew is ready.
Snow and leaves can collect around lower array edges, while summer heat makes rooftop electrical work demanding. The completed design should preserve roof drainage, required access, ventilation, and service paths. Do not crowd mounts against valleys, ridges, or flashing just to recreate the old layout exactly.

Mistakes That Make the Problem Harder
Do not let an unqualified crew disconnect or reconnect energized solar equipment.
Do not reuse damaged mounts, fasteners, or flashing by default.
Do not cover old attachment holes without repairing the deck and roof assembly.
Do not restart the system without testing and documented handoff.
Frequently Asked Questions
Can roofers work around solar panels without removing them?
Small repairs may be possible with safe access, but a full replacement under the array usually requires removal. Working around panels can leave old roofing and flashing trapped below.
Who pays for solar removal during an insurance roof claim?
Coverage depends on the policy, cause of loss, carrier scope, and claim decision. Ask the carrier in writing and document why removal is necessary for the covered roof work.
Can the same solar panels be reinstalled?
Often, if inspection shows they are serviceable and the system plan supports reuse. Mounts, flashing, fasteners, wiring, or damaged equipment may need replacement.
Will the array go back in the exact same place?
Not always. New roof layout, deck condition, attachment requirements, access, code or inspection needs, and current solar design may justify adjusted locations.
