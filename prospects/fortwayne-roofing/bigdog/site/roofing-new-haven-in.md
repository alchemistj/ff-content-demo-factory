Common Roofing Issues for New Haven Homeowners
Northeast Indiana's weather puts roofs through the wringer. Here are the challenges we see most often when working on New Haven homes.
Hail & Storm Damage
Allen County sits right in the path of severe spring and summer storms that roll across the Indiana flatlands. Hail can crack and bruise shingles in ways that aren't always visible from the ground, leading to slow leaks that cause expensive interior damage if left unchecked. After a major storm, a prompt inspection can document conditions and help limit further water intrusion. Any insurance coverage remains subject to the policy and carrier's decision.
Ice Dams & Freeze-Thaw Cycles
New Haven winters bring repeated freezing and thawing that wreaks havoc on roofing materials. Ice dams form along the eaves when attic heat melts snow on the upper roof, and the refrozen water backs up under shingles. Many older ranch homes in the Meadowbrook and Lincoln Park areas were built without adequate ice-and-water shield underlayment — something we always address during a replacement.
Aging Post-War Roofing
A large portion of New Haven's housing stock dates to the 1950s and 1960s expansion along Lincoln Highway. Many of these homes still have their original roof structure with outdated ventilation systems and thin decking. When we replace a roof on one of these homes, we inspect the decking for rot, upgrade ventilation, and install modern synthetic underlayment to bring everything up to current standards.
Wind Uplift on Low-Slope Roofs
The ranch-style homes common in New Haven often have lower roof pitches that are more vulnerable to wind uplift during severe storms. Shingles on these roofs need proper starter strip installation and high-wind-rated fastening patterns. Big Dog Roofing uses six-nail patterns and wind-rated starter courses on every New Haven project, even when code only requires four nails.
