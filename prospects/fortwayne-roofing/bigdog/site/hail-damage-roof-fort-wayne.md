How Hail Damages Your Roof
When hailstones strike asphalt shingles, they create damage that's often invisible from the ground but devastating to your roof's protective capability. Here's what hail does:
Granule displacement. The impact knocks loose the ceramic granules that protect shingles from UV rays and moisture. Without these granules, the asphalt layer deteriorates rapidly. You might notice dark spots on shingles or extra granules in your gutters after a storm.
Shingle bruising. Hail can compress the fiberglass mat inside shingles without breaking the surface, creating soft spots that weaken over time. These bruises accelerate cracking and allow water infiltration months or even years after the storm.
Cracked shingles. Larger hailstones can crack shingles outright, creating immediate pathways for water. These cracks are sometimes visible from the roof surface but are easy to miss from ground level.
Damaged flashing and accessories. Metal flashing, vent covers, pipe boots, and ridge caps can be dented, bent, or displaced by hail. These components are critical to a watertight roof system.
The challenge with hail damage is that it often doesn't cause immediate leaks. Instead, it compromises your roof's integrity over time, leading to premature aging and eventual failure years before the roof should have reached end of life.

Navigating Insurance Claims for Hail Damage
A hail claim involves separate homeowner, carrier, and contractor decisions. Big Dog Roofing stays in the construction lane and helps with factual roof information:
Documentation. We provide dated photos, measurements, observed conditions, and a written construction estimate. Ask your carrier which documents it requires.
Homeowner filing. You decide whether to report a loss and submit the claim through your insurer's official channel. Questions about policy terms, deadlines, and coverage belong to the carrier or a qualified adviser you choose.
Adjuster inspection. At your request, we can attend an adjuster visit to point out documented roof conditions and explain our construction scope. The adjuster and carrier perform their own evaluation.
Scope comparison. We can compare our estimate with the carrier estimate and support any requested construction revision with measurements, photos, product instructions, or documented requirements. The carrier approves, changes, or declines requested items.
Authorized work. After you understand the approved and homeowner-paid scope, we schedule the work you authorize and provide the completion documents required for the roofing project.
