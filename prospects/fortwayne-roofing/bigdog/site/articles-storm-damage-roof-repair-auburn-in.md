LOCAL GUIDE
Auburn storms can damage a roof without making the home look obviously wrecked from the street. Wind can lift shingles, hail can bruise them, and heavy rain can expose weak flashing. The first job after a storm is to protect the home, document what happened, and avoid guessing from the ground.
By Big Dog Roofing · Published April 30, 2026 · Updated August 9, 2026
What to check after a storm
Walk the property from the ground and look for missing shingles, lifted tabs, granules near downspouts, dents on gutters or vents, loose flashing, fallen branches, and ceiling stains. Do not climb a wet or damaged roof.
Why documentation matters
Photos of roof damage, gutter dents, interior leaks, and the storm date can help you make better decisions if an insurance claim becomes necessary. A professional inspection gives you a clearer record before permanent repairs begin.
When emergency repair is needed
Call for help quickly if water is entering the home, shingles are missing over living space, decking is exposed, or a tree limb damaged the roof. Temporary protection may be needed before the full repair is scheduled.
