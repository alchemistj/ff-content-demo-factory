Roof Damage From Squirrels, Raccoons, Birds, and Other Animals
Repair the roof opening after the animal is safely out. Closing an active entry first can trap wildlife inside and push it into a new part of the house.

Squirrels and raccoons can enlarge loose soffit or fascia openings. Birds can enter damaged vents and build nests. Woodpeckers may target trim or siding. Small animals use lifted shingles, uncapped gaps, and weak screens. The lasting repair removes or excludes the animal first, then rebuilds the roof or exterior detail with materials that restore drainage and ventilation.
Do not seal an opening while an animal may be inside. Young animals can be separated from the adult, trapped wildlife can damage ceilings and ducts, and a blocked animal may tear a second exit. A qualified wildlife professional and roofing contractor often need to coordinate.
Branches close to a roof can create access routes and hide disturbance around edges, vents, and roof transitions.

Where Animals Enter
The eave is a common entry because soffit and fascia can loosen, rot, or separate. Roof-to-wall gaps, damaged gable vents, uncapped chimneys, cracked vent covers, open ridge ends, and holes around utilities provide other paths. Tree limbs and nearby structures make access easier but are not the opening themselves.
Inspect from the ground for dark rub marks, chewed edges, bent panels, droppings, nesting material, and repeated animal traffic. In the attic, use a stable walkway and look for daylight, compressed insulation, trails, damaged ducts, and staining. Avoid direct contact with animals, droppings, or nesting material.
Why the Roof Repair Comes Second
A roofer can identify and weatherproof damage, but wildlife behavior and humane removal require different expertise. Exclusion may use a one-way device or a timed process based on the animal and season. The entry remains controlled until the professional confirms the space is clear.
Once clear, the roofer replaces damaged deck, shingles, flashing, fascia, soffit, vent, or screen in the correct sequence. Stuffing a hole with foam or surface mesh may block one gap while trapping moisture or leaving the surrounding rotten wood untouched.
Ventilation Must Survive the Repair
Soffit and ridge openings often serve attic ventilation. Closing them with solid material can stop an animal and stop the intended airflow. Use compatible insect or animal screening that preserves the required opening and does not create a debris trap.
Bath and dryer terminations need proper exterior covers and should not be tied into attic ventilation. A nest in a duct can restrict airflow and create moisture or fire concerns depending on the appliance. Have the appropriate trade inspect the complete duct before putting the fan back into service.
Prevent the Next Entry
Repair soft fascia, loose soffit, failed screens, open ridge ends, and damaged vent covers. Trim branches that provide easy access when proper tree work can be done without harming the roof. Keep gutters clear enough to inspect the eave and correct water problems that rot wood.
Monitor the repaired area for new noise, disturbed screening, droppings, or material pulled loose. An animal accustomed to one entry may test nearby weak spots. Prevention is strongest when the whole roof edge and attic are checked, not just the obvious hole.
A Safe Animal-Damage Response
Confirm activity without contact. Record sound timing, exterior travel, droppings, nesting, and the likely entry from a safe distance.
Protect people and pets. Keep the attic and affected rooms closed and avoid disturbing wildlife or contaminated material.
Use qualified exclusion. Identify the animal, occupants, season, and legal or humane removal method before closure.
Rebuild the roof detail. Replace damaged wood, roofing, flashing, vent, soffit, or fascia while preserving drainage and airflow.
Inspect nearby weak points. Check the full eave, vents, ridge ends, walls, chimney, and tree-side access for repeat entry.
What Changes in Fort Wayne and Northeast Indiana
Fort Wayne homes with mature trees, older soffits, and varied roof additions provide many access routes. Freeze-thaw movement can open small trim gaps, and spring nesting activity makes timing important. Do not assume a quiet attic means the space is empty after one night.
Storm damage can create animal entry before it creates an obvious interior leak. A lifted ridge cap, broken vent cover, or branch impact may open the attic. After wind or hail, include vents, soffit, fascia, and screens in the exterior check.

Mistakes That Make the Problem Harder
Do not close an opening until the space is confirmed clear.
Do not handle wildlife, droppings, or nests without the right protection and expertise.
Do not block a ventilation opening with solid foam or an improvised patch.
Do not climb onto the roof to chase an animal.
Frequently Asked Questions
Can squirrels chew through a roof?
They can enlarge weak wood, soffit, vent, or edge openings and damage nearby materials. They usually exploit an accessible weak point rather than chewing through a sound complete roof assembly.
Who should I call first, a roofer or wildlife removal?
If an animal may be inside, start with qualified wildlife exclusion while a roofer documents and protects the damaged roof area. Coordinate final closure after the space is clear.
Will homeowner insurance cover animal roof damage?
Coverage varies by policy, animal, cause, and whether damage is considered sudden or related to infestation or maintenance. Ask your carrier and document the condition before repair.
Can I screen over a roof vent?
Use screening compatible with the vent that preserves required airflow and weather performance. Improvised fine mesh can clog, restrict ventilation, or redirect water.
