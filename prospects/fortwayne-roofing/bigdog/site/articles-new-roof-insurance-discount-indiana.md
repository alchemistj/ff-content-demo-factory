Will a New Roof Lower My Indiana Homeowners Insurance? (2026 Guide)

Short answer: Maybe. Some Indiana carrier programs consider roof age, material, impact rating, or documented mitigation features. Others offer no premium credit, apply different deductibles, or use the roof information mainly for underwriting. A replacement does not by itself guarantee a discount, renewal, or replacement-cost coverage.
The useful question is not whether every new roof earns a discount. It is what your specific carrier will do with the exact product and installation at your address. This guide explains what to ask before signing a contract, what Big Dog can document after construction, and which decisions remain with the agent or carrier.
1. What to Verify Before Counting on a Discount
Ask your agent or carrier whether it currently offers a new-roof, roof-age, impact-resistant, or wind-mitigation program for your policy. Then request a written quote using the exact proposed shingle and installation details. Program availability can depend on the policy form, property location, roof material, installation date, rating documentation, deductible, and other underwriting factors.
Also ask whether the roof update changes anything besides price, such as wind or hail deductibles, settlement terms, inspections, or renewal eligibility. A lower premium is only one part of the policy comparison, and replacing the roof does not guarantee that any particular coverage or renewal term will continue.
2. Why Carriers Ask About Roof Details
Carriers may use roof age, condition, material, installation details, and tested product ratings when evaluating property risk. Those details can affect pricing, deductibles, inspections, available endorsements, or whether the carrier is willing to write or renew a policy.
Each carrier builds and changes its own underwriting rules. Big Dog Roofing can identify the roof system it installs and provide available construction records. Your licensed agent or carrier is the appropriate source for policy interpretation, program eligibility, premium calculations, and renewal decisions.
3. How Carrier Programs Can Differ
A program that exists for one carrier, policy, or address may not exist for another. Use the questions below to compare written answers instead of relying on a generic discount chart.
Program factor	What to ask	Useful documentation
Roof-age update	Does a completed replacement change premium, deductible, coverage, inspection, or renewal terms?	Final invoice, installation date, completion photos, permit record if applicable.
Impact resistance	Which exact products and test standards are eligible under the current program?	Exact product name plus current manufacturer or testing documentation requested by the carrier.
Wind mitigation	Which construction features qualify, and is a carrier form or independent inspection required?	Contractor scope, product records, photos, and any required inspection form.
Effective date	When would an approved change begin, and will the carrier issue updated policy documents?	Carrier confirmation, revised quote, and updated declarations page.
Ask for a before-and-after quote that identifies all meaningful changes, not just the premium. Keep the written response with your project records, and confirm the final terms again after the carrier reviews the completed-work documents.
4. Impact-Resistant Products and Program Eligibility
Some carriers have programs tied to an impact-resistance test standard or an approved-product list. A marketing name or product family alone is not enough to establish eligibility: product versions, ratings, and carrier rules can change.
Before selecting an upgrade, send your agent or carrier the exact proposed product and ask whether it qualifies under the current program, what documentation is required, and whether any policy terms change. 5. How a Policy Change Is Actually Calculated
A carrier may apply a credit to one rating component, re-rate the whole policy, change a deductible or endorsement, or make no pricing change. The result can also be affected by other rating changes that occur at the same time, so a generic percentage cannot reliably predict the new total premium.
Request a written comparison showing the current and proposed premium, deductibles, roof settlement terms, endorsements, and effective date. That carrier-issued comparison—not a roofing contractor's estimate—is the appropriate basis for an insurance savings calculation.
6. How to Request a Roof Update
Start before construction by asking the agent or carrier which exact products, forms, photos, invoices, permits, or inspections it requires. After completion, gather the final invoice showing the installation date and products, available manufacturer documentation, completion photos, and permit or inspection records when applicable.
Submit the requested records through the channel the carrier specifies, then ask for written confirmation of its decision and any revised declarations page or premium quote. Review times, effective dates, backdating, and document standards vary. Big Dog can provide records from its project file but cannot approve or time the carrier's review.
7. Wind and Hail Mitigation Programs
Some carrier programs evaluate specific materials, fastening patterns, deck attachment, secondary water barriers, or other construction features. Eligibility and proof requirements are carrier-specific, and a feature that appears on one form may not qualify under another program.
Ask for the current carrier form before construction when possible. Big Dog can describe the contracted and installed roof system and provide available project photos or product records. A carrier may require its own inspection or form, and it retains the decision on any credit. 8. Roof Age and Underwriting
Carriers may consider roof age and condition when pricing, inspecting, writing, or renewing a policy. They may also use different settlement terms for the roof. There is no universal age at which every Indiana carrier changes coverage or declines renewal.
Review your declarations, endorsements, and renewal notices, then ask the agent or carrier to explain any roof-specific deductible or actual-cash-value provision. Replacing an older roof can address a physical condition, but it does not guarantee renewal or replacement-cost coverage. 9. What to Ask Your Insurance Agent
Before you sign a roofing contract, ask your agent these questions: (1) Does my current policy offer any roof-age, impact-resistant, or mitigation program? (2) Which exact products and test standards are eligible? (3) What documents or inspections are required? (4) Would the update change my premium, wind or hail deductible, settlement terms, endorsements, or renewal eligibility? (5) What are the review process and effective-date rules?
Get the answers and any quote in writing. That lets you compare the insurance terms with the contractor's upgrade price without assuming a future credit.
