Should You Replace Gutters With a New Roof?
Gutters do not need automatic replacement with every roof. The right answer depends on their condition, capacity, roof-edge details, fascia, discharge, and how the crews sequence the work.

A new roof does not automatically require new gutters. Straight, correctly pitched gutters with sound hangers, adequate outlets, working downspouts, and good fascia may stay. Damaged, undersized, leaking, poorly pitched, or badly integrated gutters may be smarter to address while roof-edge work is open.
Make the decision before crews arrive. Roofers need clear access to drip edge, fascia, starter shingles, and eaves. Gutter installers need to know the finished roof-edge detail. If each trade assumes the other will handle it, homeowners can end up with bent gutters, blocked drip edge, duplicate labor, or water running behind the system.

Inspect the Whole Water Path
Start at the roof planes and valleys feeding each gutter section. Then check pitch, standing water, hangers, seams, outlets, elbows, downspouts, extensions, buried lines, and final discharge. An overflowing inside corner may need better capture or outlet capacity, not an entirely new system.
Look for streaks behind the gutter, soft fascia, peeling paint, washed mulch, erosion, and foundation-side pooling. These clues show where water leaves the intended path. Fix the first failed detail instead of adding a guard or seal downstream.
Decide Whether Gutters Stay During Tear-Off
Some roofs can be replaced with gutters protected in place. Other eaves require removal for rotten fascia, deck-edge repair, or correct metal integration. Put the decision in the scope: keep, protect, detach, store, reinstall, reseal, repair, or replace.
If gutters remain, ask how ladders, tarps, falling shingles, and cleanup will be managed. Photograph the existing system before work. That gives everyone a fair record if a seam opens or a section changes pitch.
Sequence Roof Edge, Fascia, and New Gutters
When both systems are new, the roofing crew typically needs to complete the deck edge, fascia repairs, underlayment, starter, and drip-edge details before final gutter placement. The exact sequence depends on the assembly and manufacturer instructions, so both scopes should name the handoff.
Confirm which contractor supplies apron or edge metal, who seals penetrations, who protects the finished roof, and who tests the gutters. Do not let a gutter fastener or splash guard compromise the new roof detail.
Verify Flow Before Closeout
After installation, inspect continuous pitch, hanger spacing, seams, outlets, and downspout connections. Use controlled water only where safe and appropriate, or observe during normal rain. Watch every discharge point for backup, leakage, erosion, or water returning toward the home.
Guards are a separate decision. They can reduce certain debris loads, but they do not correct bad pitch, inadequate outlets, or a blocked underground line. Build a maintenance schedule around the actual trees and roof geometry.
Keep, Reset, Repair, or Replace?
Keep them. Choose this when condition, pitch, capacity, attachment, and integration are sound.
Detach and reset. Use this when edge access is needed but the gutter system remains serviceable.
Repair selected sections. Correct localized seams, hangers, outlets, or fascia after confirming the rest of the path.
Replace the system. Choose coordinated replacement for widespread damage, poor capacity, bad layout, or failed attachment.
Test the handoff. Verify the finished roof edge, gutter flow, downspouts, and discharge together.

Frequently Asked Questions
Will roofers damage gutters during replacement?
Careful protection reduces risk, but work at the eaves can affect gutters. Photograph them first and put keep, detach, repair, or replacement responsibility in writing.
Should gutters be installed before or after the new roof?
The sequence depends on the assembly, but roof-edge and fascia work should be coordinated before final gutter placement. Both scopes should identify the handoff.
Can good gutters stay with a new roof?
Yes, when condition, pitch, capacity, attachment, and roof-edge integration are sound and the contractor can complete the roof correctly around them.
Do gutter guards make sense during roof replacement?
Evaluate them after pitch, outlets, downspouts, roof-edge details, and discharge work correctly. Guards reduce some debris; they do not eliminate maintenance.
