How Our Contractor-Side Claim Support Works
Here is what Big Dog Roofing can do after suspected storm damage, and where the carrier's role begins:
Step 1: Free Storm Damage Inspection
Everything starts with our comprehensive 21-point roof inspection. One of our trained inspectors climbs your roof and examines every slope, every flashing point, every vent, and every accessory for signs of storm damage. We check for hail impacts, wind-lifted shingles, cracked ridge caps, dented gutters, and damaged pipe boots.
We photograph every finding in detail — close-up shots of individual damage points, wide-angle shots showing damage patterns across slopes, and overview images that document the full scope. This photo documentation becomes the foundation of your insurance claim. We also check your siding, windows, outdoor HVAC units, and other property for collateral storm damage that may be part of your claim.
If we don't find damage, we tell you honestly. We never fabricate or exaggerate damage to generate work. Our reputation depends on integrity, and that's not something we're willing to compromise.
Step 2: Filing Your Claim
If our inspection documents conditions consistent with storm damage, we provide a written report and photo set. When you report the loss, describe the date, event, and conditions you observed accurately. Your insurer will ask policy and cause-of-loss questions and will make the coverage determination.
We can explain our roof findings, estimate, and construction scope. For policy language, filing deadlines, deductibles, coverage, or possible premium effects, rely on your agent or insurer.
Step 3: Adjuster Meeting
When scheduling allows and the homeowner requests it, we can attend the carrier's roof inspection. Our role is to show the conditions we documented, answer construction questions, and provide measurements or product details.
The adjuster and carrier independently evaluate cause, coverage, and their estimate. Our presence does not guarantee that any item will be covered or included.
Step 4: Scope Review & Supplements
After the adjuster's visit, your insurance company issues an estimate — usually generated in Xactimate, the industry-standard estimating software. We review this estimate line by line against our own documentation and the actual scope of work needed.
If the carrier estimate and our construction scope differ, we identify the specific differences. They can involve measurements, drip edge, underlayment, ventilation, flashing, gutters, or other documented work.
When appropriate, we prepare a contractor supplement package with photographs, measurements, estimates, and applicable code or manufacturer references. A supplement is a request, not a guarantee; the carrier can approve, modify, or deny each item on its own timeline.
Step 5: Project Authorization & Completion Records
After you understand the carrier's written scope and choose to authorize Big Dog Roofing, we schedule the roof repair or replacement and handle material ordering, crews, and project management under our construction agreement.
Payment structure varies by policy, carrier, deductible, depreciation terms, exclusions, limits, and lender involvement. We provide contracts, invoices, completion photos, and other contractor records the carrier requests. The carrier and any mortgage servicer decide payment and release timing.
What Does Homeowners Insurance Cover on Your Roof?
The examples below describe common policy patterns, not a coverage determination. Your policy language and carrier's review control your claim.
What's Typically Covered
Hail or wind. Many homeowners policies list wind and hail as covered causes of loss, but roof-age provisions, cosmetic exclusions, separate deductibles, and other endorsements can change the result.
Fallen trees, lightning, or fire. These events may be covered subject to cause, exclusions, limits, and the carrier's review. Tree removal and resulting roof work can be treated differently.
Ice or resulting water damage. Coverage varies depending on the source, timing, maintenance history, exclusions, and policy wording. Ask the carrier to explain the applicable provision.
What's NOT Covered
Wear, age, and maintenance. Policies commonly exclude deterioration and maintenance-related conditions. A roof can also have both old wear and new storm damage, so document the conditions and let the carrier apply the policy.
Known or continuing problems. Delayed repairs or repeated leakage can affect coverage. Follow the policy's notice and mitigation instructions and keep records of temporary work.
Cosmetic damage (some policies). Some Indiana insurance policies include a "cosmetic damage exclusion" for hail. This means that if hail dents your shingles but doesn't impair their function, the damage may not be covered. Check your policy declarations page for this exclusion — if you have it, consider asking your agent about removing it at renewal.
RCV vs. ACV Policies
Your roof claim payout depends heavily on whether you have a Replacement Cost Value (RCV) or Actual Cash Value (ACV) policy.
RCV provisions generally calculate covered work using replacement cost, subject to the deductible, policy limits, exclusions, approved scope, and actual incurred cost. Some policies withhold eligible depreciation until documented work is complete.
ACV provisions subtract depreciation from covered replacement cost. The carrier determines the depreciation and payment under the policy, which can leave a larger homeowner-paid difference.
Understanding Deductibles in Indiana
Your declarations page may show a flat deductible or a separate wind/hail deductible calculated as a percentage of dwelling coverage. For example, 2% of $300,000 is $6,000. Confirm which deductible applies with your carrier before filing.
Understanding Your Insurance Estimate
When a carrier issues an estimate, compare its covered scope, quantities, deductible, depreciation, and exclusions with the contractor's construction estimate. The documents serve different roles and may not match line for line.
What Is Xactimate?
Many carriers and adjusters use Xactimate or another estimating system. A carrier estimate may contain line items, quantities, and unit prices; it remains the carrier's estimate, not the contractor's promise to perform every item for that amount.
Line Items Homeowners Should Look For
Your Xactimate estimate should include line items for every component that needs repair or replacement. Key items to verify include:
Tear-off of existing roofing materials (removal and disposal)
Underlayment (synthetic felt or ice and water shield)
Shingle installation (including starter strip and ridge cap)
Drip edge (metal edging along eaves and rakes)
Flashing replacement at walls, chimneys, and valleys
Pipe boot and vent replacements
Ridge vent or other ventilation components
Gutter and downspout replacement if damaged
Overhead and profit (O&P) for the contractor
Common Scope Differences We Review
Carrier and contractor estimates can differ on measurements, drip edge, underlayment, ventilation, flashing, gutters, and documented interior work.
We tie any requested revision to conditions on the property, applicable requirements, and the work in our estimate. Code-related coverage depends on the adopted requirement and the policy's ordinance-or-law terms. The carrier decides whether each item is covered.
What Is a Supplement?
A supplement is a documented request to revise quantities or items in a carrier estimate. We can submit contractor-side photos, measurements, estimates, and applicable references. The carrier reviews the request and can approve, modify, or deny it.
Depreciation Holdback and How to Recover It
Some RCV estimates identify eligible recoverable depreciation. Release requirements and deadlines vary by policy and carrier and may depend on completed work and actual incurred cost. We provide the final invoice, completion photos, and contractor records; the carrier decides whether and when to release funds.
Red Flags: How to Spot Insurance Fraud & Storm Chasers
After every major storm in Fort Wayne, out-of-town contractors flood the area looking for quick insurance money. These "storm chasers" often do substandard work, disappear when problems arise, and sometimes engage in outright fraud. Here's how to protect yourself:
Never sign an Assignment of Benefits (AOB) without understanding it. An AOB transfers your insurance claim rights to the contractor, giving them control over negotiations with your insurance company. While AOBs are legal in Indiana, they remove you from the process and can lead to inflated claims, disputes, and delays. If a contractor asks you to sign one, ask why — and get a second opinion before you do.
Never pay large upfront deposits. Reputable roofing contractors in Indiana don't require large deposits before work begins. If a contractor asks for 50% or more upfront, that's a major red flag. At Big Dog Roofing, we don't collect payment until the work is completed.
Be wary of door-to-door solicitation. Storm chasers typically canvass neighborhoods door-to-door immediately after a storm, offering "free inspections" and pressuring homeowners to sign contracts on the spot. While not all door-knockers are fraudulent, this high-pressure approach should make you cautious. A legitimate contractor will give you time to research, compare, and decide.
Any contractor who offers to "waive your deductible" is committing insurance fraud. Under Indiana law (IC 27-4-1-4.7), it is illegal for a contractor to knowingly waive, absorb, or pay a homeowner's insurance deductible. This isn't a gray area — it's fraud, and both the contractor and the homeowner can face consequences. Your deductible is your contractual responsibility. If someone offers to cover it, walk away.
Verify that your contractor is local, properly insured, and meets the requirements for your jurisdiction. Ask for a permanent local business address — not a P.O. box or hotel room. Check reviews for a history of local work, confirm any registration or license required for the job, and verify general liability and workers' compensation coverage. A legitimate contractor should provide current documentation.
