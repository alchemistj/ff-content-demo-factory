Roof or Siding First? How to Sequence Exterior Work
The right order is driven by shared details, not a universal slogan. Map every roof-to-wall connection, then give one contractor responsibility for each handoff.
By Big Dog Roofing · Published August 26, 2026

When a Fort Wayne home needs both roofing and siding, the shared details decide the schedule. Roof-to-wall flashing may sit behind siding. Kickout flashing directs water into the gutter. Fascia and soffit touch the roof edge. Exterior vents and pipes pass through one or both systems. If the scopes are written separately, the most important seams can fall between contractors.
Roof first is common when tear-off could damage finished siding or when the roofing crew must rebuild flashing. Siding first can make sense when wall sheathing, structural repairs, or access must be corrected before the roof transition is built. Sometimes the best project is phased by elevation. The right answer comes from opening the details on paper before opening the house.
Shared detail	Roofing responsibility	Siding responsibility	Handoff to write down
Roof-to-wall flashing	Underlayment, step flashing, roof covering, and exposed counter-detail as scoped	Wall water-resistive barrier and siding clearance	Who removes and reinstalls siding and verifies the lapped drainage path
Kickout flashing	Roof-end flashing integrated at the eave	Wall and cladding detail below and beside it	Who supplies, installs, and photographs the final kickout
Fascia and soffit	Deck edge, drip edge, and roof-edge assembly	Fascia wrap, soffit, and cladding termination	Which damaged substrate is included and when gutters return
Vents and penetrations	Roof boots, caps, and weathering at roof openings	Wall blocks, flashings, and cladding around wall openings	Who confirms exhaust routing and final seals
Gutters	Roof-edge integration and protection	Fascia substrate and exterior finish	Detach, storage, new installation, and water test
Site access	Tear-off, delivery, trailer, and roof protection	Scaffolding, wall access, and material staging	Which crew moves first and who protects completed work
Map Every Place the Systems Touch
Walk the house with both scopes. Mark dormers, porch roofs, additions, sidewalls, headwalls, chimneys, decks, gutters, fascia, soffit, wall vents, electrical service, hose bibs, and penetrations. Photograph each transition and assign it to one scope.
Building Science Corporation's flashing guidance emphasizes shingle-style layering that sends water out and down. The Vinyl Siding Institute and James Hardie publish product-specific installation guidance. Use the actual cladding and roofing instructions for the assembly being installed.
Choose the Order From the Highest-Risk Detail
If the roof is actively leaking at wall flashing, that transition may drive the schedule. If rotten wall sheathing prevents a secure flashing or cladding attachment, the wall repair may need to start first. If both systems are sound but aged, roof tear-off often comes before finish siding so debris does not strike new work.
Do not let a general rule override a known condition. Write a short sequencing plan by elevation and detail: remove selected siding, repair substrate, install wall membrane, build roof flashing, complete roofing, reinstall cladding with required clearance, then test drainage.
Put the Handoffs Into the Contracts
Each shared component needs one named owner. Who removes siding at step flashing? Who replaces damaged sheathing? Who supplies kickout flashing? Who reinstalls housewrap and flashing tape? Who patches paint? Who detaches and resets gutters? Who photographs concealed work before it is covered?
If one company handles both scopes, ask for the same details. A single logo does not automatically create a complete handoff. The project lead should reconcile change orders and schedule inspections before assemblies are closed.
Inspect Before Concealment and After Rain
Take labeled photographs of wall sheathing, water-resistive barrier, step flashing, headwall details, kickouts, deck edge, fascia, and other concealed components before siding or shingles cover them. Final pictures should show clearances and finished drainage paths.
After completion, observe the transitions during normal rain from a safe location. Look for water behind gutters, staining below roof-wall ends, overflow, loose siding, and interior symptoms. Report a problem promptly while the installation sequence and crew records are still fresh.
How to Pick the Sequence
Find active water and unsafe conditions. Stabilize urgent roof, wall, or structural problems first.
Identify concealed dependencies. List every layer one trade must access before the other covers it.
Protect finished work. Plan tear-off, staging, scaffolding, ladders, and deliveries around completed surfaces.
Assign each transition. Name the contractor, material, timing, photo requirement, and warranty responsibility.
Verify the drainage path. Inspect concealed work, finished clearances, gutters, and post-project water flow.
Northwest-Side Homes Often Have More Than One Construction Era
Older northwest Fort Wayne homes may combine original walls, enclosed porches, additions, and reroofed sections. Newer north-side homes often have more dormers, intersecting roof planes, and short wall sections above roofs. Either pattern creates transition details that deserve a written sequence.
Permit requirements depend on the property, jurisdiction, and scope. If the combined project includes structural repair, changed openings, or more than straightforward replacement, confirm the current public review path before work begins.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is it always better to replace the roof before siding?
No. Roof first is common, but wall damage, structural work, or required access can change the sequence. Shared details should drive the plan.
Who should install step flashing?
The contracts should assign responsibility based on the assembly and local requirements. Roofing and wall layers must be integrated, and concealed work should be photographed.
Can roof work damage new siding?
Tear-off debris, ladders, and staging can affect finished siding. If siding comes first, require a specific protection and repair plan.
Should gutters wait until both projects are done?
Often final gutter placement follows roof-edge and fascia work, but the exact sequence depends on the assembly. Put detach, reset, and testing responsibility in writing.
