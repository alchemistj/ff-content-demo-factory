Fort Wayne Roof Replacement Closeout Checklist
A roof is not finished just because the crew left. Walk the property, resolve the punch list, verify the public record, and keep the documents that explain exactly what was installed.
By Big Dog Roofing · Published August 26, 2026

The last day of a roof replacement moves fast. The trailer leaves, the yard looks clean, and everyone wants the job wrapped up. Slow down long enough to verify the work and records. A proper closeout covers the roof, property condition, approved changes, permit status, payment, products, warranties, and future maintenance.
You do not need to climb the roof. Ask the contractor to provide labeled final photographs of details that cannot be reviewed safely from the ground. Match those photos to the contract and approved change orders, then keep one permanent file for the house.

Compare the Finished Work With the Written Scope
Read the contract line by line: tear-off, underlayment, ice-and-water protection where specified, drip edge, flashing, pipe boots, vents, ridge materials, decking allowance, gutters, disposal, and cleanup. Ask where each changed item was documented.
Final photographs should show roof planes and critical details with enough context to locate them. A tight close-up of metal does not prove which wall or chimney it belongs to. Ask for wide and close views, especially at valleys, walls, chimneys, penetrations, and low-slope transitions.
Resolve the Punch List in Writing
Walk the perimeter during daylight. Check siding, screens, windows, gutters, downspouts, decks, air-conditioning equipment, landscaping, driveway, and access areas. Look in the attic for disturbed belongings or new debris. Magnetic cleanup helps, but homeowners should continue watching high-traffic areas for missed fasteners.
Write every open item with a location, owner, and expected resolution. Photograph the item before and after correction. A verbal promise at the truck is hard to track after the crew moves to another project.
Verify Permit and Inspection Status
Confirm which public authority applied and whether the scope required a permit or inspection. Fort Wayne and Allen County provide an online portal for permit records. Search by the actual address or record number and ask the contractor about any status that is unclear.
Do not assume an issued permit is the same as a completed final status. Keep a copy or screenshot showing the relevant record at closeout, while recognizing the public portal remains the authoritative current record.
Build a File the Next Owner Can Understand
Keep the signed proposal, product and color, approved change orders, deck quantities, final invoice, payment receipt, permit information, inspection photos, manufacturer documents, registration proof, contractor workmanship terms, and maintenance instructions together.
GAF's homeowner guidance recommends retaining proof of purchase and warranty records, and its registration page explains that some enhanced warranties require registration. Requirements vary by product and warranty, so read the actual documents and meet any stated deadlines.
Close Out a Roof Replacement
Review the photo set. Match wide and detailed final images to the contracted roof scope.
Walk the property. Record cleanup, damage, gutters, siding, driveway, attic, and landscaping issues.
Reconcile changes. Match deck work and every added or removed line to an approved change order.
Check the permit path. Verify the applicable record number, inspections, and final status when required.
Archive the closeout package. Save invoice, payment, warranties, registration, product details, photos, and maintenance notes.
Before the Final Sign-Off
Do not review from appearance alone. Use scope, photos, changes, and records together.
Separate open items from future maintenance. A punch-list correction is different from a later care recommendation.
Ask what is still pending. Confirm inspections, registration, specialty work, or ordered components.
Know who to call. Keep the contractor and manufacturer contact process with the warranty file.
Close Out the House You Have, Not a Generic Roof
Fort Wayne's established northwest neighborhoods and newer north-side corridors can produce very different closeout questions. Older homes may have chimneys, additions, multiple deck materials, or prior layers. Newer multi-plane roofs may need more photographs of valleys, wall transitions, and concentrated drainage.
The closeout standard does not change by ZIP code: document the actual work. Use the address-specific permit record, manufacturer documents for the installed product, and photographs from this project.

Frequently Asked Questions
What photos should I receive after roof replacement?
Ask for every roof plane plus labeled wide and close views of valleys, walls, chimneys, penetrations, edges, ventilation, and any deck replacement.
Should I inspect the roof myself?
Review from the ground and accessible interior areas. Do not climb the roof; request contractor photos for details that cannot be seen safely.
When should a roof warranty be registered?
Follow the installed manufacturer's current warranty documents. Some enhanced warranties have registration requirements and deadlines; keep proof of completion.
What if a punch-list item is still open?
Record the location, promised correction, responsible party, and target date in writing. Keep before-and-after photographs when it is resolved.
