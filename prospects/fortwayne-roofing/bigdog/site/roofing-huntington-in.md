Common Roofing Issues for Huntington Homeowners
From century-old Victorians to modern subdivisions, Huntington homes face specific roofing challenges that require local expertise.
Victorian & Historic Roof Complexity
Downtown Huntington is home to some of the most beautiful Victorian-era architecture in northeast Indiana — and some of the most complex rooflines. Steep pitches, multiple dormers, turrets, and intricate valley systems all require a crew that understands how to properly flash, shingle, and waterproof these details. Cutting corners on a historic home leads to leaks that can damage irreplaceable interior woodwork and plaster. We approach every historic Huntington home with the care it deserves.
Wabash River Valley Moisture
Huntington sits at the Forks of the Wabash, and homes along the river corridor experience higher humidity levels that accelerate moss and algae growth on shingles. This biological growth traps moisture against the roofing material and shortens its lifespan. We recommend algae-resistant shingles for Huntington homes near the river and can treat existing growth during inspections to extend the life of your current roof.
Severe Storm Exposure
Huntington County's flat, open terrain can leave roofs exposed during spring and summer storms. Hail and wind damage may affect multiple homes in the same storm path. Big Dog Roofing provides contractor-side inspections, visible-damage documentation, and estimates; coverage and settlement decisions remain with the carrier under the policy.
Aging Shingles on Mid-Century Homes
Many Huntington homes built in the 1960s and 1970s are on their second or even third roof, and those aging shingles are well past their rated lifespan. Curling, cracking, and granule loss are common signs we see during inspections. Rather than patching an old roof that's reached end-of-life, a full replacement with modern architectural shingles provides better wind resistance, impact protection, and curb appeal — often at a lower long-term cost than repeated repairs.
