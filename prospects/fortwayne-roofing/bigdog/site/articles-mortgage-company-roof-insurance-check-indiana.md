Why Your Roof Insurance Check Includes the Mortgage Company
The lender is named because it has a financial interest in the property. Your servicer may endorse the check or control repair funds and release them as work progresses.

If you have a mortgage, a roof insurance settlement check is often payable to both you and the mortgage servicer or lender. The lender's name protects its interest in the house securing the loan. Depending on the servicer, claim amount, loan status, and repair plan, it may simply endorse the check or deposit the funds into a loss-draft account and release money in stages.
Call the servicer's insurance-loss or loss-draft department before signing or mailing the check. Ask for its written package and exact submission channel. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that joint checks and staged releases are common, but your servicer's current process controls your file.

Why the Mortgage Servicer Is a Payee
Your mortgage gives the lender a security interest in the property. Major unrepaired damage can reduce that security. Joint payment gives the servicer a way to monitor whether insurance proceeds repair the house instead of being used elsewhere. This is separate from your deductible and separate from whether the carrier approved the roof scope.
The insurance company determines the settlement under the policy. The mortgage servicer handles the proceeds under the loan and servicing rules. The roofing contractor performs the repair. Keep those roles separate when a delay occurs so you call the party that can actually solve it.
Open the Loss-Draft File Early
Do not wait until the crew needs a material deposit. Servicers may request the adjuster estimate, signed contractor agreement, contractor insurance, tax form, license information, permits, homeowner forms, and the original check. Requirements vary. Ask whether documents can be uploaded and whether an original check must be mailed.
Use tracked delivery for original checks and keep copies of every front, back, endorsement, and enclosure. Record the case or claim number the servicer assigns. When calling, ask which items are received, which are accepted, and which remain missing. 'We sent it' and 'the file is complete' are different statuses.
How Draws and Inspections Work
A servicer may release an initial amount, then use progress inspections before later draws. The inspector usually verifies repair progress for the servicer. The inspection is not the same as the contractor's quality control or the local permit inspection. Ask what percentage or milestone triggers a draw and who orders the visit.
Fannie Mae's current insured-loss servicing guidance shows why loan status, repair intent, proceeds, and progress can change disbursement. Not every mortgage follows that guide, so use it as background rather than a promise about your servicer.
Coordinate Contractor Payment Without Guessing
Give the roofer the servicer's written draw schedule before agreeing to payment dates. A contract that requires full payment before the lender can release funds creates avoidable stress. The contractor still needs a reasonable material and labor schedule, while the homeowner needs enough time for the servicer's process.
If a check is made payable to the homeowner, servicer, and contractor, ask how every endorsement occurs. Do not sign a blank check or mail an endorsed original to an unverified address. Confirm the official address or upload route through the servicer's statement, website, or phone number you independently know.
How to Process a Joint Roof Insurance Check
Call the loss-draft department Use a verified servicer contact and request the complete written requirements for your loan and claim.
Copy and secure the check Keep images and delivery records; follow the servicer's exact endorsement and original-check instructions.
Submit the contractor package Provide the carrier estimate, signed contract, contractor documents, homeowner forms, and other required items.
Align the draw schedule Match material deposits, work milestones, inspections, and contractor payment dates in writing.
Request progress releases Submit draw forms, invoices, photos, and inspections when the servicer's milestone is met.
Close the loss-draft file Send the final invoice and completion documents and confirm that all remaining funds are released or explained.
Questions for the Loss-Draft Department
Is this an endorsement or monitored claim? Ask whether the servicer will sign and return the check or hold funds for draws.
Which documents are mandatory? Request one current checklist tied to your loan and loss file.
Who must endorse each payment? Confirm homeowner, servicer, and contractor payees before mailing anything.
What triggers each release? Get the milestone, inspection, invoice, and processing sequence in writing.
How is a delay escalated? Ask for the loss-draft case number, status channel, supervisor route, and missing-item explanation.
What Changes in Fort Wayne and Northeast Indiana
Indiana roof projects can move quickly after a hail or wind event, while a mortgage loss-draft process may not move at contractor speed. Open the file as soon as the joint check or settlement notice arrives. Do not schedule tear-off around money the servicer has not yet agreed to release.
Permit and inspection paperwork can serve different audiences. Allen County may inspect the roof under the permit process described in our permit guide. The mortgage servicer may order a separate progress inspection. Ask whether it will accept local completion records or still requires its own visit.

Mistakes That Make the Problem Harder
Do not sign or mail an original check before verifying the servicer's instructions.
Do not promise contractor payment dates based on a guessed lender timeline.
Do not confuse recoverable depreciation with mortgage-servicer release of funds.
Do not send sensitive loan documents through an address or link you have not independently verified.
Frequently Asked Questions
Why is my mortgage company on the insurance check?
It has a financial interest in the property securing the mortgage. Joint payment lets the servicer monitor that insurance proceeds are used for repairs.
How long will the mortgage company hold the roof money?
There is no universal timeline. It depends on the servicer, file completeness, check handling, loan and claim conditions, inspections, and draw rules. Ask for current written status and missing items.
Can my contractor endorse the check?
Only if the contractor is named as a payee and follows the required endorsement process. Do not add or remove payees yourself; ask the issuer and servicer how the check must be handled.
Is the mortgage draw the same as recoverable depreciation?
No. Recoverable depreciation is a carrier claim payment issue. A mortgage draw is the servicer's release of insurance proceeds it controls. Both can occur on the same project but require different documents.
