Most homeowners assume a roofing warranty means everything is covered. Unfortunately, that’s not how it works—especially in Indiana, where our mix of storms, temperature swings, and humidity can stress a roofing system faster than expected.
Roofing warranties can be incredibly valuable, but only when you understand what they actually protect… and what they don’t. This guide breaks down the real truth about roofing warranties so you can avoid expensive surprises down the road.
The Two Types of Roofing Warranties in Indiana
There are only two main categories of warranties you’ll deal with as a homeowner:
1. Manufacturer Warranty (Covers the Material)
This warranty is provided by the shingle or roofing product manufacturer. It typically protects against defects in the shingles themselves—NOT installation errors or storm damage.
Manufacturer warranties often include:
Protection against premature shingle deterioration
Coverage for defects in asphalt, granules, or sealing
Limited labor coverage during early years (sometimes)
Pro-rated protection over time
Manufacturer shingle warranties use product-specific coverage periods and exclusions, so the fine print matters. Some terms cover replacement materials without covering every labor cost. Compare the exact manufacturer document with the workmanship terms on our warranty page.
2. Workmanship Warranty (Covers the Installation)
This comes directly from the roofing company that installed your roof. A workmanship warranty protects you from mistakes made during installation, such as:
Improper nailing
Bad flashing work
Poor ventilation setup
Incorrect shingle alignment
Leaks caused by installation errors

What Roofing Warranties Usually Don’t Cover in Indiana
This is where most homeowners get caught off guard. Roofing warranties—manufacturer OR workmanship—typically do not cover:
Storm Damage (Hail, Wind, Tornadoes)
Any storm-related roof issues require a home insurance claim, not a warranty. For guidance on storm claims, visit:
https://bigdogroofing.org/storm-damage-and-insurance/
Leaks Caused by Failed Ventilation
If the attic wasn’t ventilated properly—leading to heat or moisture buildup—warranties often deny coverage.
Improper Maintenance or Neglect
Skipping annual inspections or ignoring small leaks can void parts of your warranty.
Damage From Other Trades
Satellite installers, electricians, gutter companies, and HVAC technicians often void warranties when they penetrate the roof.
Homeowner Modifications
Solar panel additions or DIY repairs can immediately cancel coverage.
The Biggest “Surprise” Homeowners Face: Pro-Rated Coverage
Some roofing warranties change value over time. The exact schedule is controlled by the written manufacturer document, but common structures include:
An initial period with broader product coverage
A later period in which coverage becomes pro-rated
Different rules for materials, labor, disposal, and transfer
This is why homeowners should read the current warranty document instead of relying on a broad marketing label.

What Is Covered More Often Than People Think
Strong roofing systems can come with useful protection when the complete system is installed according to the product instructions and the written terms are followed.
Here’s what’s often covered:
Manufacturer defects (rare, but covered)
Early granule loss due to faulty production
Shingle blistering from material defects
Adhesion failures (if not caused by weather)
Manufacturing inconsistencies affecting shingle life

Extended Warranties: Are They Worth It in Indiana?
Manufacturers may offer different product-warranty options for a qualifying full system, such as shingles, underlayment, starter strip, and ridge cap. Availability depends on the manufacturer's current written terms and installer eligibility.
These warranties can include:
A stated period of non-prorated protection where the document provides it
Labor and material coverage only where expressly written
Transfer provisions only where the product terms permit them
Sealing-failure protection only under the stated conditions
Whether a broader option is worth it depends on its price, exclusions, registration requirements, and the roof system being installed. Ask for the exact document and compare it with our 15-year craftsmanship warranty before deciding.

Frequently Asked Questions
What should a written estimate say about roofing warranty coverage in Indiana?
It should identify the exact scope, materials, tear-off or overlay plan, flashing and ventilation work, cleanup, permit responsibility, payment terms, and applicable warranty terms in plain language.
How can I verify the contractor's answer about roofing warranty coverage in Indiana?
Ask for the answer in writing, compare it with the contract and permit requirements already documented on this site, and verify licensing or permit records with the proper local authority when relevant.
Is the lowest roofing bid usually the best value?
Not automatically. A lower bid can be legitimate, but compare line items. Missing underlayment, flashing, ventilation, disposal, decking allowances, or permit work can make a cheap number expensive later.
When is a second roofing opinion useful?
Get another opinion when the scope is vague, the contractor pressures you to sign, the explanation conflicts with local requirements, or two estimates recommend completely different work.
