Plainfield's housing is young, and its roofs are not
Plainfield is the largest of the Hendricks County towns we serve. FEMA's structure inventory counts about 9,500 single-family homes, and the typical one was built around 1998. Many of those are two-story homes on the original roof, or on a replacement after a storm. Either way, a lot of Plainfield roofs are approaching the same decision at the same time.
Ventilation is part of the roof
On two-story homes from this period, the attic is often under-ventilated, and that shortens the life of whatever roof sits on top of it. Summer heat bakes the underside of the decking, and winter moisture condenses on it. A new roof of any kind should come with a look at intake and exhaust ventilation. Metal does not fix a ventilation problem. If anything, a long-life roof makes it more important to get right, because you are not planning to revisit it.
Asphalt is a cycle
Asphalt roofing is reasonable, and on a short timeline it is often the better choice. The issue is repetition. Each replacement tends to cost more than the one before, and every tear-off is hard on the decking. Metal costs more up front. Its value is that you stop the cycle, not that it is cheap.
Here's the tradeoff. You gain a roof you should not have to replace, and you give up the lower price today. What matters more to you here, upfront cost or long-term durability?
Who this is not for
If you plan to sell in the next few years, keep the money. If you want a roof with zero upkeep, no roof offers that. If the issue is one failed boot or flashing on a sound roof, that is a repair, and we do not take repair calls.
