What I see from the ladder in Wells County
Bluffton is a river town surrounded by farm ground, and the roofs split the same way. In town, older two-story houses with steep pitches and porches, and postwar ranches on simple gables. Outside town, farmsteads — a house, a machine shed, a barn, and usually at least one already wearing screw-down panel.
The weather is freeze and thaw in winter and wind the rest of the year. There is not much between a roof here and a west wind, and on asphalt that shows up as lifted tabs on the windward slope that reseal poorly and lift again. Ice at the eaves is a normal condition. The membrane under the panel and the ventilation in the attic prevent the damage; the panel just makes the consequences smaller.
The barn and the house
Here is the tradeoff on screw-down panel. It is the least expensive metal roof and it goes on fast. Every screw has a rubber washer under it, and those wear out around year ten to twelve. That is a fair trade on the machine shed. On the house you plan to keep, I steer people to a concealed-fastener system — a mechanically seamed standing seam on the long simple runs and the low-slope porch sections, or an interlocking metal shingle on a steeper roof where you want it to read as shingles from the road. We do not install exposed fastener under 4/12, on anything.
On the older in-town houses, the job is mostly flashing and the deck underneath, which is often plank that has been wet somewhere. That is a line item, and it belongs on the quote before the tear-off, not after.
When I tell Bluffton homeowners not to do this
If you expect to sell within a few years, good asphalt is the rational choice, and I will say so on the phone. If one flashing or one pipe boot is leaking on a roof with life left in it, that is a repair — we do not take repair calls, and a repair contractor is the right answer. If you want shingles again, you want a different contractor; we install metal only.
Where we are coming from
Bluffton is about 35 minutes south of our Fort Wayne office, and it is regular territory. Metal makes sense here when you plan to stay long enough that you would otherwise be buying the roof again. Now you have the context to decide.
