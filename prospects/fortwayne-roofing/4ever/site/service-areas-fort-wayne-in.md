What a Fort Wayne winter actually does to a roof
We are headquartered here, so most of what I know about roof failure I learned on Allen County houses. Northeast Indiana freezes and thaws over and over between December and March, with the tail end of the lake-effect snow on top. Snow melts against a warm deck, runs to the cold overhang, refreezes, and backs water up under the shingles. That is an ice dam, and it is the single most common source of the interior water stains I get called about in this city.
Metal does not make ice dams impossible. Anyone who tells you it does is selling. What it changes is what happens when one forms: a mechanically seamed standing seam roof has no horizontal seams and no exposed fasteners for backed-up water to find. On an asphalt roof, that same water only has to get past a nail head.
The housing stock here
A lot of Fort Wayne is postwar ranch and story-and-a-half homes with simple gable roofs — straightforward to re-roof, and a good fit for standing seam because we roll-form panels on site to the full length of the run. The older neighborhoods near downtown and in West Central are steeper, with dormers, valleys, and additions tied in at different times. Those roofs need more flashing detail, and flashing is where the money and the leaks both are. That is an argument for a concealed-fastener system, not against it — but the quote should be higher than a simple gable, and you should be suspicious of one that is not.
When I tell Fort Wayne homeowners not to do this
If you are planning to sell in the next few years, a metal roof will not return what it costs. Buyers in this market do not reliably pay a premium for it. Asphalt is the rational choice for a short hold, and I will say so. If your roof has one bad detail and the rest of the field is sound, that is a repair, and we do not take repair calls — a repair contractor is the right answer.
Where we are coming from
Fort Wayne is home base, and most of this city is a short drive from the office. Where metal earns its cost here is the long hold: you plan to stay, you have already replaced asphalt once, and you would rather pay once than repeat the cycle. Now you have the context to decide.
