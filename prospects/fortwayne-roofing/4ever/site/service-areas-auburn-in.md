Auburn roofs, and the bids that describe them
Auburn has an older core of well-kept houses near downtown — steep roofs, dormers, porches tied in at a lower pitch — and newer subdivisions with simple hips and gables, with farm ground and screw-down barns around it. Most of the confusion I run into here is not about the houses. It is about bids. "Metal roof" is not one product. It is four or five assemblies that behave, cost and fail differently, and homeowners in Auburn often end up comparing estimates that are not describing the same thing.
Start with how the seam is formed
The seam is the joint between panels, and it decides how well the roof keeps water out. A mechanically folded standing seam is the most water-resistant joint we install and the only one comfortable on very low slope; panels are roll-formed on site to the full length of the roof, so there are no horizontal seams. A four-side interlocking metal shingle locks on every edge and wants at least a 3/12 pitch. A snap-lock panel snaps together instead of being folded — fine on steep, uncomplicated roofs; it manages water rather than locking it out. An exposed-fastener panel holds water back with a rubber washer under each screw head.
That order is a ranking of how much the joint depends on something wearing out, not of manufacturing quality.
Pitch decides more than taste does
We do not install exposed fastener under 4/12. Interlocking shingle wants 3/12 or better. Mechanically seamed standing seam handles genuinely low slope, and there is a variant built for the near-flat porch roofs and additions around DeKalb County. If a contractor quotes you a system your pitch does not support, that is the end of the conversation with that contractor.
When I tell Auburn homeowners not to do this
If you are moving in the next few years, the payback window does not close in time. If one flashing is leaking on an otherwise sound roof, that is a repair — we do not take repair calls, and nobody should convert a repair into a replacement. If the lowest cost this year is the deciding factor, asphalt wins that comparison and I will say so.
