What it is
A modular steel shingle — usually stamped to look like slate or wood shake — that interlocks with its neighbors on all four sides and fastens under the next course. We call our version the 4Ever Fortress system. It is installed as a true system, not as overlapping pieces, which is the whole point: it limits water getting in, rather than planning for it after it does.
What it does well
Gives you a roof that does not read as "metal" from the street, with the durability of painted steel. Because the shingles lock on four sides, it relies far less on the underlayment to keep the house dry than stone-coated or overlapping systems do. It handles complex roofs with a lot of hips and valleys gracefully.
Where it commonly fails
Installed below its minimum pitch. Most interlocking shingles want 3/12 or steeper, and I would not go lower.
Installed by a crew that has not done it before. The locking sequence is unforgiving; if a course is started wrong, the problem repeats across the roof.
Poorly designed products. Only a few manufacturers make an interlocking shingle with good water management. We install EDCO's because of how it is built, not because of the name.
The tradeoffs
Higher upfront cost than exposed-fastener panels — in the same range as mechanically seamed standing seam. Skilled installation is required and there are not many crews with real experience. Limited manufacturers.
Who it fits
Homeowners who want metal performance and a shingle appearance, who have a roof at or above 3/12, and who want the least reliance on what is under the metal.
Who it does not fit
Anyone comparing on price alone, or anyone with a roof below the pitch limit. If you like the vertical-seam look, standing seam is the simpler choice.
This is usually where the decision becomes clear.
