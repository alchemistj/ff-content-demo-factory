Fishers roofs, and the second asphalt roof
Most of Fishers was built from the 1990s on, so a lot of the roofs I look at here are coming off their first builder-grade asphalt or are partway into their second. Hip and gable, decking sound, framing modern. The decision here is mostly financial rather than structural.
The places it gets more involved are the larger homes out toward Geist, where rooflines pick up dormers and valleys, and any house with a low-slope porch or sunroom roof tied into the main pitch. Low slope is where the system gets chosen for you.
Hail, wind, and what metal does with them
Hamilton County catches spring storms that put enough hail down to total an asphalt roof, and plenty of Fishers roofs have been through a claim already. Asphalt bruises, sheds granules, and the failure clock starts. Metal stays watertight after the storm; a big stone can leave a cosmetic dent, especially on a broad flat pan. Metal shingle and textured profiles hide that better. None of that is hail-proof. Ask your insurance carrier how they treat metal at renewal; the answer varies by company.
Which system fits which Fishers house
On a simple gable with a long run, mechanically seamed standing seam — panels roll-formed on site to the full length, no exposed fasteners — is hard to beat, and it is the only one I trust on a low-slope addition. In an HOA with traditional guidelines, the four-side interlocking metal shingle is the conversation; it looks like a shingle roof from the curb and wants 3/12 or steeper. Exposed-fastener panel is a fine outbuilding roof and the wrong roof for a house you plan to keep: the washers under every screw age and need attention around year ten to twelve. Stone-coated I am cautious about, because it manages water after it gets in.
Most of Fishers is covenanted. Get color and profile approved in writing before material is ordered; we supply the documentation.
Who should not be calling us from Fishers
If you expect to move in a few years, put the insurance money toward good asphalt and keep the difference. One leaking boot or flashing on a sound roof is a repair, and we do not take repair calls. If you want shingles again, you want a different contractor.
