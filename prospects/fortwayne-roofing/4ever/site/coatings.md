Why the paint matters as much as the panel
A metal roof is steel, a metallic coating on the steel, a primer, and a top coat — applied at the mill on the coil before the panel is ever formed. That top coat is what you see for the next forty years. Two roofs with identical panels and very different coatings will look nothing alike at year twenty.
PVDF vs. SMP
PVDF — fluoropolymer resin, sold under names like Kynar 500 and Hylar 5000, and made into coil coatings by Sherwin-Williams (Fluropon) and AkzoNobel (Trinar). It holds color and gloss the longest and chalks the least. On our jobs it shows up on interlocking metal shingles, and it can be had on 24-gauge standing seam steel if you ask for it — it is not what most houses get. It is a softer film, which matters for how panels are handled, and it costs more per square.
SMP — silicone-modified polyester, such as Sherwin-Williams WeatherXL or AkzoNobel Ceram-A-Star. Harder, scratch-resistant, less expensive, and it has improved a lot. This is what goes on most of the homes we do, on both standing seam and exposed fastener. It still fades and chalks sooner than PVDF, especially in darker colors and on south and west slopes — but the gap is not what it used to be, and across a whole roof the difference in cost is real.
What fails, and how it looks
Chalking — the resin breaks down at the surface and leaves a white powder. Every finish does this eventually; the question is when.
Fading — color shift from UV. Reds and dark bronzes go first on lesser coatings.
Edge creep and scratches — exposed steel at cuts and scratches can rust if the metallic layer under the paint is thin or damaged.
The finish warranty will tell you the manufacturer's own expectation. A 40-year film-integrity warranty with 30-year chalk-and-fade numbers is the kind of language you want to see on a PVDF system; be careful reading a "40-year" headline that turns out to cover only peeling.
Where the coatings on this site come from
The panels we install carry one of two coil coatings: AkzoNobel Ceram-A-Star 1050 or Sherwin-Williams WeatherXL. Both are silicone-modified polyesters, and we use both. The product pages under this section are those paint systems — what they are made of, their color cards and their warranties — so you can read the same documents we do.
