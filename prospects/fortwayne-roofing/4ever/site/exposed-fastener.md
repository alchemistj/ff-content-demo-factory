What it is
Ribbed steel panels screwed straight through the face into the deck or purlins, with a rubber-washered screw sealing each hole. We call ours the 4Ever Guard system. It was adapted from wall and siding use because it is fast and inexpensive, and it has a long service history on barns, shops and plenty of houses.
What it does well
Lowest upfront cost of any metal roof. Simple, proven construction. No seaming equipment and a faster install, which is part of why it costs less. A practical option when affordability is the main priority and the roof is steep enough.
Where it commonly fails
The screws. Every fastener sits in the sun, snow and ice, and every panel expands and contracts around it, working the hole and aging the washer. The fasteners and washers wear out long before the panel does. Most people don't realize that a screw-down roof is a maintenance item: it wants an inspection every year, and the fasteners typically need replacing in the 10-to-12-year range. Claims about special or protected fasteners do not change the physics — in real use I have yet to find an exposed fastener design that is impervious to the elements.
Pitch matters more here than on any other system. Most manufacturers limit warranties below 3/12. We do not install it below 4/12.
The tradeoff
You pay less now and you take on a maintenance schedule. That is a fair trade for some homeowners and buildings. It is not a fair trade for someone who wants to stop thinking about the roof.
Who it fits
Outbuildings, shops, agricultural and some commercial roofs. Houses where budget and speed are the top priorities, the pitch is 4/12 or better, and the owner will actually do the maintenance.
Who it does not fit
Anyone whose reason for choosing metal is "never again." A low-pitched roof. Anyone who will not get up there — or pay someone to — every year.
If this is what you are expecting from a metal roof, standing seam is the honest answer, and it costs more. Now you have the context to decide.

What we install
We do not carry a branded exposed-fastener product line on this site, so there are no product pages here. On this system what decides the roof is the gauge, the coating and the fastener — not the name stamped on the profile.
The coating is the same one we use on standing seam: AkzoNobel Ceram-A-Star 1050 or Sherwin-Williams WeatherXL, both silicone-modified polyesters applied to the coil at the mill. The paint systems page covers what that means over forty years.
If you want a metal roof with no exposed fasteners in the weather at all, standing seam and interlocking shingles are the two systems to read next.
