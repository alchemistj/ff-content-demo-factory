Why we install their products
Tilcor makes stone-coated steel panels — a steel base with a granule finish, stamped to read as tile, shake or shingle. People choose them for the look, and I install them for people who have looked at the tradeoff and want that look. I am careful about where I recommend them, and I would rather you hear why from me than from a competitor.
A stone-coated panel overlaps and uses channels underneath to move water that gets past the surface. It is designed to manage water after entry, which means the roof leans on its underlayment and its flashing details for the long term. That is more places for water to find a path over forty years than a system that stops it at the surface. Pitch has to be 3/12 or steeper. The granule finish also weathers differently than a painted PVDF panel.
What Tilcor does well: the panels are well made from New Zealand-grade steel, the CF panels fasten on the concealed edge, the fire and impact ratings are good, and the warranty document is one of the clearer ones. If the tile or shake look is the reason you are choosing metal at all, this is the honest option for it.
