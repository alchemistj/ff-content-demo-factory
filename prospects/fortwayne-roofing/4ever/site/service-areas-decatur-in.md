Why roofs fail in Adams County
Roofs in Decatur do not fail because of one dramatic event. They fail from repetition — the same freeze, the same thaw, the same summer heat, over and over, until a material that was never designed for that gives up.
Three things wear a roof out here. Freeze and thaw at the eaves, backing water up under the shingles. Thermal cycling in the field, until the asphalt granules let go. And wind with a clean run across open ground, lifting shingles at the tab so they reseal poorly and lift more easily the next time.
What metal changes, and what it does not
Metal does not lose granules, and there is no tab for the wind to catch. What it does not change: ice still forms at a cold eave. Anyone who tells you a metal roof makes ice dams impossible is overselling. Ask instead what is going under the panel at the eaves and how the attic is vented.
The houses and the barns
Decatur has an older core with steep roofs and additions, newer ranches, and a lot of farm ground where screw-down panel on barns is everywhere. That panel is a fair trade on a barn. On the house you plan to keep, the rubber washers under those screws wear out around year ten to twelve, and I steer people to a concealed-fastener system: mechanically seamed standing seam on long runs and low-slope sections, an interlocking metal shingle on a steeper roof where you want the shingle look. We do not install exposed fastener under 4/12. Stone-coated steel I am cautious about for a house, because it manages water after it gets in.
When I tell Decatur homeowners not to do this
If you are moving in the next few years, the payback window does not close in time. If one flashing is leaking on an otherwise sound roof, that is a repair — we do not take repair calls, and nobody should convert a repair into a replacement. If the lowest cost this year is the deciding factor, asphalt wins that comparison and I will say so.
