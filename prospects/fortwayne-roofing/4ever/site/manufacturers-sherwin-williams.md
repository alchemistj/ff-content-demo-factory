Why we install their products
Sherwin-Williams Coil Coatings is one of two paint suppliers behind the panels we install. AkzoNobel is the other. Both make the finish at the mill, on the coil, before a panel is ever formed — which is why the coating is a decision you make once and then live with for forty years.
What we buy from them is WeatherXL, their silicone-modified polyester. It goes on exposed-fastener and standing seam panels, alongside AkzoNobel's Ceram-A-Star 1050, and I am comfortable with either one. SMP is a step down from a PVDF fluoropolymer on paper. In practice the gap has narrowed a lot, and it costs a good deal less across a whole roof.
Here is where people misread this. PVDF holds color and gloss the longest, and it is worth understanding — but it is not what sits on most of the roofs we install. The one system where we routinely use a PVDF finish is an interlocking metal shingle, and only on certain lines. On standing seam, Kynar can be had on 24-gauge steel if you ask for it; it is uncommon on houses and I would rather you did not walk in expecting it.
The documents on this page are Sherwin-Williams' own. If a quote names a coating, look it up before you compare the numbers.
