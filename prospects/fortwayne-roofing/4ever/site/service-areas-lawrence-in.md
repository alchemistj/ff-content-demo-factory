Lawrence has every generation of house in it
Lawrence, on the northeast side of Indianapolis, is one of the more mixed areas we work in. There are mid-century ranches near the older core, newer subdivisions out toward the county line, and the redevelopment around Fort Benjamin Harrison. FEMA's structure inventory counts close to 14,000 single-family homes in the city, and about half of the housing was built between 1990 and 2009.
That mix means there is no single Lawrence answer. The roof on a 1960s ranch and the roof on a 2005 two-story are different conversations.
Pitch decides more than people expect
A lot of the older Lawrence ranches have low-slope roofs. That matters, because every metal system has a minimum pitch. Metal shingles need at least a 3/12 pitch. We will not install exposed-fastener panels under 4/12. Standing seam handles lower slopes better, but the lower the pitch, the less forgiving the details become.
This is where homeowners get tripped up. A system that looks right in a brochure can be the wrong system for a shallow roof, and a wrong system fails even when the material is good.
The asphalt cycle is the real cost
Many of these homes are on their second or third asphalt roof. Each replacement usually costs more than the last, and the decking takes some damage every time the roof comes off. Metal costs more up front. The case for it is that you stop the cycle, not that it is cheap.
Who should not do this in Lawrence
If you are selling within a few years, keep the money. If the price is the main question, metal will disappoint you. If the leak is one flashing on a sound roof, that is a repair, and we do not take repair calls.
If you plan to stay, our Noblesville office serves Lawrence. Now you have enough information to decide.
