Greenwood catches more hail than most of our area
Greenwood is south of Indianapolis in Johnson County, on the far side of the city from our Noblesville office. FEMA's structure inventory counts about 18,000 single-family homes, and roughly 44% of the housing was built between 1990 and 2009. Weather radar recorded hail of an inch or larger on four separate days around Greenwood between 2021 and 2026, more than most of the towns we serve.
So the conversation here usually starts with hail and insurance.
Metal does not make a roof hail-proof
Here is the honest version. Large hail can dent a metal panel. Flat standing seam pans show it most; metal shingles and textured profiles hide it better. What metal does is stay watertight after the storm. The dent is cosmetic. Hail-bruised asphalt is different: it loses granules, and the clock on that roof starts running faster.
If you are coming off a claim, ask your carrier how they treat metal at renewal before you decide. Some carriers favor it and some apply cosmetic-damage exclusions, and that changes the math.
The first roof is coming due
A lot of Greenwood homes from the 1990s and early 2000s are still on the builder's original asphalt, or on the replacement that followed a storm. That is the point where homeowners ask whether to do asphalt once more or stop the cycle. Metal costs more up front. It makes sense when you plan to stay.
When I tell Greenwood homeowners not to do this
If you are moving in a few years, put the insurance money toward good asphalt. If you need zero maintenance, no roof will give you that. If the damage is one area on an otherwise sound roof, that is a repair, and we do not take repair work.
