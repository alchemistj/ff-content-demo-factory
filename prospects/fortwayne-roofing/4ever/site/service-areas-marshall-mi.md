Marshall's roofs are older than most we see
Marshall is known for its historic homes, and their roofs are the most involved geometry we work on: steep pitches, dormers, intersecting gables, valleys, chimneys, porches added over a century at different slopes. On a roof like that the field is the easy part. Every valley and sidewall is a place water can get in, and every one has to be formed in metal, not solved with sealant. Flashing is where the money and the leaks both are, and the quote should reflect that.
Here is the tradeoff. A concealed-fastener system is the right answer on these houses — an interlocking metal shingle keeps a period look and locks on all four sides, and standing seam handles the low-slope porch roof a shingle should not be on. But a roof with that many details is less forgiving of a bad install than a simple gable is, and the installer matters more than the material. Ask who certifies the crew and what the warranty covers if the problem is the workmanship.
Decks on old houses
On homes of this age I look at the deck first. Plank decking with wide gaps is normal and changes the underlayment and fastening approach, and decking that has been wet before is common after a few ice-dam winters. Neither is a dealbreaker. Both are a line item, and I would rather quote it than find it mid-tear-off.
Winter in Calhoun County
Marshall gets some lake-effect and a real freeze-thaw season. Melt, refreeze, water backing up at the eave — the membrane at the eave and the attic ventilation are what prevent the damage, and old houses with finished attics need that conversation. Metal changes what backed-up water can get into. It does not fix a warm attic.
Outside town, screw-down panel on the barn is right for the barn — the washers wear out around year ten to twelve. On the house, concealed fasteners, and never exposed fastener below 4/12.
Who should not be calling us
Selling in a few years. One leaking detail on a sound roof — a repair contractor; we do not take repair calls. Wants shingles again. Lowest number this year.
