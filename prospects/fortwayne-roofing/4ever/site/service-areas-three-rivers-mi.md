Wet snow decides a lot here
Three Rivers is squarely in the lake-effect belt off Lake Michigan. Snow comes in heavy and wet, it sits on anything low-slope, and the melt-and-refreeze at the eave happens more often than anywhere on the Indiana side of our map. The roofs that give trouble are the low-slope additions and porch roofs where wet snow sits, melts, and freezes again at the edge.
For those sections, I use a mechanically seamed panel rated for that pitch rather than stretching a steeper-roof system past its minimum. If someone quotes a standard panel on a near-flat porch roof, ask what that panel's minimum slope is. Snap-lock standing seam and exposed fastener both belong on roofs where water leaves quickly. Here, a lot of it does not.
The houses
Three Rivers has older housing in town — two-story homes with real pitch, valleys, dormers, and porches tied in at a different slope. The field is easy on those; the valleys and sidewalls are the job. An interlocking metal shingle keeps the look of an older street and locks on all four sides, which matters in wind. Standing seam is the answer on the long simple runs and the low-slope bits.
Outside town, farms with screw-down barns and sheds. That panel is right for those buildings and wrong for the house you plan to keep — the rubber washer under every screw wears out around year ten to twelve, and skipping that is how a fifty-year roof leaks in year fifteen. We do not install exposed fastener below a 4/12 pitch.
Metal does not make ice dams impossible. It changes what backed-up water can get into; the membrane at the eave and the attic ventilation prevent the damage.
When I tell Three Rivers homeowners not to do this
Selling in a few years — asphalt. One leaking detail on a sound roof — a repair contractor; we do not take repair calls. Wants shingles again — a different contractor. Lowest number this year — metal loses that comparison and I will not pretend otherwise.

Three Rivers is about an hour and a half from our Fort Wayne office, which puts it at the edge of where we regularly work. We are licensed and in good standing in Michigan. If you would rather have a crew from Kalamazoo or closer, that is fair. The system, the certification, and the warranty that transfers with the house are the same either way.
