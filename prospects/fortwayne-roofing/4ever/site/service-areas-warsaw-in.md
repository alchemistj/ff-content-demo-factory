Deciding between another asphalt roof and metal in Warsaw
Most Warsaw homeowners who call us are not shopping for metal specifically. They are looking at a shingle roof on its second or third go-round and asking whether there is a way off that treadmill. That is the right question, and the answer depends on how long you plan to stay.
If you expect to sell within a few years, asphalt is usually the rational choice, and I will tell you that on the phone. Metal earns its higher upfront cost over decades, not over a listing photo. If you plan to be in the house ten years or more, you are no longer comparing one roof against one roof, but against the next asphalt roof and the one after that, each costing more than the last.
What the lakes do to roofs here
Two things I look for on a Kosciusko County site visit. Homes on open water take wind with a longer run-up than homes in town, and wind is where fastener choice shows itself — a concealed-clip standing seam roof holds every panel without a screw head exposed to the weather, which is worth more on a shoreline lot than on a sheltered street. Heavily wooded lots are the opposite problem: shade holds moisture, which chews through asphalt granules but is mostly a cleaning question on a coated metal surface.
The house and the outbuilding
A screw-down panel on a detached garage or pole barn is a fair trade — lower cost, and a fastener pass around year ten to twelve. On the house you plan to keep, a concealed-fastener system is the better answer, and we will not put exposed fastener on anything under 4/12. An interlocking metal shingle fits the older, steeper in-town houses where you want a shingle look; standing seam fits long simple runs and the low-slope sections over porches and additions.
One bad flashing or one pipe boot on a roof with life left in it is a repair, and we do not take repair calls — a repair contractor is the right answer.
Where we are coming from
Warsaw is about an hour from our Fort Wayne office, and it is territory we are in regularly rather than a special trip. If what you take away from a visit is that one more asphalt roof fits your situation better, that is a fine outcome. The point is to decide once, with the whole picture in front of you.
