Lake Erie decides a lot of this
Toledo sits at the west end of Lake Erie, and the weather that comes off it is the reason roofs here age the way they do. Wet, heavy snow that sits rather than blows off. Freeze-thaw cycling through most of the winter. Ice at the eaves on any house with a warm attic. None of that is exotic — it is just harder on a roof than fifty miles inland.
What it means practically: the eave detail matters more here than the panel does. Ice damming is an attic problem before it is a roofing problem, so we look at insulation and ventilation before we talk about steel, and on a re-roof that is the one chance anyone gets to fix it with the deck open.
For the panel itself, this is a place I want a mechanically seamed standing seam rather than a snap-lock one. A seam that is folded closed keeps standing water and ice out of the seam; a snapped seam relies on the water moving off quickly, which is exactly what does not happen when snow sits for a week.
The housing here
A lot of the Toledo-area stock is pre-war through 1960s, with steeper roofs and real geometry — good candidates for an interlocking metal shingle if you want the roof to look conventional, and good candidates for standing seam if you do not mind vertical lines. Out in the townships there are farm buildings and shops where exposed fastener is the honest answer, above 4/12, with the maintenance that comes with it.
Who should not call us
If you need a repair — one flashing, one boot, one section — we do not take that work, and a service contractor in Lucas County will do it faster and cheaper. If the whole field of a screw-down roof is losing washers, or the asphalt is at the end of its life, that is a replacement conversation and it is ours.
Toledo is served from our Fort Wayne office, about an hour up US-24. Far enough that we schedule it in blocks rather than one house at a time, close enough that we work there regularly.
