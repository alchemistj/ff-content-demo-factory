What northwest Ohio does to a roof
We work the western counties of northwest Ohio out of Fort Wayne — Van Wert, Paulding, Defiance and Williams counties, mostly — and the weather there is the weather of flat, open farm ground, the old Black Swamp. There is nothing between a roof here and a west wind for miles.
On the ladder, that shows up as lifted shingle tabs on the windward slope, resealed poorly and lifted again. Winter is freeze and thaw more than deep cold, and on asphalt the water that backs up at the eave only has to get past a nail head. The counties along the Michigan line get a bit more snow than Fort Wayne; the Lake Erie weather Toledo deals with is not really this part of the state's problem.
What that means for system choice
Wind is where fastener choice shows itself. On an exposed-fastener panel the wind load is carried by screws and their rubber washers, and those washers are a wear item due attention around year ten to twelve. On a barn that is a reasonable trade. On a house taking wind across open fields, it is a shorter answer than most people want, and we will not install it under 4/12 anywhere.
A mechanically seamed standing seam roof holds the panels with concealed clips that let the metal expand and contract, with no fastener head exposed to the weather at all. On this side of the state line that difference earns its cost more often than not. For genuinely low-slope porch roofs and additions we use a panel rated for that pitch rather than stretching a standard profile past its minimum. An interlocking metal shingle fits the older in-town houses where you want a traditional look at 3/12 or steeper.
The houses
The towns here are older two-story homes near a courthouse square with steeper pitches and additions — more flashing, and flashing is where the leaks and the money both are — plus postwar ranches on simple gables and farmsteads. The decking on the older houses is often plank that has been wet somewhere. That is a line item, and it belongs on the quote before the tear-off.
When I tell Ohio homeowners not to do this
If you are selling in the next few years, good asphalt is the rational choice, and I will say so on the phone. If one flashing or one pipe boot is leaking on a roof with life left in it, that is a repair — we do not take repair calls. If you want shingles again, you want a different contractor. If the smallest number this year is the question, metal loses that comparison.
Being straight about the Ohio side
Ohio is not our home market — Fort Wayne is, and most of these towns are between thirty minutes and an hour from the office. We work here because the climate and the housing stock are close enough to what we know that our answers transfer. If you would rather have a crew ten minutes away, that is a legitimate thing to want, and there are good ones here.
What we bring is that we install metal only, our crews are manufacturer-certified, and the warranty transfers if you sell. Ohio does not require a roofing license in most areas; we carry general liability, workers' comp, auto and umbrella insurance on every project.
