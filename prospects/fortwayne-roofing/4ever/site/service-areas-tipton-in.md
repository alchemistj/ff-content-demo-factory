Open ground and older houses
Tipton sits in flat farm country, and that is the first thing a roof here deals with. Wind gets a long run across open fields with nothing to slow it down, and it works on the edges, the ridge, and any gable facing the weather. Shingles lift at the tab and reseal poorly. On a concealed-clip metal panel there is no tab to lift and no screw head to work loose.
Most of the housing in town is older — homes around the square and on the older streets with steeper pitches, porches, and decking that has been through a lot of seasons — with some newer houses at the edges and farmhouses with screw-down barns out in the county. On the older stock, the tear-off decides scope; ask any contractor what their number assumes about the deck.
Hail, and what metal does with it
Tipton County catches central Indiana's spring storms. Hail bruises asphalt and starts the failure clock. Metal stays watertight after the same storm; a heavy stone can leave a cosmetic dent, more visible on a flat pan than on a metal shingle or textured profile. Ask your carrier how they treat metal at renewal; the answer varies.
The barn and the house are different decisions
I get asked here whether the house can get the same screw-down panel that is on the barn. It can, and I usually steer people away from it for the house they plan to keep. The washers under those screws age on the same clock and need attention around year ten to twelve. On the house, mechanically seamed standing seam — panels roll-formed on site to full length, every fastener under the seam — holds up to open-country wind better than anything else we install, and it is what goes on a low-slope porch. A four-side interlocking metal shingle is the answer when you want a shingle look and have 3/12 or better. Stone-coated I am cautious about; it manages water after it gets in.
Who this is not for
If you are selling in a few years, good asphalt is the rational choice. One leaking flashing or boot on a sound roof is a repair, and we do not take repair calls. If the lowest number this year is the question, metal loses.
Tipton is about thirty-five minutes from our Noblesville office. Whether the answer ends up metal or one more asphalt roof, you will know why.
