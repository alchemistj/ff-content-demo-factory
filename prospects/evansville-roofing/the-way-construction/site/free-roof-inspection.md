WHAT WE INSPECT
EVERY SURFACE, EVERY SYSTEM.
A real inspection goes beyond counting missing shingles. We look at every component that affects the performance and longevity of your roof system.
SHINGLES AND SURFACE CONDITION
We assess granule retention, cracking, curling, blistering, and overall wear across the full roof surface. We note which areas are aging faster and why.
FLASHING AT ALL PENETRATIONS
Chimneys, skylights, vents, dormers, and wall intersections all have flashing that can lift, corrode, or separate over time. Each is examined individually.
GUTTERS AND DRIP EDGE
We check gutters for granule buildup (an indicator of shingle wear), proper attachment, slope, and downspout routing. Drip edge condition at eaves and rakes is also documented.
VENTILATION
Proper ridge and soffit ventilation protects the roof from inside out. We check that air can move through the system and flag any blocked or failed vents.
ATTIC INTERIOR (WHERE ACCESSIBLE)
The attic tells us a lot that the exterior does not. We look for daylight, moisture staining, mold indicators, insulation contact with the decking, and signs of past or active water intrusion.
FASCIA AND SOFFIT
Damaged or rotted fascia and soffit can allow pests and moisture into the structure. We note any areas of concern so you have the full picture, even if they are not roofing repairs we would perform ourselves.

WHEN TO GET AN INSPECTION
FOUR GOOD REASONS TO SCHEDULE ONE.
You don’t need to see an active leak to warrant an inspection. There are several situations where a clear picture of your roof’s condition is worth having.
AFTER A STORM
Hail and high wind damage is not always visible from the ground. An inspection after a significant storm documents the condition of your roof at that point in time, which can be important for an insurance claim. Even if there is no visible damage, knowing for certain is better than finding out later.
BEFORE BUYING OR SELLING
A roof inspection before closing gives both buyers and sellers accurate information. Buyers avoid inheriting an unknown problem. Sellers can address issues before they become negotiating points or kill a deal.
WHEN YOUR ROOF IS 15 OR MORE YEARS OLD
Roofs do not fail all at once. Deterioration builds gradually, and most homeowners have no idea how much life is left. A periodic inspection gives you time to plan for a replacement on your schedule rather than being forced into a decision by an active leak.
WHEN YOU NOTICE SOMETHING
Stains on interior ceilings, granules in the gutters, curling or missing shingles visible from the ground — any of these are worth a look. The sooner a small problem is identified, the less likely it is to become an expensive one.
