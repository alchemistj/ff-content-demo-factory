## Gate result
Top claim: the braid and the speed row, **15**. **Below the gate (16+).** Lead with the best honest claim, mark the demo "below gate", and show one offer on the strategy page only.

**Offer (proposed, strategy page only): phone first, price first on after-hours calls.** The caller describes the problem, you try to talk them through a simple fix, and if a visit is needed you quote the after-hours price before anyone drives out. Score: Appeal 4 (fear #5), Exclusivity 4 (no competitor states a phone-first or price-first after-hours rule; Dave's only says "no overtime charges"), **16**. It is a proposal, not a claim: Samantha C. is the only reviewer who describes a phone fix.

## Core customer insight (three lines)
- **What the buyer doesn't know:** how long they'll really wait, and whether the person at the door will explain or just sell.
- **What most companies do:** claim 24/7 or "prompt" service without saying how fast or explaining the repair.
- **What Cool Concepts does instead:** someone comes, finds the problem, explains it in plain words and says what the options are, as ten or more reviewers describe.

## Three USP statements
- **A, pain point:** For Lincoln homeowners whose AC or furnace just quit, Cool Concepts gets someone out, finds what failed and explains your options in plain words.
- **B, method:** Unlike companies that say 24/7 and leave the wait unexplained, Cool Concepts gets someone to the house fast and tells you what it found before anything is replaced.
- **C, emotional outcome:** The AC is back on, you know why it quit, and nobody pushed you toward a new system.

**Pick: A.** It meets the #1 and #3 fears with what most reviewers describe, and it stays inside what the folder can prove. The braid scores 15, so the demo is marked below gate.

## What's thin
- **Below gate.** No claim reaches 16 with conservative Exclusivity scoring. The offer is a proposal, not a fact.
- **Residential AC repair material is thin on the business's own side.** The site has no residential AC repair page (its repair page mixes HVAC and commercial kitchen appliances), no hours, no brands beyond "specialize in Daikin", no prices and no warranty terms. The service page rests on six residential AC-repair reviews (Andrea F., Marcus P., Alisa M., Tami B., Miss H., S M.) plus general how-AC-fails content, and the 760-word target is reached with trade explanation, not business facts.
- **Reviews are old.** Most of the 36 pulled reviews are from 2016 to 2020. Only Rocky A. (2025), Miss H. (2025), Kristi A. (2023) and McKenna K. (2023) are recent.
- **24/7 is not used.** The site says "24/7 service" for business customers; residential is Unverified (Conflict in facts.md). No hours are printed.
- **Daikin dealer status and warranty work are Unverified.** The copy says "specialize in Daikin", which the homepage states, and nothing about authorized dealer or warranty.
- **Who James is.** "1234 James" on the case study looks like a placeholder, and reviewers also write "Jimmy". The demo says "we" and never calls James the owner.
- **Email conflict.** coolconceptsne@gmail.com (header) vs coolconcepts@neb.rr.com (contact block). The demo uses the gmail address.
- **Years.** "Since 2005" is the site's own claim and appears once, in the trust bar and the footer.
