## Where Your House Loses Its Comfort

In July, the attic is the hottest place in the house, and that heat works its way down into the rooms below. In January, the cold comes up through the floor and the warmth you paid for goes out through the ceiling.

Insulation slows that movement. A house with little or none works against its own furnace and air conditioner all year.

You usually notice it before you understand it: a bedroom that never gets right, a floor you won't walk on barefoot, a utility bill that doesn't match the size of the house. Those often point to insulation before they point to windows.

## Attics

The attic is where a house gains the most heat in summer and loses the most in winter, so it's the first place to look.

A lot of old attics have thin insulation or none at all.

Before the attic insulation goes in, our crews flag the electrical junction boxes, so the boxes can be found again later and don't get buried.

Some older houses have no way into the attic at all. When that happens, we build an access panel so the crew can get in.

## Floors and Crawl Spaces

If your floors are cold, look underneath. A house over a crawl space often has bare joists with nothing between your feet and the ground, and the pipes run through that same cold air.

Our crews insulate under the floors and lay a vapor barrier on the ground in the crawl space. They wrap exposed pipes and seal duct work where it meets the floor registers. They also seal the gaps where plumbing comes through, like under a sink.

## Walls

Walls are the hardest part of an old house to improve, because you can't see inside them. In an older Wichita house, an exterior wall can have little or no insulation in it.

We insulate exterior walls too, and we'll seal air leaks in the attic on the same visit.

## Mobile Homes

A mobile home loses much of its heat through the underside. The belly is thin, and the wind gets under it.

We wrap the underside and install insulation behind the wrap. That keeps the cold and the wind from pulling heat out of the floor.

## The Weatherization Program

A lot of our insulation work happens through the weatherization program run by the South Central Kansas Economic Development District (SCKEDD). We work under that program.

You apply to the program to see if you qualify. The program decides who qualifies and when your home comes up, and the wait can be long. When your home comes up, our crews do the insulation, the air sealing and the other weatherization work.

If you're not sure whether you're in the program, or you want to know where to start, call us.

## Questions Before You Call

**Will insulation lower my bills?**
It slows the heat moving through your attic, floor and walls, so your furnace and air conditioner run less to hold the same temperature. The comfort shows up first. We don't promise a percentage, because it depends on how little you have now.

**Is my house too old or too small?**
Older houses often gain the most, since they were built with little or no insulation. We've insulated mobile homes and an old house with no way into the attic.

**Do I have to be in the weatherization program?**
Call and tell us about your house. Let us know whether you're already in the program or you just want your attic done, and we'll tell you how we can help.

**Should I do windows instead?**
If a window is fogged, broken or hard to use, replace it. If your windows are fine and a room is still hot or cold, start here. We do [window replacement](/window-replacement-wichita-ks/) too, so you'll hear which one your house needs.

**Where do you work?**
