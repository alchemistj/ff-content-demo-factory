## Start With the Parts of the House You Can't See

A cold floor in January. An upstairs that never cools off in August. A furnace that runs and runs and never quite catches up.

Most people blame the windows, because the windows are the part you can see. But heat leaves a house through whatever part of it has the least in the way, and that is more often the attic, the floor or a gap around a pipe.

That's why we're an insulation company and a window company, not one or the other. We can look at the whole house, tell you where it's losing comfort and fix that, whether it turns out to be the attic or the window.

## Insulation

An attic that's too hot to climb into by July is a house that's paying to cool the roof. A floor that's cold under your feet usually has a crawl space under it with nothing between you and the ground.

Heat moves toward cold, so in winter your warmth heads up and out, and in summer the attic heat comes down. Insulation is what slows that down.

We insulate attics, floors, crawl spaces and exterior walls, and we wrap and insulate the underside of mobile homes. Before attic insulation goes down, our crews flag the electrical junction boxes so they can be found again. If a house has no way into the attic, we make one.

## Air Sealing and Weatherization

Some drafts aren't about insulation at all. They come in around a back door, under a sink where the pipes go through the wall, at the fireplace, or at the duct work by a floor register.

Sealing those gaps is the other half of a weatherized house. We install door sweeps and weatherstripping, seal around plumbing, seal duct work at the registers, wrap pipes and put in smoke alarms, and we'll seal a fireplace if you ask.

A lot of this work happens through the weatherization program run by the South Central Kansas Economic Development District (SCKEDD). We work under that program, and the work is the same insulation and sealing.

## Window Replacement

Windows wear out in ways you can feel before you can see them.

The curtains move with the window shut. The glass is cold to the touch. There's fog between the panes, a sash that won't stay up, or street noise that comes straight through.

Each one means a window that has stopped sealing, and you're heating and cooling the outdoors through it.

We replace them with vinyl windows, and our own crew puts them in.

Windows are built to the size of your openings, so there is a wait between the estimate and the install day. The install itself is a day's work.

## Questions Before You Call

**Should I replace my windows or add insulation first?**
If the windows aren't fogged, broken or hard to use, and a room is still hot or cold, start with the attic and floor. They are the likelier cause, and it's usually the cheaper fix. We do both, so we can tell you which one your house needs.

**How long does a job take?**
Many insulation jobs are done in a day. A whole-house job can bring in more than one crew. Window installs take a day, but the windows are built to order first, so allow weeks between the estimate and install day.

**Do you do weatherization program work?**
Yes. We work under the SCKEDD Weatherization program. The program decides who qualifies and when your home comes up, and the wait can be long.

**Will you make a mess?**
