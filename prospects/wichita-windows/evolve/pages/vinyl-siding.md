## Siding Takes the Weather First

Before the windows, the doors or the roof edge feel a Kansas storm, the siding has already taken it. Wind, hail and summer sun hit it every year.

It rarely fails all at once. It fades, loosens and cracks a little at a time, and the real damage happens behind it, where gaps let in drafts and water.

That's why siding is worth a close look before it's an emergency.

## What Worn-Out Siding Looks Like

Here is what to look for, and what each sign usually means:

- **Faded or chalky panels.** The finish has worn off in the sun. Rub a hand across it and the color comes off as powder.
- **Cracks and hail dings.** Vinyl turns brittle with age. A crack lets water behind the panel.
- **Warped or melted spots.** Panels that have buckled or sagged have been pushed past what they can take, often by heat or by being fastened too tight.
- **Pieces the wind keeps loosening.** Panels that rattle or lift in a storm have lost their hold on the wall.
- **Gaps at corners and around windows.** This is where air and water find their way in.
- **Energy bills creeping up.** Siding that no longer seals the wall lets the house leak heat all winter and gain it all summer.

If you see two or three of these, it's time to have someone look at the whole wall.

## What New Vinyl Siding Does for the House

Vinyl doesn't need painting, and a wash with a hose is most of the upkeep. That makes it a good fit for the part of the house you'd rather not think about.

It also helps your energy bills. New panels that lock together tight leave fewer gaps for air and water, so the house holds its heat in winter and keeps more of the summer out.

You choose the colors and styles with us at your consultation, with the trim and windows in mind.

## Soffit and Fascia Go On With It

Soffit and fascia are the boards under and along the edge of your roof. When they go soft or pull away, water gets behind the gutters and critters find a way into the attic.

We replace them along with your siding, so the whole edge of the house matches the new walls.
