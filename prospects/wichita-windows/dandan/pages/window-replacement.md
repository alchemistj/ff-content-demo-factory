## How You Know a Window Is Done

Some windows fail loudly. A sash that sticks, a crank that spins without moving anything, a window that won't stay up on its own.

Most fail quietly. The room never gets comfortable, you feel a draft on your hand at the glass, or you hear the road as if the window were open.

Fog between the panes is the clearest sign. A double-pane window is two sheets of glass with sealed air or gas between them. When the seal fails, that space fills with moist air and the window stops insulating.

After that, cleaning won't help. The glass unit has to be replaced, and on an older window the frame usually has worn out along with it.

## Window Styles, and Where Each One Fits

We replace double-hung, sliding, casement, picture, awning and shaped windows, and basement windows too. The right style depends on the opening and how you use the room.

**Double-hung** windows slide up and down and suit most older homes. **Sliders** slide side to side, which works for wide, short openings and basements. **Casements** crank outward on a hinge and press tight against the frame when closed.

**Picture windows** don't open at all, which means fewer parts to fail and more glass for the view. **Awning windows** hinge at the top, so you can leave them open in the rain. **Shaped windows** fill the odd openings, the arches and angles that a standard window won't fit.

Some openings are harder than others. Rick works out what fits instead of telling you it can't be done.

## What Goes Into Our Windows

Our windows are custom-built double-pane windows with double-strength glass, a Low-E coating and argon gas between the panes. They exceed ENERGY STAR qualification criteria.

Low-E is a thin coating that reflects heat. It keeps your furnace heat inside in January and the sun's heat out in July. The argon between the panes slows heat down more than plain air does.

Replace one window, a few, or every window in the house. You don't have to do it all at once, and plenty of people start with the worst rooms and come back for the rest.
