## Why a Door Wears Out

A door opens and closes thousands of times a year, and the weather works on it from the outside the whole time.

The weatherstripping flattens and lets air through. The threshold at the bottom takes the water and, over the years, rots. The hinges loosen and the door sags until it rubs the frame.

On a patio slider, the rollers wear and the track bends. That's why you end up shoving the door open with your shoulder.

A new door in a good frame fixes the cause. Adjusting an old door fixes the symptom for a season.

## Can a Door Be Fixed Instead?

Sometimes. If the door itself is sound and only the weatherstripping is tired, new seals may be all it needs.

Replace it when the threshold or frame has rotted, when the door has warped so it won't latch, or when a slider's track is bent. Those problems come back no matter how many times you adjust the door.

Rick looks at it with you and tells you which one you have. If the door is worth keeping, he says so.

## The Doors We Install

We install entry, patio, French and side doors. They're custom-manufactured, so they're made for your opening instead of pulled from a shelf.

**Entry doors** are the front of the house. The door, the frame and the seal around it decide how much of the draft you feel in the hall.

**Patio doors** are the biggest opening most houses have, and the leakiest when old. **French doors** swing on hinges and give you the wide opening with a tighter seal than an old slider.

**Side doors** get less attention, and they get a lot of use. A swollen side door that you slam every summer is worth replacing, not planing.
