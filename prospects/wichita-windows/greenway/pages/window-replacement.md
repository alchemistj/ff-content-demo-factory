## Fog, Drafts, Noise and Windows That Stick

You usually know a window is finished before you can say why. A room that never gets comfortable. Curtains that move with the window shut.

A sash you have to bang to open, or one that won't stay up.

Fog or a haze between the two panes is the clearest sign. Modern windows are sealed double panes with gas between the glass, and when the seal fails the gas leaks out and moist air gets in. The glass can't be cleaned from the inside, and it no longer insulates the way it did.

Drafts and noise mean the same thing from another direction. The frame, the weatherstripping or the seal around the glass has worn out, so air and sound come straight through.

You end up paying to heat and cool the outdoors.

## Why Old Windows Quit

Builder-grade windows are made to a price. The glass seals, the balances that hold a sash up and the plastic or aluminum frames wear out, often after fifteen or twenty years, sometimes sooner on the south and west sides of a Wichita house that take the sun and wind.

Repainting or re-caulking can slow a leak, but it can't fix a seal inside a sealed glass unit or a frame that has warped. Once the glass has fogged, the unit is done.

That is why the fix is usually a new window and not a repair. You don't have to replace every window at once. If only the bedroom windows are failing, I'll tell you so, and we do those.

## Basement and Egress Windows

The small windows below grade get ignored, and they take a beating: ground moisture, lawn mowers, dirt packed against the frame. Old ones leak, rot the framing around them and let in cold air all winter.

We replace basement windows and egress windows and seal them in. An egress window is the one a person has to be able to climb out of, so it matters that it opens properly.

## Sliding Glass and Entry Doors

An old sliding glass door is usually the leakiest opening in the house. The rollers wear down, the track bends, and the seal where the panels meet stops sealing.

You end up shoving it open with your shoulder and feeling the draft all winter. A new slider glides and shuts tight.

We install sliding glass doors and entry doors by themselves or with your windows, so the glass, frames and trim all match.
