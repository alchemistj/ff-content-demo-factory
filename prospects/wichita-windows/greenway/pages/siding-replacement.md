## Signs Your Siding Is Done

Siding takes the Kansas wind, hail and summer sun before anything else on the house does. After enough years it shows.

Look for cracked or warped panels, boards that have rotted at the bottom edge, chalky or badly faded color, and pieces the wind keeps loosening. Look inside, too. A stain on a ceiling or a damp corner in a room along an outside wall means water is getting behind the siding.

That is the part that costs money. Siding is the outer layer that sheds water, and the wall behind it, the sheathing and the framing, is what rots when the siding stops doing that.

## Siding Repair

Not every problem is a replacement. Rotted boards, a section the wind took, storm damage, siding around a chimney or a window that has pulled loose: these are repairs, and they're priced as repairs.

A repair makes sense when the rest of the siding still has years left. If it's failing everywhere, patching only buys time, and I'll say so.

## Fascia, Soffit and Trim

The fascia is the board along the edge of your roof, the soffit is the underside of the overhang, and trim goes around the windows, doors and corners. When they go soft or pull away, water gets behind the gutters and animals find a way into the attic.

We replace them along with your siding, so the roof edge matches the new walls. We also repair single pieces. Gutters and exterior painting are done the same way, alongside the siding or windows.

**How do I know if I need new siding or just a repair?**
If the damage is on one wall or one spot and the rest is sound, it's a repair. If it's cracking, warping or fading across the house, or water has been getting in, replacement is the better money. 

I look at the whole house before I tell you which.
