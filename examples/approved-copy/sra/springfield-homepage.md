**How long does a roof replacement take?**

Most Springfield residential replacements take one to three days, depending on the size, pitch, and complexity of the roof. A straightforward single-story home usually wraps up in a day. Multi-gable rooflines or roofs with significant decking repair take longer.

**Do I need a full roof replacement or just repairs?**

That depends on the scope and location of the damage, the age of the roof, and the material. A few isolated shingles lifted by wind can usually be repaired without replacing the whole system. Widespread hail damage, significant granule loss across the whole roof, or an older roof with compounding issues often makes replacement the more cost-effective call.

**What's the best roofing material for Missouri weather?**

For most Springfield homes, architectural asphalt shingles are the right call. They handle Missouri's combination of hail, summer heat, and humidity better than three-tab shingles, and they cost less long-term because they tend to need fewer repairs between full replacements. For rooflines that take the full force of spring storm systems, a Class 4 impact-rated shingle is worth serious consideration. It resists hail damage better than a standard-rated shingle, and some insurance carriers offer a premium discount for it. Metal roofing is the right answer for homeowners who want a longer service life and are willing to pay more upfront for it.

**How do I know if my roof has hail damage?**

Start at the ground. Check your gutters for heavy granule accumulation after a storm — granules washing off in volume usually means something happened. Dents on soft metals like gutters, vents, and AC units are a reliable sign that hail was large enough to do real damage to the shingles. On the roof itself, hail hits show up as dark spots or “bruised” areas where granules have been knocked loose, exposing the asphalt underneath…that's where leaks and cracking. Significant hail damage can be easy to miss from the ground. If there was a storm event in your area and you're not certain, a free inspection is the only way to know what actually happened.

**Will insurance cover my roof replacement?**

It depends on three things: your policy, the type of damage, and how well that damage is documented. Your policy type matters more than most homeowners realize. Replacement cost value policies pay to replace the roof at today's prices. Actual cash value policies deduct for age and wear, so a 15-year-old roof might only pay out a fraction of what replacement costs. Many carriers have quietly shifted homeowners to actual cash value coverage in recent years. Documentation determines what the adjuster sees.
