Finding dark mold in your Springfield basement, crawl space, or attic is alarming — and it should be taken seriously. ## The Full Remediation Protocol

1. Confirm what's present. When species identification is needed, samples are sent to an accredited third-party lab. That confirmation helps determine the right removal response instead of guessing based on what the mold looks like.
2. Contain the work area before anything is disturbed. Greene Planet sets up sealed barriers, negative air pressure, and HEPA filtration before removal begins. Containment protects the rest of the home from cross-contamination during the remediation process.
3. Remove contaminated materials the right way. Materials that need to come out are removed according to EPA guidelines. The goal is not to cover, spray, or hide the problem — it is to remove what cannot safely stay.
4. Treat structural wood without adding more moisture. Greene Planet uses dry ice blasting. It removes mold from porous structural wood without soaking already moisture-compromised surfaces.
5. Treat remaining surfaces. Greene Planet treats non-porous materials that can safely stay put with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. This closes the cleanup without removing more than necessary.
6. Verify the space is clean before the job closes. Before the job closes, an independent environmental hygienist checks the space and performs clearance testing. That way, the final post-remediation verification does not come us.
7. Fix the conditions that allowed mold to grow. Removal is only half the job. Dehumidification, vapor barrier installation, drainage correction, and foundation crack sealing address the moisture conditions behind the mold. Without that step, remediation is temporary. David's background covers both sides of the problem: removing the mold and correcting the moisture source that caused it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if the dark mold in my home is actually black mold?

You can't tell by looking at it — Stachybotrys, Cladosporium, and Aspergillus niger are visually indistinguishable without lab analysis. The species gets confirmed through air or surface sampling sent to an accredited third-party lab. David collects samples during the inspection and the lab results determine the removal response. What doesn't change regardless of species: visible mold in your home needs professional assessment and proper removal.

### What happens if mold keeps coming back after remediation?

It means the moisture source was never fixed. Surface cleaning without identifying and addressing what's allowing moisture into the space is a temporary solution — the conditions that allowed mold to grow are still there. Greene Planet identifies the moisture source before removal begins and addresses it as part of every job, whether that's drainage correction, vapor barrier installation, foundation crack sealing, or ventilation repair depending on where the mold is and what's feeding it.

### Why does Greene Planet use independent clearance testing instead of clearing jobs in-house?

When the same company performs the remediation and clears the job, they're grading their own work — a built-in conflict of interest. Post-remediation verification by an independent environmental hygienist means the clearance results belong to you, not the contractor. If the numbers aren't back to normal background levels, the job isn't done. Greene Planet doesn't self-clear any job, and recommends independent IEP testing at both the inspection and clearance stages.
