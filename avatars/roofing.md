# Trade avatar: Wichita residential roofing

Method: `docs/writer-guides/AVATAR-AND-USP.md` §1, structured after Ted's SRA brief and Ben Davis analysis (structure only, no facts). Evidence: the four `prospects/wichita-roofing/*/reviews.json` (pulled 2026-11-09) and each `facts.md` "From reviews". Reviewer names are cited as Google shows them. Anything not quoted is marked (inferred). The facts.md "do not quote" lists govern site copy, not this research file.

## The buyers

1. **The storm-hit or leak-hit homeowner (primary).** Pays, decides, usually deals with an insurer.
2. **The landlord or property manager.** Owns rentals, or handles a relative's house. Wants it fixed, documented and closed out. The only evidence: a roof on "one of my commercial buildings" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, Fred O.`) and roofs done for a parent or aunt (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Pam G.`; `flawless/reviews.json, Joseph I.`). The rest is (inferred).
3. **The referrer.** An insurance agent or broker who gets asked "who do I call?" Optionally, a realtor with a roof holding up a closing. They don't pay, but they pick for the buyer and put their own name on it. "my insurance broker sent me to this company after my roof insurance claim got denied" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Crystal E.`, 2025-03, herself a realtor); "I will definitely recommend him to all my Clients!" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, C. M.`).

## Primary avatar: "Storm-Hit Steve and Diana," Wichita

45 to 70, a single-family house in Wichita, Derby or Andover, an aging shingle roof. They've hired contractors before but don't understand their own policy. (inferred) Their insurer may have moved the older roof to actual cash value, or added a separate percentage wind/hail deductible, and they found out at renewal or after the storm. (inferred) They arrive after a spring hailstorm, with yard signs up and a door-knock already behind them, or after a brown ring shows up on the ceiling. They find you through Google, a neighbor or their agent. "Saw that they did an awesome job on the neighbor's house" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Rylie T.`).

## Parking-lot moments

**1. Storm and insurance.** (inferred scene) A May evening in a Dillons lot, the day after the hail. Steve's windshield has a new chip, and half his street has roofer signs in the yard. A man in a company polo knocked at noon offering a "free inspection." Diana's sister just texted that her claim came back partial. Steve opens the renewal email he never read and sees "wind/hail deductible," and he has no idea what it means in dollars. "I'm not signing anything with the guy who knocked," he tells Diana. "But I don't know who else to call."

## Top 5 fears (ranked by emotional intensity)

1. **The claim is denied or cut short and they pay out of pocket.** "the insurance only wanted to replace part of the roof" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Kristi I.`).
2. **Getting taken by a storm chaser or a dishonest roofer.** "we kept getting money hungry companies trying to do business" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Samantha S.`); "WE WERE TAKEN ADVANTAGE OF BY ANOTHER COMPANY... SOMEONE ELSES WORK THAT STOLE MONEY FROM US" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Shelli S.`).
3. **Water getting inside and damage spreading.** "a roof leak that was going into my daughter's closet" (`flawless/reviews.json, Jordan T.`); "They saved my home, my kids room and my quality of life!" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, amanda C.`).
4. **Being sold a roof they don't need.** "if roof is good they will say so instead of TRY to get a new roof out of the deal when not needed" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Crystal E.`).
5. **The roofer goes silent: no callback, no paperwork, no finish.** "Received a quote and have tried to contact to start repair. No calls back" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, Free W.`); "getting these guys to send the documents to the insurance is like talking to a brick" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Gary C.`).

## Top 5 objections (surface, then underlying fear)

1. **"Will insurance cover this?"** → stuck with a bill they can't plan for. Answer with process, not outcome.
2. **"Are you one of those storm guys?"** → nobody to call when it leaks next year. "locally owned operation with a permanent crew... he's here to stay and weill be back to do any sort of call backs" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Tom B.`).
3. **"Do I really need a whole new roof?"** → can't tell diagnosis from pitch. "fully explained the bid without overselling" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, Rachel Y.`).
4. **"Do I have to sign or pay today?"** → locked in before they understand. "He required no upfront money and no sign contracts" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Tom B.`).
5. **"Who's actually doing the work?"** → the seller vanishes and a stranger shows up. "They sent out a sub contractor... Jason was on the job for 5 min and never came back to over see" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, Bobo P.`).

## Top 5 wants

- **Practical:** someone who gets on the roof and shows them photos; a roofer who meets the adjuster; one person who answers the phone; a dry house before the next storm; a clean yard with no nails.
- **Emotional:** to not feel "in the dark"; to be treated as a capable adult; relief that someone is handling it; to be proven right ("I knew that was hail"); to be able to tell the neighbors who to call.
- Evidence: "Juan got up on the roof with the adjuster in support of our claim" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Debbie L.`); "I loved the magnetic tool they used in my yard" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Shelly T.`).

## Phrases to use

"we'll get on the roof and show you photos" · "we'll meet your adjuster" · "if it's a repair, we'll say so" · "you won't be in the dark" · "we call back, even on small jobs" · "local, and here next spring" · "tarp it before the next storm" · "the person who quoted it checks the work" · "find where the water is actually coming in" · "ask us what your deductible means for this job" (we explain, the agent confirms).

## Phrases to avoid (hard nos)

- **Never promise approval or a claim outcome.** No "we'll get it approved," "the full roof you're owed," "free roof." Reviewers say these things; the business can't promise them. Describe what the roofer does (photos, meeting the adjuster, sending documents), never the result.
- **No invented stats:** no roof counts, "#1 in Wichita," response times or savings figures. Reviews say impact-resistant shingles lowered premiums; the amount is the insurer's to say.
- No made-up licenses, certifications, years in business, warranties or guarantees.
- No interpreting policies ("your policy covers..."). Send them to their agent.
- No storm-chaser register: "act now," "limited time," "we'll cover your deductible." 
- No corporate filler: "solutions," "best-in-class," "industry-leading."

## Tone and triggers

**Tone:** a plain-spoken Kansas neighbor who knows roofs. Short sentences. Calm about insurance, never salesy, never cute. Respect what the reader knows, and explain what they don't (ACV, deductible) without talking down.
**Triggers that land:** proof you can see (photos, getting on the roof); relief ("someone has this"); vindication ("you were right, that's hail"); permanence (local, calls back); a clean yard; "no pressure, you decide" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Ramona C.`: "They give you the information and allow you to make your decisions").

### By buyer
- **Landlord or property manager:** fast dry-in, photos and an itemized invoice for the file, one contact, work scheduled around tenants. Lead with documentation and response, not emotion. (inferred)
- **Insurance agent or broker:** "your client won't make you look bad." The roofer documents honestly, says so when there's no claim ("assured me that there was no damage", `ict-roofing/reviews.json, A F.`), and sends paperwork on time. Gary C.'s complaint is the agent's nightmare. (inferred framing)
- **Realtor:** a fast inspection and repair before closing ("our roof replaced before the closing of our property", `douglas-fry/reviews.json, K W.`).

## What's thin

- **Landlords:** almost no review evidence; mostly inferred. Ask an owner who does rental work.
- **ACV and wind/hail deductibles:** from the brief, not reviews. No reviewer says "ACV" or "deductible." Ask an owner or a local agent how often it comes up, and in what words.
- **Door-knockers cut both ways:** Debbie L. and Carl W. (`ict-roofing`) hired the roofer who knocked or showed up the morning after, and were happy. Attack pressure and disappearing, not door-knocking.
- **Negatives:** only 8 written reviews of 3★ or below across four prospects. Most bad-experience language is 5-star reviewers describing the *previous* roofer.
- **Skew:** ICT and Douglas Fry supply most quotes. River City has 7 written reviews.
- **Referrers:** one broker referral, two realtor reviews. Ask owners which agents send work and why.
