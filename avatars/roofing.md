# Trade avatar: Wichita residential roofing

Method: `docs/writer-guides/AVATAR-AND-USP.md`, section 1. Structure follows Ted's SRA brief and Ben Davis analysis; no facts copied from them. Evidence: the four `prospects/wichita-roofing/*/reviews.json` files (Google, pulled 2026-11-09) and each `facts.md` "From reviews". Citations are `slug/reviews.json, Reviewer`. Anything not quoted is marked (inferred). Reviewer names are cited as Google shows them; this file is research, so the facts.md "do not quote" lists (which govern site copy) do not apply here.

## The buyers

1. **The storm-hit or leak-hit homeowner (primary).** Pays, decides, usually deals with an insurer.
2. **The landlord or property manager.** Owns rentals or manages a relative's home. Wants it fixed, documented and closed out. Evidence is thin: one owner of "commercial buildings" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, Fred O.`), people managing a parent's or aunt's roof (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Pam G.`; `flawless/reviews.json, Joseph I.`). The rest is (inferred).
3. **The referrer.** An insurance agent or broker who gets the "who do I call?" question, and a realtor with a roof holding up a closing. Real cases: "my insurance broker sent me to this company after my roof insurance claim got denied" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Crystal E.`, 2025-03, who is also a realtor); a realtor recommending a roofer "to all my Clients" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, C. M.`); a roof "replaced before the closing of our property" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, K W.`). The referrer doesn't pay but chooses for the buyer. They're putting their own name on the recommendation.

## Primary avatar: "Storm-Hit Steve and Diana," Wichita

Ages 45 to 70, own a single-family house in east or west Wichita, Derby or Andover, with a 12- to 25-year-old shingle roof. Both have hired contractors before. Neither understands their own policy. (inferred) Their insurer may have moved the older roof to actual cash value, or added a separate percentage wind/hail deductible, and they found out at renewal or after the storm. (inferred) They come in one of two ways. After a spring hailstorm, half the street has a sign in the yard and someone already knocked on their door. Or there's no storm, just a brown ring on the ceiling. They search Google, ask a neighbor, or call their insurance agent. "Saw that they did an awesome job on the neighbor's house" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Rylie T.`).

## Parking-lot moments

**1. Storm and insurance.** (inferred scene) It's a May evening in the Dillons lot on 21st, the day after the hail. Steve's windshield has a new chip. On the drive home, every third house on his street had a roofer's sign in the yard. A man in a company polo knocked at noon and offered a "free inspection." Diana got a text from her sister: her claim came back partial, they'll only cover one slope. Steve opens the renewal letter in his email he never read and sees "wind/hail deductible." He doesn't know what that number means in dollars. "I'm not signing anything with the guy who knocked," he tells Diana. "But I don't know who else to call."

**2. Old roof, new leak.** (inferred scene) Diana is at the QuikTrip pump on a Saturday after a week of rain. She keeps picturing the brown ring on the guest-room ceiling. It's the size of a dinner plate and bigger than it was Tuesday. The roof was on the house when they bought it. There was no storm, so she figures insurance is probably out. She's scared of two calls: the roofer who says "you need a whole new roof, sign today," and the one who never calls back. "I just want someone to get up there and tell me the truth," she thinks. This matches real reviews: "We had a leak in the kitchen ceiling. We assumed it was from the roof... Anthony crawled into the attic and onto the roof and found that our CHIMNEY CHASE was leaking" (`rivercity/reviews.json, Brant C.`).

## Top 5 fears (ranked by emotional intensity)

1. **The claim is denied or cut short and they pay out of pocket.** Premiums paid for years, then "partial" or "denied." "the insurance only wanted to replace part of the roof" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Kristi I.`).
2. **Getting taken by a storm chaser or a dishonest roofer.** "we kept getting money hungry companies trying to do business" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Samantha S.`); "WE WERE TAKEN ADVANTAGE OF BY ANOTHER COMPANY... SOMEONE ELSES WORK THAT STOLE MONEY FROM US" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Shelli S.`).
3. **Water getting inside and damage spreading.** "a roof leak that was going into my daughter's closet" (`flawless/reviews.json, Jordan T.`); "They saved my home, my kids room and my quality of life!" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, amanda C.`).
4. **Being sold a roof they don't need.** "if roof is good they will say so instead of TRY to get a new roof out of the deal when not needed" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Crystal E.`).
5. **The roofer goes silent: no callback, no paperwork, no finish.** "Received a quote and have tried to contact to start repair. No calls back" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, Free W.`); "getting these guys to send the documents to the insurance is like talking to a brick" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Gary C.`).

## Top 5 objections (surface, then underlying fear)

1. **"Will insurance cover this?"** They're really afraid of being stuck with a bill they can't plan for. Answer with process, not outcome.
2. **"Are you one of those guys who shows up after every storm?"** They're really afraid the roofer won't be around when it leaks next year. "locally owned operation with a permanent crew... he's here to stay and weill be back to do any sort of call backs" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Tom B.`).
3. **"Do I really need a whole new roof?"** They can't tell a diagnosis from a pitch. "fully explained the bid without overselling" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, Rachel Y.`).
4. **"Do I have to sign or pay something today?"** They're afraid of getting locked in before they understand. "He required no upfront money and no sign contracts" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Tom B.`).
5. **"Who's actually doing the work?"** They're afraid the person who sold the job won't be the one who shows up. "They sent out a sub contractor... Jason was on the job for 5 min and never came back to over see" (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, Bobo P.`).

## Top 5 wants

- **Practical:** someone who gets on the roof and shows them photos; a roofer who meets the adjuster; one person who answers the phone; a dry house before the next storm; a clean yard with no nails.
- **Emotional:** to not feel "in the dark"; to be treated as a capable adult; relief that someone is handling it; to be proven right ("I knew that was hail"); to be able to tell the neighbors who to call.
- Evidence: "I never felt in the dark throughout the entire process" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Matthew P.`); "Juan got up on the roof with the adjuster in support of our claim" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Debbie L.`); "I loved the magnetic tool they used in my yard" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Shelly T.`).

## Phrases to use

"we'll get on the roof and show you photos" · "we'll meet your adjuster" · "if it's a repair, we'll say so" · "you won't be in the dark" · "we call back, even on small jobs" · "local, and here next spring" · "tarp it before the next storm" · "the person who quoted it checks the work" · "find where the water is actually coming in" · "ask us what your deductible means for this job" (we explain, the agent confirms).

## Phrases to avoid (hard nos)

- **Never promise approval or a claim outcome.** No "we'll get it approved," "full roof you're owed," "free roof," or "we'll fight until you win." Customers say these things in reviews. The business can't promise them. Describe what the roofer does (photos, meeting the adjuster, sending documents) and never the result.
- **No invented stats.** No "X roofs," "#1 in Wichita," response-time numbers or savings percentages. No premium-discount amounts. Impact-resistant shingles *can* lower premiums per reviews, but the amount is for the insurer to say.
- No made-up licenses, certifications, years in business, warranties or guarantees.
- No interpreting policies ("your policy covers..."). Send them to their agent.
- No storm-chaser register: "act now," "limited time," "free roof," "we'll cover your deductible." (inferred: also legally sensitive in Kansas; verify before stating anything about it)
- No corporate filler: "solutions," "best-in-class," "industry-leading."

## Tone and triggers

**Tone:** a plain-spoken Kansas neighbor who's been on a thousand roofs. Short sentences. Calm about insurance, never salesy, never cute. Respect what the reader knows, and explain what they don't (ACV, deductible) without talking down.
**Triggers that land:** proof you can see (photos, getting on the roof); relief ("someone has this"); vindication ("you were right, that's hail"); permanence (local, calls back); a clean yard; "no pressure, you decide" (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, Ramona C.`: "They give you the information and allow you to make your decisions").

### By buyer
- **Landlord or property manager:** speed to dry-in, photos and an itemized invoice for their files, one contact, scheduling around tenants, handling the insurance paperwork. (inferred) Lead with documentation and response, not emotion.
- **Insurance agent or broker:** "your client won't be embarrassed": the roofer documents the damage honestly, doesn't push claims that aren't there (`ict-roofing/reviews.json, A F.`: told "no damage... normal wear and tear"), and sends paperwork on time. Gary C.'s complaint is the agent's nightmare. (inferred framing)
- **Realtor:** fast inspection and repair before closing, with a written scope (`douglas-fry/reviews.json, K W.`; `douglas-fry/reviews.json, C. M.`).

## What's thin

- **Landlords:** almost no direct review evidence. The section is mostly inferred. One owner call with a business that does rental work would sharpen it.
- **ACV and wind/hail deductibles:** come from the brief and general knowledge, not reviews. No reviewer uses "ACV" or "deductible." Confirm with an owner or local agent how often Wichita homeowners hit this, and use their words.
- **Door-knockers:** reviews cut both ways. Samantha S. complains about "money hungry companies," but Debbie L. and Carl W. hired the roofer who knocked or showed up the morning after and were happy. Don't attack door-knocking as such. Attack pressure and disappearing.
- **Negative reviews:** only 8 low reviews across the four prospects. Bad-experience language mostly comes from 5-star reviews describing the *previous* roofer.
- **Bias:** ICT and Douglas Fry supply most quotes. River City has 7 written reviews, all 5-star.
- **Referrers:** one broker referral and two realtor reviews. An owner call should ask which agents and agencies send work and why.
