# Trade avatar: Wichita garage door repair and installation

Built once (2026-11-13, ff-architect #651) and reused across every Wichita garage door demo. Method: `docs/writer-guides/AVATAR-AND-USP.md`, section 1. Structure modeled on `avatars/hvac.md` and `avatars/roofing.md`; no facts carried over.

**Evidence base:** `review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros` (76 text reviews of 84 on the listing, all 5 stars; no owner replies in the pull). Plus the `qualify.md` files and `STATUS.md` under `prospects/wichita-garage-doors/` for context on the four prospects. The other three prospects have no reviews on `review-data` yet. Quotes are word for word. Anything not quoted is marked (inferred). This file describes the buyer, not any one company: never put a quote here on a demo for a business other than the one it came from.

---

## 1. The buyers

**A. The homeowner (primary).** Owns the house, has a door that won't move, and often has a car stuck inside or a kid waiting. Messaging: someone answers, someone comes soon, and the price matches what you were told.

**B. The new-home or new-build buyer.** Buying, building or listing, and wants the garage to match the house. "When I was purchasing my new home, the garage was really not something I was thinking much about, but knew I wanted it to complement the house" (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Debbie S.`). Also sellers: "We experienced a couple of attempted breakins through the garage before we listed our house" (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Sheila G.`). Messaging: a clear quote, a door that looks right, installed when they said.

**C. The shop, barn or farm owner.** Not a house door at all. "We need 3 shop doors for our new shop" (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Brittany C.`); "They have been a blessing for our new barn." (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Cody W.`); "Had then install two garage doors on a old steel framed farm garage" (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Charles A.`). Messaging: they handle odd sizes and old buildings, and they haul off the old door.

**Also: referrers.** Almost none show up. One reviewer says "I will be recommending them to family and friends!" (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Jaqueline G.`), and one says they "really went to bat with my insurance company" (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Gail S.`). No realtor, landlord or property manager speaks. See "What's thin."

## 2. The primary avatar

**"Stuck-Door Sam," 35 to 65, Wichita or nearby (Newton, Cheney, Derby, Andover) homeowner** (inferred composite). One- or two-story house with an attached two-car garage, a door that's 10 to 20 years old, and a car inside. Works a normal week, so the door usually quits on a Saturday morning or a weeknight. Doesn't have "a garage door guy." Found the company by calling down a list: "I was on my 9th and last call" (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Dane F.`), "calling five other companies to get quotes" (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Kimberly B.`), or a Google search at the worst possible hour.

## 3. The identity iceberg

- **What he says publicly:** "My garage door is broken. I need somebody out here." (inferred)
- **What he admits privately:** "I can't tell a fair price from a rip-off, and I'm tired of calls that end in voicemail or 'next week.'" (inferred, from the call-around stories in section 8)
- **What he won't admit to anyone:** "I was hoping the door would hold out another year. I'm embarrassed I don't know what a torsion spring is, and I'm afraid I'll pay a weekend premium for something I should have fixed sooner." (inferred)

## 4. Parking-lot moments

**Moment 1: "The spring snapped."** (inferred scene; details drawn from reviews) Saturday, 7:15 a.m. There was a bang in the garage like a gunshot, and now the door is stuck closed with the car behind it. He has a 9 o'clock to be at. He has called two companies. One says a service fee for coming out, plus the repair. One says a "weekend rate." The third says Tuesday. He's standing in the kitchen with the keys in his hand, looking at the clock.

**Moment 2: "I backed into it."** (inferred scene; details drawn from reviews) Early morning, rushing to get the kids to school. The door is off its rollers and hanging crooked, and the car won't fit past it. Everyone gets out of the car. He sends a quote request on his phone and wonders whether this is a $200 problem or a $2,000 one. "Backed up into my garage door early in the morning." (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Jaqueline G.`)

**Moment 3: "The opener just quit."** (inferred scene; details drawn from reviews) A Thursday evening, thunderstorm coming. The remote clicks and nothing happens. The door is stuck down, and he can't tell if it's the opener or the door. He calls after 5 p.m. and the answer is "somebody will call you tomorrow." He's already planning to park in the driveway and walk in the rain.

**Moment 4: "The door is loud now."** (inferred scene; details drawn from reviews) It's not an emergency. The door groans, jerks and bangs, and the neighbors can hear it. He's been putting it off for months. Then it's 7 a.m. and the kids ask if the garage is haunted. He'll call today, if someone makes it easy.

## 5. Top 5 fears, ranked by emotional intensity

1. **Stuck with a broken door and nobody will come.** The car is trapped, the house can't be closed up, and the weekend is gone. "Our garage door spring broke, leaving the door stuck closed. After another company lost our appointment and told us we'd have to wait another week" (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Ashley S.`). "My door that morning almost collapsed and was stuck in place." (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Jay R.`)
2. **Paying a weekend or after-hours surcharge on top of the repair.** "Everyone I called wanted to charge a 'service fee for coming out on top of the service' or even crazier 'double charge for being after hours and over time for a weekend'." (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Dane F.`)
3. **Being oversold.** "I never felt like I was being oversold on products or services." (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Eric W.`) "They fixed my door after another company told me I had to replace it." (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Carrie F.`)
4. **Booking a visit that never happens, or a wait that costs a day off work.** "Either they would give me a two hour time window to meet and discuss my options, they had no openings for several days, or their prices were sky high." (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Kimberly B.`)
5. **A repair that doesn't hold, and the last company won't come back.** "Cheney Door drug their feet on call backs, sending out a person and providing an estimate." (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Janet S.`, about a spring job from a year and a half earlier.)

## 6. Top 5 objections (surface, then the fear underneath)

1. **"Can you come today?"** → "If you can't, I'm stuck here and so is my car." (fear 1)
2. **"What does it cost just to come out?"** → "I'll pay a fee and still not have a door." "no extra charge" is in the reviews more than once: "He came out at 8:30 p.m. and repaired my door. There was not extra charge." (sic; `review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Fred O.`) (fear 2)
3. **"Do I really need to replace the whole door?"** → "I can't tell a repair from a sales pitch." (fear 3)
4. **"Will somebody actually be here when you say?"** → "I've been stood up before." (fear 4)
5. **"Are you a real local company, or a big outfit with a call center?"** → "I don't want to be a ticket number." "So much better than the other big guys to work with as Rachel is actually happy you called the business!" (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, David R.`) (fears 4, 5; also inferred)

## 7. Top 5 wants

**Practical:** a person answers, or calls back the same evening; same-day or next-morning arrival; the tech arrives with the parts ("he brought the tools just in case", `review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, aMoistWaffl3`); a quote before the work starts, and the bill matches it; the door runs quiet afterward and the garage is cleaned up.

**Emotional:** relief ("They are a life saver and do miracle work!" `review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Janet S.`); being told the truth ("Honest, efficient and high quality work" `review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Stacy G.`); not feeling taken advantage of ("Don't let the big names take advantage of you in a pinch" `review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Dane F.`); a small local company that treats them like a neighbor ("I really like supporting small local businesses." `review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, papa S.`); having "a guy" at last ("They will be my first call when it's time to replace the opener." `review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Eric W.`).

## 10. Phrases to use and to avoid

**Use:** "a real person answers" (only if true for the business) · "we'll tell you what's broken before we touch it" · "the door's down, we'll look at it" · "a quote before we start" · "we bring the parts" (only if true) · "we clean up and haul off the old door" · "springs, openers, rollers, cables" in plain words · the tech's first name, if the business provides one · "the neighbors won't hear it."

**Avoid (hard nos):**
- Invented stats ("90% fixed on the first visit"), prices ("$89 spring repair"), guarantees ("same-day guaranteed") or warranties unless that business's facts.md proves them.
- "24/7" or "emergency service" unless the business confirms it picks up at night. Reviews describe Saturday, Sunday and evening visits, and that's a pattern for one company, not a claim for the trade.
- "No after-hours charge" or "no service fee" as a promise, unless that business confirms it. Reviewers say it; the page can't promise it for them.
- Licenses, certifications, BBB status, brand dealer status, awards, years in business or "family-owned" not in facts.md. (GarageStars' site makes several such claims that `qualify.md` flags as unverified.)
- Dollar figures from reviews used as the company's price.
- Any claim about injuries, door safety standards, or insurance outcomes. Gail S. says the company "went to bat with my insurance company": don't turn that into a promise about claims.
- Corporate filler: "premier," "industry-leading," "comprehensive solutions," "your one-stop shop."
- Door puns ("we'll open the door to savings") in the hero. The reader is stuck, not amused (inferred).

## 11. Tone and triggers

**Tone:** plain, calm, quick to the point, neighborly. Short sentences. Talk like the tech in the driveway, not like a sales office. Respect the reader's intelligence; don't explain torsion springs unless they ask.

**Triggers that land:** a named person who answers (reviews name Rachel, Eli, Elias and Esteban constantly, and they're one business's staff); speed stated as plain fact ("within 2 hours", "within an hour of my initial call"); "one spring broke, the other will soon, your call"; the price matching what the quote said; "no weekend surprise" (if true); a clean garage when they leave.

## 12. What's thin

- **One business, all five stars.** Every quote comes from The Door Pros, LLC (76 text reviews, no 1- to 4-star ones). Fears and past bad experiences come from relief stories about "the other company", not from complaints. GarageStars (11), Albert's (24, 4.5) and Love (140) have no reviews on `review-data` yet.
- **Parking-lot moments are mostly inferred.** The spring-break, backed-into-it and opener-quit scenes are drawn from review details and common garage-door failures. The Wichita-specific parts (Saturday spring breaks, storms, Newton and Cheney addresses) are only partly in the reviews. Seasonal triggers (cold snaps shrinking weather seals, spring storm damage) are not in the reviews at all.
- **Fear 1 is a ranking, not a measurement.** Reviews show the stuck door and the lost weekend again and again, but nobody says what they were most afraid of.
- **The "no extra charge on weekends" theme is one company's policy.** It appears in at least four reviews (Lori B., Darin P., Dane F., Fred O.), but it can't be written as a trade pattern. An owner call must confirm what each business charges and when.
- **Insurance, landlords, property managers and realtors barely appear.** Gail S. mentions insurance once; Sheila G. mentions selling a house. No landlord voice. Ask an owner whether rental or realtor work is real.
- **Installation is thinner than repair.** About a dozen reviews cover new doors (including shop, barn and farm doors), versus several dozen for repairs and springs. Price, financing, lead times and permits are barely discussed.
- **Spanish-speaking buyers:** one review is in Spanish (`review-data/wichita-garage-door-pros, Alexander O.`: "Buen servicio y rapido"), and the site says "Hablamos Español!" Don't write a Spanish version without the owner.
- **An owner call would sharpen:** what share of calls end in a spring versus an opener versus a full door; how weekend and after-hours calls are handled and priced; how they handle a customer who wants a second opinion; whether they do commercial, farm and shop doors on purpose; and who actually answers the phone.
