# Trade avatar: Wichita residential electrical

Built once (2026-11-13, ff-architect #651) and reused across every Wichita electrician demo. Method: `docs/writer-guides/AVATAR-AND-USP.md`, section 1. Structure modeled on `avatars/hvac.md` and `avatars/roofing.md`; no facts carried over.

**Evidence base:** `review-data/` folders ballinger-electric-wichita, bybee-electric-wichita, enegren-electric-wichita, all-phase-electric (Springfield MO) and stone-electric-llc (Ozark MO), plus `prospects/wichita-electrical/TRADE-NOTES.md` on `origin/claude/wichita-electrical`. linnebur-electric-wichita has no reviews (the pull failed; see section 12). Quotes are word for word. Anything not quoted is marked (inferred). This file describes the buyer, not any one company: never put a quote here on a demo for a business other than the one it came from.

---

## 1. The buyers

**A. The homeowner (primary).** Owns the house, pays the bill, and is the one standing in a dead kitchen or looking at a scorched outlet. Messaging: find the real problem, say it plainly, price it before the work starts.

**B. The landlord, property manager or investor.** Doesn't live there; the tenant is the one without power. "Marcus and Enegren Electric continue to show up on time and perform extremely great electrical work for the properties that I manage." (`review-data/enegren-electric-wichita, Tyler H.`) "I already have him scheduled for my other properties." (`review-data/ballinger-electric-wichita, Spencer L.`) The tenant's side counts too: "I’m the tenant at this location. My experience with the tech was extremely good." (`review-data/enegren-electric-wichita, Rosalie G.`) Messaging: one call, it gets handled, the tenant is treated well, the invoice is clean.

**C. The adult child calling for an aging parent.** "My elderly parents were having a few issues at their 60+ year old home. Ben provided a thorough walk through, explaining options & a detailed estimate." (`review-data/ballinger-electric-wichita, Natalie O.`) Messaging: patience, plain explanations, and a call to the adult child.

**Also: referrers.** Remodelers, realtors and other trades send electrical work. "We are glad our kitchen remodelers suggested them." (`review-data/bybee-electric-wichita, John H.`) "As a realtor, I’m always looking for reliable contractors" (`review-data/ballinger-electric-wichita, Rhonda L.`). Also home inspectors: "Had Stone Electric out to get a quote on some needed fixes that were pointed out during a home inspection." (`review-data/stone-electric-llc, Jordan R.`) Treat them as a channel, not a page.

## 2. The primary avatar

**"Dead-Outlet Dana," 35 to 65, Wichita homeowner** (inferred composite). A house from the 1960s to 1990s, or a newer one bought with a wiring puzzle in it. Reviewers describe a "50 year old house" (`review-data/ballinger-electric-wichita, Rajeeb H.`), "a 60+ year old home," and a house bought last fall where "the circuits didn't make any sense" (`review-data/bybee-electric-wichita, TSX00`). She has a remodel, a new appliance or a tripped breaker on her hands and no electrician she already trusts. She found the company the way reviewers did: a review search ("I chose Ben because he has the best reviews", `review-data/ballinger-electric-wichita, Marilyn R.`), three or four quotes ("I got four quotes", `review-data/bybee-electric-wichita, Meredith S.`), or a referral from a contractor or neighbor.

## 3. The identity iceberg

- **What she says publicly:** "I've got outlets that quit working. Can somebody come look?" (inferred)
- **What she admits privately:** "I can't tell a small fix from an expensive one, and I'm scared of what's behind the wall." (inferred, from the second-opinion and old-house stories in section 8)
- **What she won't admit to anyone:** "I've been living with this for a while. I plugged a power strip into a power strip. If something goes wrong in this house, it's on me." (inferred; Vanessa B. lived with a doorbell problem "for years", `review-data/enegren-electric-wichita`)

## 4. Parking-lot moments

**Moment 1: "The morning after the storm."** (inferred scene; details drawn from reviews and Kansas weather) A line of thunderstorms went through Wichita overnight. There was a bang around 2 a.m. Now the microwave is dead, half the outlets don't work, and the garage door won't open. She's in the kitchen with her phone on 12 percent, looking at the panel, which looks fine and tells her nothing. She isn't sure whether to flip the main back on. Her father, who lives with them, has a medical device that needs power. She has called one company that took a message. She doesn't want the cheapest. She wants someone who will answer, come today and tell her what happened. Reviewers lived this: "I called them the morning after our house was struck by lightening" (`review-data/enegren-electric-wichita, Robin A.`).

**Moment 2: "The outlet is warm."** (inferred scene) She's plugging in a vacuum and the plate is warm. There's a faint smell she can't place. It's been a little warm for a week. She's not sure it's a problem or whether she's the kind of person who calls an electrician over a warm outlet. She thinks about the fire marshal story from the news and the kids asleep upstairs. She'll call in the morning, but she'd really like someone to tell her tonight whether it can wait.

**Moment 3: "The panel quote."** (inferred scene) The inspector, or the last electrician, said the panel is old and "needs to be replaced." She's sitting in her car in the driveway with a number on her phone that has a comma in it. He may be right. The other three companies said they were too busy to come look. She doesn't know whether to sign, wait, or call someone else and lose another week. She wants someone who will tell her what's actually wrong.

## 6. Top 5 objections (surface, then the fear underneath)

1. **"Can you come today?"** → "If you can't, I'm in the dark, or I'm leaving a bad outlet live overnight." (fear 1, 2)
2. **"Is this actually dangerous, or are you selling me something?"** → "I can't tell a real hazard from a sales pitch." Reviewers rewarded the one who said it plainly and said when nothing was needed. (fear 1, 3)
3. **"What's it going to cost?"** → "I'll pay for a visit and a vague invoice." Reviewers want the bid first: "He reviewed and gave a bid of what we wanted completed and he did what he said." (`review-data/ballinger-electric-wichita, Alan B.`) "gave me multiple options to choose from" (`review-data/enegren-electric-wichita, Nathan R.`) (fear 3)
4. **"Will it pass inspection, and will you be back if it doesn't?"** → "Once you're paid, I'm on my own." "We had a problem with a city inspector wich was NOT their fault, and they were so very focused on resolving the issue for me." (`review-data/bybee-electric-wichita, Jr R.`) (fear 4)
5. **"Who exactly is coming into my house?"** → "I can't be there, or I'm home alone, and I can't tell if they'll be decent." (inferred, from buyers B and C; see Catie M. above) (fear 5)

## 7. Top 5 wants

**Practical:** a person answers; someone comes today or within a day or two; the problem is found and explained ("explained every step of his work, gave me options, and let me choose," `review-data/enegren-electric-wichita, Michael K.`); the bid and options before the work starts; a call or text when they're on the way; a clean house; the panel labeled ("labeled my panel", `review-data/enegren-electric-wichita, Nathan R.`).

**Emotional:** relief; being told the truth; not feeling dumb ("explained everything in a way we could understand", `review-data/enegren-electric-wichita, Steve L.`); knowing the house is safe; having "a guy" again ("He is on our 'Definitely ask for list!'", `review-data/enegren-electric-wichita, Patricia J.`).

## 10. Phrases to use and to avoid

**Use:** "we'll tell you what we found and what it means" · "a warm outlet is a loose connection; here's what we do about it" · "options, with prices, before we start" · "we'll tell you if it doesn't need work" · "we call or text when we're on the way" · "we'll label your panel" · "your parent or tenant gets the same respect you would" · "we leave it clean."

**Avoid (hard nos):**
- Invented stats, prices ("$89 service call"), guarantees ("same-day guaranteed"), or warranties unless that business's facts.md proves them.
- "24/7" or "emergency service" unless the business confirms it answers at night. (All Phase: Lydia K. says the owner answered at 10 PM once; that is one story, not a policy.)
- License numbers, license types, "master electrician," certifications, brand or utility partner status, awards or years in business that are not in that business's facts.md. Wichita and Sedgwick County license through MABCD; state a license only if a primary source confirms it (TRADE-NOTES.md).
- Dollar figures from reviews used as the company's price.
- Claims about fire prevention, insurance outcomes, code compliance or "passes inspection every time."
- Corporate filler: "electrical solutions," "premier," "industry-leading," "powering your life."
- Electricity puns ("shocking prices") in the hero. The reader is scared, not amused (inferred).

## 11. Tone and triggers

**Tone:** plain, calm, quick to the point, neighborly. Short sentences. Explain what the sign means and what we do about it, without scaring anyone. Talk like the electrician who walked you through the panel, not like a sales office.

**Trade knowledge readers value** (from TRADE-NOTES.md; general trade knowledge, not claims about any business): why breakers trip (overload, short, ground fault); what a warm outlet, buzzing or flicker means (loose connection, fire risk); when an old panel is a safety problem (fuse box, known-failure brands, scorch marks, no room for new circuits, small service in a house with AC or an EV charger); what a panel upgrade involves (permit, inspection, power company disconnect, usually a day).

**Triggers that land:** a named electrician and a photo ahead of time; a scheduled time, not a window; an explanation of what happened and how to prevent it; options with prices; a labeled panel; the invoice saying what was done.

## 12. What's thin

- **Linnebur has no reviews.** The review pull for `linnebur-electric-wichita` failed (Apify HTTP 402 in the receipt), so this avatar has nothing from Linnebur.
- **Selection bias.** Ballinger and Enegren are almost entirely five-star. The one- to three-star evidence is thin: Bybee (about five), All Phase (two), Stone (one), Ballinger (a four). Fears 3 to 5 lean on those few complaints.
- **Two of the six folders are Springfield and Ozark, Missouri** (All Phase, Stone). They support the fears and wants, but not Wichita specifics.
- **The #1 fear is partly inferred.** Fire and safety are in the reviews as gratitude stories (Bybee's bird's nest, Ballinger's Federal Pacific panel), not as stated fear. The "warm outlet" and "storm night" moments are from public trade knowledge and Kansas weather, not reviews.
- **Parking-lot moments 1 to 3 are inferred scenes.** Only the lightning-strike calls (Enegren: Robin A., Shawn C.) and the oxygen-dependent father (Bybee: Meredith S.) are backed by reviews.
- **Money is vague.** Financing appears once ("I even got to pay for 3 months at 0% APR", `review-data/ballinger-electric-wichita, Lita M.`). How people pay for a panel upgrade isn't in the reviews.
- **Landlord and tenant voice is light:** two property managers (Tyler H., Spencer L.) and one tenant (Rosalie G.). No landlord complaints.
- **Licensing, permits and inspections:** reviewers mention them rarely (Wayne R.: "proactively checked in with the city inspector"; Darryk L.: "passed inspection first time"). We don't know how each business handles them.
- **An owner call would sharpen:** how night and storm calls are handled; whether the phone is answered after hours; how a second opinion is treated; panel upgrade scope and who coordinates with Evergy; how landlords and tenants are handled; whether they work with realtors, inspectors and remodelers.
