# Trade avatar: Wichita residential HVAC

Built once (2026-11-12, ff-architect #651) and reused across every Wichita HVAC demo. Method: `docs/writer-guides/AVATAR-AND-USP.md`, section 1. Structure modeled on Ted's SRA brief and Ben Davis avatar; no facts carried over.

**Evidence base:** every `reviews.json` in `prospects/wichita-hvac/` (cooks, integrity, aircapital, dreammakers, alpha, epic, firstcall, rods; 529 text reviews, owner replies included) plus each `facts.md` review section. Quotes are word for word. Anything not quoted is marked (inferred). This file describes the buyer, not any one company: never put a quote here on a demo for a business other than the one it came from.

---

## 1. The buyers

**A. The homeowner (primary).** Owns the house, pays the bill, and is usually the one standing in the hot kitchen. Messaging: speed, a straight diagnosis, and a fair bill.

**B. The landlord or short-term-rental host.** Doesn't live there. The tenant or guest is the one who's hot. "I had an issue with my unit in a short term rental after 8pm, and Sam (the owner) personally took care of it himself." (`integrity/reviews.json, Hunter M.`) "They made our tenant happy, and that makes us happy!" (`integrity/reviews.json, Lynne L.`) Their bad experiences are about the tech's conduct and paying for a visit that did nothing: "I spent $115.00 for somebody to be late, dodge phone calls, and not even do what I asked." (`cooks/reviews.json, Brian B.`) Messaging: they get one call and it gets handled, the tenant is treated well, and the invoice is clean.

**C. The adult child calling for an aging parent.** Calls from their own house or their phone at work. "Thank you Cooks for the fast response to take care of my 90 year old father." (`cooks/reviews.json, Carolyn R.`) "My parents are elderly and my mom was crying after the rudeness of the other company." (`rods/reviews.json, Chris A.`) They judge the company by how it treats Mom: "they way the treater her, her house and the entire project really made an impression on me" (`integrity/reviews.json, Brett J.`). Messaging: patience, respect, and a call back to the adult child.

**Also: referrers.** Realtors, investors and contractors send work: "for not only myself but for my real estate clients!" (`epic/reviews.json, Trisha H.`); "I was referred to Integrity by another local investor." (`integrity/reviews.json, Hunter M.`). Treat them as a channel, not a page.

## 2. The primary avatar

**"Hot-House Dana," 35 to 60, Wichita homeowner** (inferred composite). Ranch or two-story in town or a nearby suburb, with an original system that is getting old; reviews mention units 20, 21, 24, 30 and "nearly 40" years old. Kids, pets, or a parent who stays over. She has used an HVAC company before but doesn't have "a guy" anymore. She found the company the way reviewers did: "I did a quick Google search, and called the 1st on the list" (`rods/reviews.json, Mrs. A.`), or "Reached out on facebook for recommendations for reliable HVAC service" (`rods/reviews.json, Dale C.`), or a neighbor or sister-in-law.

## 3. The identity iceberg

- **What she says publicly:** "Our AC went out. We just need somebody out here." (inferred)
- **What she admits privately:** "I can't tell a bad part from a sales pitch, and I'm scared the first quote I hear will be 'replace the whole thing.'" (inferred, from the second-opinion stories in section 8)
- **What she won't admit to anyone:** "I let the maintenance slide, I don't know what's in the savings account for this, and I'm afraid I'm about to make an expensive decision in a panic while my kids are sweating." (inferred)

## 4. Parking-lot moments

**Moment 1: "It died tonight."** (inferred scene; details drawn from reviews) It's 9:40 on a July night in Wichita. It's been triple digits for days and the forecast says more. The thermostat says 86 and climbing. The baby finally fell asleep in a damp onesie; her mother, staying with them for the week, is fanning herself on the couch and saying she's fine. Dana has called three companies. Two went to voicemail; one said next Tuesday. Her husband is googling capacitors. She's thinking about a hotel and what it costs. She doesn't care who's cheapest. She wants the one who will actually pick up and show up. The furnace version is the same scene in January: a cold snap, the house dropping into the 50s, and her 84-year-old dad alone across town who keeps saying "don't make a fuss."

**Moment 2: "Replace my system."** (inferred scene) The tech left 20 minutes ago. Dana is sitting in the car in the driveway with the quote open on her phone: a whole new system, with a number that has a comma in it. He was polite. He said the unit was old and it wasn't worth fixing. Maybe that's true. But she remembers her neighbor saying another company once told her the same thing, and it turned out to be a small part. She doesn't know whether to sign, call someone else and lose another day, or ask questions that make her sound stupid. She just wants someone she can believe.

## 5. Top 5 fears, ranked by emotional intensity

1. **Someone at home gets hurt by the heat or the cold.** A baby, a toddler, a parent with health problems. "Absolute necessity with toddlers inside." (`dreammakers/reviews.json, Ashley N.`) Being brushed off is the worst outcome: "They also told me we would be fine and to just put a box fan in front of my two month old." (`rods/reviews.json, Katie H.`)
2. **Being sold a new system they didn't need.** "Rather they turned on their salesmen pitch and went down that road." (`rods/reviews.json, Haley H.`; a second company found a bad capacitor.) "it actually was just to sell a new system" (`cooks/reviews.json, N R.`)
3. **Nobody will come.** "everyplace I called was tied up for over a week!" (`alpha/reviews.json, ali C.`) "of the 7 companies that I called, this was the 1st and only one that had a real person who answered my call" (`rods/reviews.json, Dustin R.`)
4. **A bad install, then nobody answers.** "No accountability for improper install even under warranty." (`cooks/reviews.json, Blakeracer R.`) "Never got a call from anyone!!" (`epic/reviews.json, Kevin B.`)
5. **Paying for visits that fix nothing.** "we were charged 95 for them to come out and do nothing" (`cooks/reviews.json, Candy H.`); "Paid $200 for info that was wrong" (`epic/reviews.json, Tyler E.`); "Finally fixed furnace after 4 trips!" (`cooks/reviews.json, Terry S.`)

## 6. Top 5 objections (surface, then the fear underneath)

1. **"Can you come today?"** → "If you can't, my family spends another night in this." (fear 1, 3)
2. **"Do I really need a whole new system?"** → "I can't tell your diagnosis from your sales pitch." Haley H. got a second opinion because "The price we were quoted seemed high" (`rods/reviews.json, Haley H.`). (fear 2)
3. **"What's this going to cost me just to have you look?"** → "I'll pay and still be hot." "Here I was preparing myself to fork out $$$ and melting." (`alpha/reviews.json, Desiree F.`) (fear 5)
4. **"Will you be back if it acts up again?"** → "Once you're paid, I'm on my own." (fear 4)
5. **"Will you deal with my tenant / my mom without me there?"** → "I can't be there, and I don't trust strangers to be decent to them." (inferred, from buyers B and C)

## 7. Top 5 wants

**Practical:** a real person answers; someone comes today or first thing tomorrow; the problem is found and explained ("explained everything" appears across all eight companies); repair and replace options with prices before work starts; the house left clean.

**Emotional:** relief ("Prompt timely response to AC malfunction during sweltering summer heat is like a divine intervention." `integrity/reviews.json, Charles M.`); being told the truth; not feeling dumb ("I never felt like I was being talked at or down to" `epic/reviews.json, Jennifer D.`); knowing the family is safe; having "a guy" again ("I think we just found our new hvac guy." `rods/reviews.json, Mrs. A.`).

## 8. Past bad experiences

- **The replace pitch that wasn't true.** "Another HVAC company told me I would have to replace my heater and ac and then gave me an estimate that was insane." (`cooks/reviews.json, LaVona B.`) "One partially fixed the issue and the other told us we needed to replace the the whole unit." (`alpha/reviews.json, Jenae W.`) "Every other company we have had out over the years said we needed a bigger unit." (`epic/reviews.json, Amy I.`) "the other company was lying to us" (`cooks/reviews.json, Matthew L.`, about a return duct).
- **The wait.** "it took them 4 hours to have someone reach out to me just to get on their schedule" (`aircapital/reviews.json, Jayci G.`). "After about 4 days without heat we were finally able to get first call out" (`firstcall/reviews.json, Britiany P.`, after a home warranty assignment).
- **The repeat visits.** A renter on her fifth visit: "I have to take off during the day to fit THEIR time slots" (`cooks/reviews.json, Amanda C.`).
- **The install that never worked.** "It never worked right." (`rods/reviews.json, Chris A.`) "Shoddy installation has caused numerous problems." (`cooks/reviews.json, Cecelia S.`)
- **The shrug on the permit.** Other bidders said "eh, we can pull a permit if you really want us to" (`alpha/reviews.json, Nick B.`).
- **The defensive owner.** Some owner replies argue with the reviewer ("Please don't leave negative feedback for my company." `epic/reviews.json, Richard V.`, owner reply). Buyers read the replies.

## 10. Phrases to use and to avoid

**Use:** "a real person answers" (only if true for the business) · "we'll tell you if it's a part or the whole system" · "fix it if it can be fixed" · "we explain what we found before we touch your wallet" · "options, with prices, before we start" · "your mom/your tenant gets the same respect you would" · "we call you back" · "we leave it clean."

**Avoid (hard nos):**
- Invented stats ("95% fixed on the first visit"), prices ("$79 service call"), guarantees ("same-day guaranteed," "satisfaction guaranteed") or warranties ("lifetime warranty," "10-year parts and labor") unless that business's facts.md proves them.
- "24/7" or "emergency service" unless the business confirms it answers at night.
- Licenses, certifications, brand dealer status, awards or years in business not in facts.md.
- Dollar figures from reviews used as the company's price.
- Any claim about carbon monoxide, health or insurance outcomes.
- Corporate filler: "comfort solutions," "industry-leading," "premier," "best-in-class."
- Heat puns ("don't sweat it") in the hero. The reader is scared, not amused (inferred).

## 11. Tone and triggers

**Tone:** plain, calm, quick to the point, neighborly. Short sentences. Talk like the tech who explained it in the driveway, not like a sales office. Respect the reader's intelligence; don't explain refrigerant to them unless they ask.

**Triggers that land:** a named person who answers (reviews name techs constantly: Tyler, Sam, Nate, Mike, Preston, Tom); speed stated as a plain fact, not a promise; "fix vs. replace, your call"; family safety without fear-selling; the bill matching what you were told.

## 12. What's thin

- **Selection bias.** Only cooks, epic and rods have 1- to 3-star reviews in the pull. The other five are nearly all five-star, so fears come mostly from relief stories ("finally someone came") and three companies' complaints.
- **The "replace" fear is mainly second-opinion stories.** We hear from people who got a second opinion and were right, not from people who got one and still needed the new system (Kevin S., alpha: "with a burnt up condenser you have to break the bad news").
- **Money is vague.** Financing appears once (`epic/reviews.json, Courtney H.`). How buyers pay for a replacement isn't in the reviews.
- **Short-term-rental hosts:** one review (Hunter M.). Landlord voice is mostly praise, plus Brian B.'s one-star.
- **Furnace safety (gas smell, carbon monoxide) barely appears.** One smoke-smell visit (`epic/reviews.json, Chad S.`) and "possibly my grand sons lives" (`rods/reviews.json, Jerry T.`). Don't build a page on it without the owner.
- **Home warranty companies** come up several times (S C., Britiany P., Mark S., Rafael M.) but we don't know how each business handles them.
- **facts.md gaps:** aircapital and dreammakers facts.md still say reviews are pending, though `reviews.json` exists for both.
- **An owner call would sharpen:** what share of night calls end in a repair vs. a replacement quote; how they handle a customer who wants a second opinion; landlord and tenant terms (who pays, who's called); whether they work with home warranty companies; and whether the phone is really answered after hours.
