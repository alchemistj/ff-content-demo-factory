## 1. The buyers

**A. The owner (primary).** Runs a roofing, HVAC, window or exterior crew or a clinic in the Springfield area. Signs the agency invoice, answers the phone half the time, and judges the agency by whether the phone rang. Messaging: show me the calls and the booked jobs, in plain words.

**B. The office manager or dispatcher.** Sits in the CRM (ServiceTitan for SRA) and sees which calls were junk, which booked, and which "leads" were a VA pitch. Often the first to say "I don't think the agency's number is real." Messaging: your numbers will match my numbers. Evidence: SRA's site scheduler bookings match ServiceTitan exactly since Nov 7 (FF Reports).

**C. The referrer.** Another owner, a networking-event contact, a client's friend. They put their name on the pick. Evidence: "I can't imagine where I'd be if I hadn't randomly met him at a Springfield networking event." (fluidframemarketing.com, Annette R., The Biblical Nutritionist.) Messaging: easy to vouch for, no drama if it goes wrong, and Josh answers.

## 5. Top 5 fears, ranked by emotional intensity

1. **Paying an agency that can't show what I got.** Reports full of traffic and impressions, no calls tied to jobs. "Most marketing agencies give you reports" is how Ted's copy puts the pain; the real version is "I'm paying and I can't tell."
2. **Fake leads and spam, and numbers that are padded.** Real: 16 of 18 SRA submissions and 26 of 35 Luecke's submissions in the last 30 days looked like spam or pitches (Central Forms). Ads counted any form submit or a phone tap as a conversion (FF Reports).
3. **Being locked in.** A contract he can't leave, or a site and ad account he doesn't control. (inferred; nothing in INPUTS confirms how FF handles ownership or contracts, so FF can't answer this yet.)
4. **An agency that talks jargon, then goes quiet.** "Agencies that talk a good game but never deliver." (Ted's FF web copy.) He wants a person who picks up.
5. **A site that looks great and brings no calls.** Pieces that don't talk: site from one company, ads from another, a Google profile nobody touches (live site, "How We Work").

## 6. Top 5 objections (surface, then the fear underneath)

## 7. Top 5 wants

**Practical:** the number of real calls and booked jobs, in one place; a site that works on a phone and tells him when something breaks; junk filtered before it reaches his crew; one change at a time on his own site, with a note on what happened; one person to call.

**Emotional:** to stop feeling dumb about the report; to trust the number; to feel his marketing is "handled" without giving up control; to be able to say to a peer "my agency's straight with me."

## 10. Phrases to use and to avoid

**Use:** "real leads, not just traffic" · "we tie the call to the booked job" (only for clients with a CRM tie, like SRA) · "you see the same number we see" · "we filter the spam so your office doesn't" · "we test changes on your own site and keep what wins with real leads" · "one page, one change" · "you talk to the people doing the work" · "start with one piece" · "hit me up."

**Avoid (hard nos):**
- Invented results, stats, guarantees, awards, or years in business. Only numbers in INPUTS.md, with the client named (SRA: 1 to 60 Google clicks a month, Jul to Oct 2026, Search Console).
- "Our tests show X converts better." No split test has a proven winner. Luecke's scheduler test: about 150 visitors per side since Oct 21, no bookings yet.
- Claims about Window Dudes, HealthRev, A New You or Luecke's search growth. They are flat or down in Search Console in 2026 so far.
- "Our happiest clients" or any ranking of client satisfaction. No data.
- Client feedback quotes about spam filtering. None are recorded.
- Ted's unverified lines: cost ranges, "pay for themselves several times over," "over 50 websites," "hundreds of thousands of monthly visitors," "AI-ready," "Reliable Results."
- Agency-speak from `JOSH-VOICE.md` section 4 (synergy, leverage, ROI, KPI, scalable, solutions, elevate, unlock).
- Naming clients' tracking mistakes in anything public. The audit facts are in-house.

## 11. Tone and triggers

**Tone:** Josh's 2026 voice. Plain, fast, dry. A guy at the next table who tried it and tells you straight. Short sentences. Open with a question or the point. "Is your website bringing in calls? Be honest." Admit one thing that didn't work (the Luecke's headline test was too small to read). Close with one next step and a person.

**Triggers that land:** a number the office manager can check; a named client with a before and after (SRA); "we found a tracking number leaking into a Google snippet and fixed it"; "your junk goes to me, not your crew"; one small first step instead of a big contract; a real person (Josh, Charis) behind the work.

**Triggers that backfire:** "system" talk with no example; any claim that sounds like a guarantee; big adjectives; prices in the hero; naming other clients.
