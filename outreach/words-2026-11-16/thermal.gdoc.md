### When your air conditioner stops cooling

We fix air conditioning systems and service the components of most makes and models. Whether your system is malfunctioning or you want it ready for summer, we bring the expertise, guidance and equipment to improve its performance.

### Before you call
A few checks take only minutes and sometimes solve it. Set the thermostat to cool, a few degrees below the room temperature, and replace its batteries if the screen is blank. Then look at the breaker panel for a tripped switch, and at the filter. A clogged filter often restricts airflow enough that the coil ices over and cooling stops. If you find ice on the indoor coil or the refrigerant line, turn the system off and let it thaw.

If you reset a breaker and it trips again, leave it off and call us. In most cases that points to a real electrical fault. Please don't open the outdoor unit's panels either. Its capacitors can hold a charge after the power is off.
### What usually fails
Most no-cooling calls come from a short list of parts. A weak capacitor (the part that gives a motor its starting jolt) often keeps the compressor or fan from starting. A worn contactor (the switch that powers the outdoor unit) can leave the outdoor unit humming without running. A clogged condensate drain (the line that carries off water from the coil) can trip a safety switch that shuts the system off. A dirty outdoor coil usually makes the system run long and cool poorly. A refrigerant leak generally needs to be found and repaired before more refrigerant goes in.
### What a repair visit usually involves
Most diagnoses follow a similar order. The technician typically asks what the system did and when, checks the thermostat and the power supply, then tests the electrical parts and the refrigerant side. Starting there often finds the cause without taking anything else apart.
### Replacing an air conditioner
When your current A/C is old or can't be repaired, we provide a replacement. If you are building, we outfit your new home or building with an A/C system customized to your construction plans.

Repair or replace is a judgment about the whole system, not the part alone. In most cases a capacitor, contactor or fan motor is worth repairing at any age. A failed compressor or a leaking coil on an older unit is harder to justify. Systems built before about 2010 often use R-22 refrigerant, which is no longer produced, so even a leak repair on one can cost a large share of a new system.

### Air conditioning questions
How long should an air conditioner last?

Often 12 to 15 years, and sometimes less if it has never been serviced. Past that age, a major repair is worth weighing against replacement.

Is my A/C leaking refrigerant?

Delayed or longer cooling cycles and ice on your evaporator coils can be signs of a leak. If you think yours is leaking, we'll inspect it.

What should I do with my A/C in winter?

You don't need to do much preventive work. To keep it in good condition, put a tarp over it in fall and winter to keep snow and leaves out. Take the tarp off before you turn the condenser on again. If the tarp stays on while the air conditioning runs, the unit can overheat and be damaged.

Why does the house feel clammy when the A/C is running?

Our summers are humid, and an air conditioner removes moisture as well as heat. A system that is oversized, or that switches off before it has run long enough, can leave the air cool but damp. That is worth a call.

Many furnaces flash a code on the control board that points toward the failed part, so a technician will usually read that code first.
### Before you call
If the furnace won't start, check three things. Make sure the thermostat is set to heat, above the room temperature, and replace its batteries if it has them. Check the breaker and the switch on or near the furnace. It looks like a light switch and often gets turned off by accident. Then look at the filter, because a packed one can make a furnace overheat and shut itself off.

If the furnace starts and then stops again, call us. Resetting it over and over rarely fixes the cause.
### Gas smell and carbon monoxide
If you smell gas, don't switch anything on or off, lights included. Leave the house, then call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

Gas and propane furnaces can produce carbon monoxide when something is wrong, and you can't smell it. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call 911 first. Call us after the house is cleared.
### What usually fails
On gas furnaces, a dirty flame sensor (the small rod that confirms the flame) or a worn igniter often keeps the burners from staying lit. A failing inducer (the fan that vents exhaust) or blower motor commonly stops a furnace from starting or running. In electric furnaces, a burned-out heating element or a failed relay is a frequent cause. Parts like these are usually repairable once the technician has found them.

### Heating questions
My system makes noise. Is that normal?

HVAC motors hum, and you'll hear air moving through the vents and ductwork. Those sounds are usually nothing to worry about. Unusual rattling or hissing is a reason to schedule an inspection.

Is it normal for a boiler to make noise?

A boiler may pop or click as it warms your water. Loud noises such as banging or booming usually mean something is wrong and are worth an inspection.

When should I change my furnace filter?

Check your filter monthly and replace it when it looks packed with dust.
