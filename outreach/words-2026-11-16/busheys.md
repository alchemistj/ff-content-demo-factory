### Why we sell one window

Infinity by Marvin is the only replacement window we sell. It is built from Ultrex, Marvin's own fiberglass material, which Marvin says has eight times the strength of vinyl. The reason we picked fiberglass has to do with the weather. Fort Wayne often runs from near zero in January to the 90s in July, and any window frame expands and contracts with that swing. Vinyl can move enough over the years to loosen the seal around the glass, which lets in drafts or moisture. Fiberglass holds its shape better, so the seal stays tight, and Marvin builds Infinity to resist warping, fading and failing.

### The inside of your window

The inside is the part you see every day. EverWood is Infinity's interior finish: an engineered wood grain that looks and feels like wood without the sanding, staining and swelling that real wood asks for. You can stain it or paint it. Marvin says it won't warp, swell or rot. Real wood windows tend to need refinishing every few years, and the bottom ledge, where condensation and rain collect, is usually where wood goes first.

### What is between the panes

Most of a window's efficiency comes from the glass. Low-E glass has a thin, nearly invisible metal coating that reflects heat and filters out ultraviolet light, so the house stays warmer in winter and cooler in summer. Argon is a colorless, odorless gas sealed between the panes in place of air. It is denser than air, so it slows the heat moving through.

Two numbers let you compare windows. The U-value measures how fast heat passes through, and a lower number means a better window. The solar heat gain coefficient, written SHGC, measures how much of the sun's heat gets in, and a lower number means a cooler house in summer. You may also see an R-value, but for windows the U-value and SHGC are the more reliable pair. When you compare quotes, compare these numbers on windows of the same size. Windows with the ENERGY STAR label meet guidelines from the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency and Department of Energy, and your consultant can tell you which options carry it.

### Insert or full-frame

We offer both, and the choice is usually settled in your consultation. An insert fits a new window into your existing frame. It keeps your interior trim and usually takes less time, but it works only if that frame is sound. A full-frame replacement takes everything out down to the bare opening and puts in new framing, trim and window.

A full-frame job usually makes sense when the old frame has rot or water damage, when it has dropped out of square, or when you want to change the size of the opening. If wood at the bottom ledge feels soft or the paint under a window keeps peeling, the frame may be the problem and not only the glass.

### Sweating or fogged: two different problems

Moisture on the inside of the glass in winter is usually humidity from showers, cooking or a humidifier meeting cold glass. It is common in Indiana winters and doesn't mean the window has failed. Haze or droplets between the two panes are different. That usually means the seal has failed and the insulating gas has leaked out, and no cleaning will fix it. A few fogged windows don't automatically mean the whole house needs new ones, so the condition of the frame matters as much as the glass. A free consultation is how we help you sort out which one you have.

### Measured for your house

Windows are made to order, so the measurements matter. After you choose, we come back and measure every opening, allowing for frames that aren't perfectly square, which in an older house they often aren't. A window made a fraction too small leaves a gap you will feel as a draft, so accuracy here saves trouble later. Made-to-order windows often take several weeks to arrive. We give you a timeline, updates along the way and confirmed installation dates.

### Why we chose ProVia

A front door is the first thing anyone sees of your house, and it also has to keep out weather and intruders, so we help you balance looks, security and budget.

### Fiberglass or steel

Most entry doors are either fiberglass or steel, and each has strengths. Fiberglass can look like real wood grain, and it usually resists dents and doesn't swell with moisture the way wood can. Steel is generally strong and good for security, though a deep scratch that exposes bare metal can eventually rust if it isn't touched up. We sell both, and your consultant will help you find the right fit for your home and budget.

### Signet fiberglass doors

Signet has the most door styles and sidelite options in ProVia's line. A sidelite is a narrow glass panel beside the door, and it is a common way to bring daylight into a dark entry. Signet comes in a smooth finish, or in woodgrain finishes of Cherry, Mahogany, Oak, Knotty Alder or Fir. ProVia calls it structurally superior to traditional fiberglass doors, and it carries a 15-year finish warranty.

### Ascent fiberglass doors

Ascent is the line for doorways that aren't a standard size. Widths and heights are custom in 1/8-inch steps, which can help in an older house where the opening is a little off. It has a White Oak woodgrain or a smooth fiberglass finish in a range of stains and colors, and styles including Inscribed doors and doors with Panel Planks. It carries a 10-year finish warranty.

Legacy is made with 20-gauge steel, and gauge measures thickness, with a lower number meaning thicker steel. You can give the two sides of a Legacy door different styles or finishes, so the outside can match your house while the inside matches your hall.

### Before you choose a storm door

A storm door on a door that faces the afternoon sun can trap heat between the two doors. Some entry doors, especially dark ones, can be damaged by that heat over time, so it is worth asking about the glass and your door's finish before you buy. A storm door also needs a frame that's square and sound. If the casing, the trim around the door, is worn, it is often replaced along with the door.
