### When the air conditioner stops on a hot day

Air conditioners often fail when they are working hardest, on the first run of 90-degree days. Fort Wayne summers are humid as well as hot, so a house without cooling can turn uncomfortable within a few hours. When you call, tell us what the unit is doing: whether the fan outside is running, whether the air from the vents is warm or only weak, and whether there is ice or water anywhere. Those details often narrow the cause before anyone drives out.

### Before you call

Three quick checks are worth a minute. Set the thermostat to cool and a few degrees below the room temperature, and replace its batteries if it has them. Look at the breaker. Then hold the air filter up to a light, and if you can’t see through it, change it, because a clogged filter restricts airflow and can ice up the indoor coil. If ice has formed on the pipes or the unit, turn the system off and call. Running a frozen system can damage the compressor, the main pump in the cooling system.

### What usually fails on an air conditioner

A handful of parts cause many no-cooling calls:

  - **The capacitor,** a part that gives the motors a jolt to start, can weaken. A common result is an outdoor unit that hums but doesn’t start.
  - **The contactor,** the switch that sends power to the outdoor unit, can wear out.
  - **The fan motors,** outside and indoors, can fail.
  - **The refrigerant level** can drop. Refrigerant isn’t used up like fuel, so a low level usually points to a leak, and a recharge alone may not last.
  - **The condensate drain,** the line that carries away the water the cooling coil collects, can clog. In a humid summer that water can overflow or shut the system off.

> — Mark S.

### Older air conditioners and refrigerant

Many air conditioners installed before the mid-2000s use a refrigerant called R-22. Its production ended in 2020, and the supply that remains is often much more expensive. When an older system with R-22 leaks, that cost can make repair or replacement a closer call than the part itself would suggest. Ask what the refrigerant is before you decide.

### When the furnace stops in a Fort Wayne winter

Fort Wayne winters can bring overnight lows in the teens or single digits, and furnaces tend to stop after days of nearly continuous running. If the heat stays off, pipes along outside walls can freeze, so it is worth calling early instead of waiting to see whether the furnace restarts. Tell us whether it runs at all, whether the air is warm or cold, any noise, and any number or light flashing on the control board, the circuit board that runs the sequence, inside the cabinet.

On a gas furnace, a few parts cause many no-heat calls:

  - **The igniter,** which lights the burners, can wear out.
  - **The flame sensor,** a thin rod that confirms there is a flame, can get coated, so the burners shut off after a few seconds.
  - **The inducer motor,** a small fan that clears exhaust, and the **blower motor,** which pushes warm air through the ducts, can fail.
  - **The control board** can fail. Many furnaces flash a numbered code, and the meaning of a code differs by brand.
  - **The limit switch,** a safety switch that shuts off the burners when the furnace overheats, often trips because of a clogged filter.

### Repair or replace?

Age alone doesn’t decide it. A furnace commonly lasts 15 to 20 years and an air conditioner 12 to 15, and an older unit can still be worth repairing when what failed is an igniter, a motor or a control board. A rule of thumb says replacement makes more sense when a repair would cost about half the price of a new unit and the unit is already near the end of its life. Repeated repairs in one season point the same way.

One failure usually settles the question, a cracked heat exchanger. That is the metal chamber where the burner’s heat passes to the air your house breathes, and a crack can let combustion gases mix with it. A technician who finds one will usually recommend replacement, and you should not run the furnace until it is addressed.

### Gas smell and carbon monoxide

If you smell gas, don’t touch a switch, lights included. Go outside and call your gas utility or 911 from there. Gas furnaces can produce carbon monoxide when something is wrong. You can’t smell it, and the only warning is an alarm. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone out of the house and call 911 first. Call us once the house is clear.

### The equipment attached to the system

Parts that sit beside the furnace can be the real problem while the furnace itself runs. A whole-house humidifier can clog with mineral deposits, which are common where water is hard, as it often is in northeast Indiana. A boiler that has lost water pressure can leave radiators cold, and the pressure gauge reading near zero is worth a call before anyone adds water. A thermostat that has drifted out of calibration can make a healthy system look faulty. An electronic air cleaner has cells that need regular washing to keep working. A mini split’s indoor unit has filters and a drain that collect dirt, and a unit that drips or smells is often asking for cleaning.

An air conditioner check usually covers cleaning the coils, tightening electrical connections and checking the refrigerant level. ### When the hot water stops

A water heater that leaks is a different problem from one that simply stops heating, and the two are handled differently. If you find water around the base, close the cold water valve on the pipe above the heater, turn off its power or gas, and call. A leak from a fitting or a relief valve, a safety valve that opens if the temperature or pressure gets too high, can often be repaired. A leak from the steel tank itself usually means replacement, because a tank can’t be patched in a way that lasts. In a basement, a floor drain may take the water. In a garage or utility closet, it can spread quickly.

> My water heater died and water was all over the garage. Mike was able to come out quickly and install a new one for me. He was very knowledgeable and professional. I will use Borchelts again and will recommend them to others.

> — Jennifer B.

### What usually fails

When the heater is still intact but the water is cold or lukewarm, the cause is often a small part.

  - **On a gas heater,** the pilot flame or the thermocouple, the small sensor that proves the flame is lit, can fail. The gas control valve, which regulates the burner, can also wear out.
  - **On an electric heater,** a heating element or a thermostat inside the tank can burn out, and a tripped breaker can be the whole story. Many electric heaters have two elements, so one can fail while the other keeps the water warm.
  - **On either type,** the relief valve can wear out or drip. A drain valve can leak too.

Not every call ends in replacement, and many are simple. One customer described it:

> It was a simple fix on our water heater. The technician explained everything ,and showed me a couple of tips. We are very satisfied with the service.

> — David T.

### Noises and hard water

Rumbling or popping from a tank is often sediment. Water in much of Indiana is hard, which means it carries dissolved minerals, and well water outside the city often carries more. Over the years those minerals settle at the bottom of the tank. The heater then takes longer to heat, uses more fuel or electricity, and the layer can make the tank noisy. Manufacturers generally recommend draining some water from the tank each year to clear it, and a tank that has never been drained is often harder to clear later. Inside the tank, a metal rod called the anode rod is made to corrode instead of the tank. When it is used up, the tank itself starts to corrode, which is one reason heaters in hard-water homes tend to wear out sooner.

### How long a tank lasts, and when to swap it

A tank water heater commonly lasts around 8 to 12 years, and a tankless one often lasts longer. The age on the label decides less than what has failed. A new element, thermostat or valve can make sense on a heater that is otherwise sound. A heater near the end of its range that has begun leaking from the tank, is usually better replaced. Replacing before the tank fails also lets you choose the day, instead of finding out on a holiday weekend.

Sometimes a heater can’t be repaired and has to be replaced. One customer’s timeline shows how that went:

> I called Tuesday because our water heater stopped working on Labor Day they came in on Wednesday and unfortunately it couldn’t be fixed, came back today and replaced it. Very polite gentlemen, very professional, answered all my husband’s questions and made sure everything was running properly before leaving. I recommend them to everyone and would use them again and have used them in the past.

> — Karen Z.

### What a replacement involves

A replacement generally means shutting off the water and the power or gas, draining the old tank, disconnecting it and carrying it out, then setting and connecting the new one. A gas heater needs its venting checked and its gas connection tested. A new heater is generally filled, with the air let out of the lines, and the relief valve is checked. Many homes also need an expansion tank, a small tank that absorbs the pressure that builds as water heats, particularly when the home’s plumbing has a one-way valve at the meter. We also do gas lines.

Old shutoff valves are a risk in any replacement. A valve that hasn’t been turned in many years can corrode, and it sometimes breaks the moment it is turned. A house of 30 years or more is more likely to have one. When that happens, the water has to be stopped farther back, and a new valve goes in.

> RJ replaced our water heater. Exceptional customer service. Our water main valve broke while replacing, which is probably common on a 30 year old home. He handled the situation, called City Utilities to shut off at the curb, and had us back in business with a new water heater and shut off valve within 3 hours. Thank you\!

### Gas heaters, venting and safety

A gas water heater vents its exhaust through a flue, the vent pipe above it, and a flue that is blocked, loose or poorly sloped can send exhaust gas, including carbon monoxide, into the house. After any replacement, the venting is worth checking, and a carbon monoxide alarm near the heater and the bedrooms is worth having.

Temperature is a safety matter too. Many manufacturers suggest setting the thermostat around 120 degrees to reduce the risk of scalds, which matters most with children or older family members at home.

### Tank or tankless

A tank heater stores hot water and reheats it when the tank cools. A tankless heater heats water as it flows, so it doesn’t run out the way a tank can, but it can only heat so much at once. The water coming into a Fort Wayne house is usually much colder in January than in July, so a tankless unit has to be sized for winter, and running a shower and a dishwasher together can be too much for a small one.

Tankless also changes the house. Many models need a larger gas line and special venting, and an electrical connection for the fan, so the installed cost often runs higher than for a tank. In hard-water homes, tankless units generally need to be flushed to clear mineral scale, or the water flow slows over time.
