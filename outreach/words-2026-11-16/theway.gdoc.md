### What Goes Into a Replacement
A full replacement means the old roof comes off completely, down to the wood. We don't lay new shingles over old ones. Taking everything off lets us look at the wood underneath before anything new goes on, and that wood is where most surprises are.

Then we put on the new roof in layers, from the bottom up: new waterproof layers, shingles, metal strips where the roof meets chimneys and walls, and airflow along the peak. Each replacement covers everything from the wood to the peak.
### What We Do About the Wood Under Your Old Roof
Once the old roof is off, we inspect the wood under the shingles, which roofers call decking, looking for soft spots, water damage and nails that have let go. On older Evansville homes, that wood is often individual boards, not sheets of plywood, and boards that have been wet at some point can rot. Damaged sections are replaced before installation begins.

This is the part that can change a price once the roof is open.

### The Waterproof Layers You Never See
Under the shingles, a waterproof layer covers the whole roof. We add a second, sticky waterproof layer at the roof's edges, where two slopes meet, and around chimneys and vents, since those are the places water is most likely to back up under the shingles. A roof with good shingles and a skipped layer underneath can still leak at its weakest spot, and you wouldn't see it until the ceiling stained.
### The Shingles: One Maker, Three Levels
We install GAF Timberline shingles, the most-installed shingle line in North America. There are three levels:

- **A premium level** with a thicker body and a deeper shadow line, for the look of a heavier roof.
- **The everyday level**, which is the one most homes get. It's built for wind up to 130 mph and comes in more than 30 colors.
- **A standard level**, priced to give good performance at a lower cost.

Ask to see each level on a sample before you choose, so you pick for your house and your budget.
### Metal Strips and Attic Air
We inspect the metal strips sealed around chimneys, skylights and walls and replace them as needed. A strip that held up under the old roof can wear out years before the new one does, and replacing it now costs far less than opening the new roof later. We also confirm that air can move along the peak of the roof, and correct it if it can't.

### The Stain Is Rarely Under the Hole
Water doesn't stay where it enters. It runs along the wood and the attic insulation and drips off somewhere else, sometimes several feet from the opening. A repair at the stain fixes nothing, and the leak comes back with the next rain. That's why we start with a real diagnosis, not a patch.

We check the roof surface, the metal strips sealed around everything that comes through the roof, and the attic for signs of where the water traveled. We want to know where it's getting in, not where it's landing.

### What We Repair, and Why It Fails
- **Active leaks.** We find the entry point, check the wood underneath for damage and make the repair. You get photos of what we found and what we did.
- **Missing and damaged shingles.** Wind, impact and age loosen or remove shingles, exposing the layers under them. We match the new shingles as closely as we can and seal them down.
- **The metal strips around chimneys, skylights, dormers and walls.** These strips, called flashing, are among the most common leak sources, since a strip that lifts, corrodes or separates lets water in behind it. We remove, reset and reseal them.
- **The shingles along the peak.** They take the most weather and often fail before the rest of the roof does. We replace worn or missing ones and reseal the peak.
- **Rubber collars around vent pipes.** The rubber cracks and separates over time, especially through temperature swings. We replace failed collars and reseal every opening, including vents and gas lines.
- **Where two slopes meet, and the roof edge.** When the metal there fails, water can run behind the gutters or through the seam into the wood. We repair or replace those sections when they're letting water in.

Repaired my ridge vent with new shingles quickly and gave me before and after pictures! Could not be happier!

### Where Older Evansville Roofs Usually Leak
On older houses, leaks usually start at the places where something interrupts the shingles: a chimney, a skylight, a vent pipe or a wall. Years of sun, heat and winter freezing wear out the sealant and the rubber long before the shingles themselves wear out. A roof that's otherwise sound can leak at one of these spots, and a repair there often costs far less than a new roof.

Hail is different. It bruises shingles across a whole slope, and a repair rarely fixes that. If a storm caused what you're seeing, take photos before anyone touches it. We photograph the problem and the fix on every repair anyway, so you have a record of what was done.
### Repair or Replace? How We Decide
A repair that buys five more years can be the right call. A repair that only delays a replacement isn't.

A repair usually makes sense when:

- The damage is in one area and the rest of the roof is in good shape
- The roof is under about 15 years old
- The wood beneath the damaged spot is sound
- The failure is one specific part, like a vent collar, a metal strip or a section of shingles

Replacement usually makes more sense when:

- The roof is 20 or more years old and the shingles are losing their gritty surface coating, which you'll often see collecting in the gutters
- Several areas leak or show wear
- The wood is significantly damaged or rotted
- Another layer of shingles would go past the limit for layers on one roof
- Repairs keep adding up toward the price of a new roof
### What Happens When You Call About a Leak

**Can you fix just part of my roof?** Often, yes. When the damage is in one area and the rest of the roof is sound, a repair is usually the sensible choice.
