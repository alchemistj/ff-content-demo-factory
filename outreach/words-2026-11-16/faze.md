### Repair or Replace: What Decides It

A missing shingle or a ceiling stain doesn't automatically mean a new roof. The question is whether the trouble is in one spot or spread across the roof. A few blown-off shingles or a single leak is often a repair. Cracking or curling across most of the roof, or leaks that come back after patches, usually points to replacement. Age matters too, since asphalt shingles are often near the end of their life at 20 to 25 years, though dark streaks alone are usually only cosmetic.

A ceiling stain shows where water arrived, not always where it got in, because water can travel along the wood before it drips. That's why it helps to have a roofer connect three things: what's damaged, what probably caused it, and what fix is proposed. If the damage is in one spot and the proposal covers the whole roof, ask why.

### What Comes Off, and What's Under It

Replacing a roof starts with the tear-off, which is removing the old roofing, and then a look at the decking under it. The decking is the wood surface the whole roof is nailed to. If it's rotted, soft or water-stained, it's usually replaced before new roofing goes on, because nails need solid wood to hold. Indiana homes can generally have no more than two layers of roofing, so a roof that already has two usually comes off down to the decking.

Some damage can't be seen until the old roof is off. Before work starts, it helps to agree on what happens if rotted decking turns up: how it's documented, how the extra work is priced, and when you're asked to approve it.

### What Goes Between the Wood and the Shingles

Shingles are only the top layer. Under them, a roof usually has several parts that each keep water moving down and off the house. A metal strip called the gutter apron sits at the eaves, the lower edge of the roof where the gutters hang, and guides water into the gutter.

A sticky waterproof membrane called ice and water barrier usually goes along the eaves and in the valleys, the low lines where two roof slopes meet. We commonly lay two rows at the eaves. Fort Wayne's freeze-and-thaw winters are the reason it matters. Over the wood goes a synthetic underlayment, a second waterproof layer that has largely replaced felt paper. It's held down with cap nails or cap staples, because ordinary staples can tear it. Flashing, the metal pieces that seal where the roof meets a chimney, a wall or a vent pipe, is where leaks often start, so ask how each one will be handled.

### Ice, Snow and Your Attic

If ice builds up along the roof edge in winter, the cause may be warm air leaking into the attic, thin insulation or poor ventilation. Replacing shingles alone may not fix that, so it's fair to ask a roofer to look at the attic as well as the roof. A new roof is a good moment to check that air can move through the attic the way it should.

### Shingles, Metal and Other Roof Types

Asphalt shingles are the most common roof on houses, and we install them in architectural styles, which are thicker and layered. We also install metal, tile, slate, flat, low-slope and wood-shake roofs. Metal roofs are often built to last 50 years or more, and a natural slate roof can last a century.

Hail is a regular part of Indiana storms, so impact-rated shingles are worth asking about. Class 4 is the highest hail rating, and some companies discount premiums for it, so ask yours.

### Repairs After a Storm

Not every storm damage job is a replacement. Blown-off shingles, worn flashing and weak spots around vent pipes can let water into the house if they're left alone, and a small leak can turn into a big one. Repairs find the affected area and fix what caused it, and they often stop the damage from spreading into the wood beneath.

### What an Estimate Should Spell Out

Two estimates can cover different amounts of work, so compare what each one includes, not just the total. A replacement estimate should name the roofing material, say how many layers of old roofing come off, and describe the ice barrier, underlayment and flashing work. It should also explain how hidden decking damage is handled and who handles cleanup and permits. Allen County has said re-roofing can need a permit, so ask who confirms it for your address.

### Where Siding Goes Wrong

Water does most of the damage. Once it gets behind the panels, it can rot the wood under them and show up inside as stains on a wall or peeling wallpaper. Cracks, holes and missing pieces let it in, and so can panels that bubble out or pull away from the house.

Vinyl has its own problems. Sun reflected from windows, a grill or a fire pit can soften vinyl until it warps, and warped vinyl usually can't be repaired, only replaced. Manufacturer warranties often don't cover heat damage, so it's worth reading yours. Lower-grade vinyl can also chalk, leaving a white powder on the surface as the filler in it works its way out. Wood siding invites pests as well as rot.

### Repair or Replace

Repair makes sense when the damage is in a small area, the siding is under about 10 years old, the wall behind it is dry and the problem is only on the surface. Replacement makes sense when damage is widespread, the siding is 20 to 30 years old and showing its age, energy bills have jumped, or some rooms never hold their temperature.

### Taking the Old Siding Off and Putting New On

The work starts with removing the old siding down to the wall. With the wall bare, the sheets of wood the siding fastens to can be checked, and any that are water-damaged can be replaced. A waterproof sheet called house wrap goes over the wall. Tape goes around each window opening, because windows are one of the most common places for siding to fail. The tape is layered so water is directed out, not trapped behind the siding.

Starter strips, corner pieces and trim go on before the panels, and panel seams are staggered so they don't line up.

### Four Siding Materials

Vinyl is our most popular option. It comes in a wide range of colors and textures and needs little more than an occasional rinse with a hose. Fiber cement is a premium choice that looks like wood and resists fire, rot and pests. Composite siding gives you the look of wood and is engineered to resist warping, splitting and insects. Metal siding is built to last and has a sleek, modern look.

For Indiana weather, we recommend fiber cement or vinyl, because both handle temperature swings and moisture well. Vinyl expands and contracts as the temperature changes, so it shouldn't be fastened tight against the wall. Insulated vinyl, which has foam backing, helps reduce heat loss through the walls.

### The House With Metal Studs

On one recent job near Fort Wayne, we took the old siding off about 23 squares of wall, and a square is 100 square feet, so roughly 2,300 square feet. Behind it we found a house framed with metal studs instead of wood. Siding nails don't hold reliably in thin metal, so we changed the fastening system to screws, about two inches long, set at every stud, which were 24 inches apart.

By the end of the job, that came to roughly 1,600 screws. The panels still had to move with the weather, so the screws went in the fastening slots without being driven tight. The old windows had nail flanges tied into metal pieces around the openings, and those had to be cut and ground out by hand before the new windows could go in.

### Siding on Older Homes

Asbestos siding is still found on many older Fort Wayne homes. When it's intact, it's often best left alone. The trouble starts when it's cracked, drilled, cut, sanded or broken, because that can put fibers in the air. It can look like fiber cement, so only a sample tested by a qualified lab can confirm it. Don't break off a piece to test it yourself. If your siding might be asbestos, get it tested before anyone tears it off.

### Colors, Trim and What's in Stock

Less common colors and trim pieces can take longer to get than standard ones. If you've chosen a dark or designer color, board-and-batten accents, which are vertical boards with narrow strips over the seams, or shake accents, it's worth confirming that every piece is available before anything comes off the house.

### Questions About Siding

**Can new siding go over my old siding?** Sometimes it's possible, but it's usually not the best approach, because new siding over old can cover up damage and moisture problems. We recommend taking the old siding off first.

**Do I need new insulation with new siding?** With the wall open, it's a good time to check the insulation for damage, moisture or thin coverage. If it's in good shape it can stay, and many homeowners choose to upgrade it while the walls are exposed.

**Will new siding lower my energy bills?** Some customers tell us they notice a difference, mostly in the hottest and coldest months. How much depends on the siding, the insulation behind it and the house itself.
