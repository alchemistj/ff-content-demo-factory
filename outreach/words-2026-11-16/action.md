### Furnace Problems and What Usually Causes Them

**It clicks but never lights.** The cause is often a worn igniter, the part that starts the flame, or a dirty flame sensor, the part that confirms the flame is burning. Both are small, and the repair is usually short once the technician knows which one it is.

**It lights, then shuts off in a few minutes.** A clogged filter can make a furnace overheat and shut itself down. A flame sensor that can't prove the flame does the same. Airflow is usually the first thing to check.

**It runs, but the house won't warm.** A weak blower motor, a restricted filter or a thermostat set wrong can all do it. A fan setting left on "on" blows cool air between heating cycles, so check that it's on "auto" first.

**A loud bang, a rumble or a squeal.** A bang can come from delayed ignition, and a squeal from a worn motor or bearing. A bang or grinding is the time to shut it off and call.

**A cracked heat exchanger.** This is the metal part that keeps burning gases apart from the air you breathe. A crack is a safety problem, not a small repair, and the technician should tell you plainly.

### Air Conditioner Problems and What Usually Causes Them

**It hums, but the fan won't start.** A weak capacitor is a common cause. It's the part that gives the motor a jolt to start, and replacing it is usually a short repair.

**Warm air from the vents, or ice on the pipe outside the house.** Poor airflow, such as a clogged filter, can let the indoor coil freeze. If airflow is fine, low refrigerant is the next suspect. An air conditioner doesn't use refrigerant up, so low usually means a leak that needs finding.

**Water around the indoor unit.** A clogged drain line is the usual cause, and clearing it stops the leak.

**Some rooms cool and others don't, or it turns on and off all day.** Ducts can be the cause. So can a system that's the wrong size for the house, and a technician can help you work out whether it needs replacing.

### Repair or Replace a Water Heater?

A tank water heater usually lasts somewhere around a decade or a bit more. A part such as a heating element or a gas control can often be repaired, and that's the smaller bill. A leak from the tank itself usually means replacement, because the tank can't be patched.

Age is the other half of the answer. If the unit is old and it needs one repair after another, put the money toward a new one. If it's fairly young and the problem is a single part, repair. We'll tell you which, and what each costs, before work starts.

### Gas or Electric

A gas water heater has a burner, a pilot or igniter, and a vent that carries exhaust outside. An electric one heats with elements inside the tank and has no flame. That changes what usually goes wrong. Gas units tend to fail at the pilot or the gas control, and electric units tend to fail at an element or a thermostat.

Both have a pressure relief valve, a safety valve that opens if the pressure or temperature gets too high. A valve that drips, or a pipe running from it that's wet, tells a technician something is off. Leave that valve and the gas and wiring parts to us.

### Tank or Tankless

A tank unit stores hot water and keeps it warm. A tankless unit heats water as you use it, so it doesn't run out in the middle of a long shower and it uses space more sparingly. Tankless usually costs more up front, and a tank unit costs less.

The right choice depends on how much hot water your household uses, how much room you have, and your budget. A gas tankless unit often needs bigger gas and venting connections than a tank unit does, so we look at what's already in your house before recommending one.

### A Leaking Water Heater: What to Do First

Turn off the cold water supply to the heater, which is usually a valve on the pipe above it. For an electric unit, switch off its breaker. For a gas unit, don't touch the gas parts, and leave the building if you smell gas. If you're not sure where the main water shut-off is, tell us when you call. Our technicians usually take the time to show you where water enters your home, so the next time takes thirty seconds.

### Rusty Water, Noise and Slow Hot Water

**Rusty water.** If only the hot water is rusty, the tank is often corroding inside, and that points toward replacement. If the cold water is rusty too, old pipes may be the cause.

**Rumbling or popping.** It's worth having a technician look, because it can come from the tank or from a part that is wearing out.

**Hot water that runs out fast.** A failed element or a thermostat problem can cut the supply. So can a unit that's too small for the household, which is a sizing question when you replace it.

### What to Check Before You Call

Look at the breaker for an electric unit, and make sure the gas valve is open on a gas one. Check whether it's only one faucet, since a clogged faucet screen can look like a water heater problem. If those don't help, call and describe what it's doing.
