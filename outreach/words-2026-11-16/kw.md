### Furnace Problems and What Usually Causes Them

**It clicks but never lights.** Often a worn igniter or a dirty flame sensor. Both are small parts.

**It lights, then shuts off in a few minutes.** A clogged filter can make a furnace overheat and shut itself down for safety. A flame sensor that can't prove the flame is the other usual cause.

**It's blowing cold air.** Possibly a dirty filter, a thermostat problem, or the overheating shutdown above. If a new filter doesn't change it, it needs a diagnostic.

**It runs but the house won't warm.** A weak blower motor, a restricted filter, or ductwork that is losing heat before it reaches the rooms.

**A loud bang, rumble or squeal.** A bang can be delayed ignition, and a squeal is often a worn motor or blower bearing. Fixing either early is usually the cheaper time.

### Gas Smell or Carbon Monoxide Alarm: What to Do First

A gas furnace burns fuel, so a few problems are not wait-and-see. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone out and call 911 before you call us.

Once it's safe, a technician can check the parts that keep combustion gases out of your living space, and tell you plainly what they find. Never heat the house with a gas oven or stove, because that puts carbon monoxide in the air.

### Before You Call

Four quick checks can save a trip: the thermostat is set to heat and above the room temperature, the furnace switch is on, the breaker isn't tripped, and the filter isn't packed with dust. Leave everything inside the furnace to a technician, because it's gas and high-voltage work.

### Repair or Replace Your Furnace?

If the problem is an igniter, a sensor, a blower motor or a thermostat, repair is usually the smaller bill and the furnace keeps going. If the furnace is over 10 years old, breaks down often, or your energy bills keep rising, it may be time to consider a replacement. Gas furnaces commonly last 15 to 20 years, so a 12-year-old furnace with one cheap repair is usually not a replacement call.

A cracked heat exchanger, the part that separates combustion gases from the air your family breathes, is the one repair that usually ends the conversation. We inspect the furnace and tell you which option costs less over time.

### Replacing a Furnace

### A Fall Tune-Up Before the Cold

A seasonal tune-up for a furnace or heat pump means we clean, inspect and test the system. We recommend one at least once a year, ideally in the fall before the heating season begins. It helps prevent breakdowns, can extend the life of the furnace and keeps it running efficiently.

A tune-up is also when the small parts get a look: the filter, the igniter and flame sensor, the blower, the thermostat, and the safety shutoffs that stop the furnace if something goes wrong. A part that is wearing out is far cheaper to replace on a calm October afternoon than on the first freezing night.

### AC Problems and What Usually Causes Them

**It hums but the fan won't start.** Often a weak capacitor, the small part that gives the fan and compressor the jolt they need to start. It's usually a short repair.

**Warm air from the vents, or ice on the copper line.** Blocked airflow can let the coil freeze, and low refrigerant is the next suspect. Dirty filters and airflow restrictions can cause the same symptom.

**Unusual sounds.** A noisy or slow fan or compressor motor is a warning. Replacing a motor early is usually a smaller job than waiting for it to fail.

**Running longer, or a higher energy bill.** The unit is working harder than it should, often from a dirty coil, a clogged filter or a failing part.

### Low Refrigerant Usually Means a Leak

An air conditioner doesn't use up refrigerant, so a unit that is low has usually leaked. Adding more without finding the leak means it escapes again and you pay twice. Handling refrigerant takes certification and the right equipment, which is one reason to leave it to a technician.

### Water Around the Indoor Unit and the Humid Summer

Water near the indoor unit usually points to a clogged drain line, a frozen coil on the indoor unit or a damaged drain pan. Southern Indiana summers are humid, so an air conditioner pulls a lot of water out of the air, and the drain line carries it away. A clog can back water up into the house, so don't ignore moisture around the unit. Wiping it up and waiting rarely works, because the drain stays clogged and the water comes back.

### Before You Call

Set the thermostat to cool, a few degrees below the room temperature, and check that the outdoor unit's breaker hasn't tripped. If you see ice, hear grinding, smell something burning or the outdoor fan isn't spinning, switch the cooling off and call. Leave the electrical panel on the outdoor unit alone, because it is dangerous to open.

### Repair or Replace Your Air Conditioner?

If it's a capacitor, a motor or a cleaned coil, repair is usually cheaper than a new system and can put off a replacement for years. If the unit is over 10 years old, breaking down frequently or your energy bills keep rising, it may be time to look at replacing it. Central air conditioners commonly last 10 to 15 years.

Older units that use a refrigerant called R-22 are a special case. It was phased out in 2020, so it's scarce and costly to top up, and a leak on an old unit often tips the decision toward replacement. We'll inspect the system and tell you which option costs less over time.

### Replacing an Air Conditioner

When it's time for a new system, we help you choose a high-efficiency air conditioner for your space and budget, then handle the full installation from start to finish. The result should be quieter operation, steadier comfort and lower utility bills.

Size matters more than most people expect. In a humid climate, a system that is too large cools the air quickly and shuts off before it removes much moisture, leaving the house cool and clammy. A replacement is usually sized to the home's cooling load.

### Indoor Air Quality

We install air purifiers, UV lights and humidity control to reduce allergens and improve the air you breathe. Humidity is the one that matters most here, because a damp house in an Evansville summer can feel muggy even when the air conditioner is running, and humidity control works on that directly. Indoor humidity is usually comfortable somewhere between 30 and 50 percent, and much higher than that tends to encourage mold and dust mites. Air purifiers and UV lights are for the other complaint: a household with allergies or a stuffy, stale feel.

### A Spring Tune-Up Before the Heat

AC maintenance includes cleaning, inspection and performance checks, so the system is ready for the summer heat. Routine tune-ups help it run efficiently, prevent costly breakdowns and extend its lifespan.

Spring is the time to catch the things that fail on the first hot day: a weak capacitor, a dirty coil, a drain line that is starting to clog. Finding them in April means you don't learn about them in July, when everyone else's air conditioner is also acting up.
