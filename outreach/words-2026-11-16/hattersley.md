### When water is where it should not be

Fort Wayne cold snaps are when pipes tend to split. Water freezes and expands, and the pipes at the most risk are the ones in outside walls, an unheated garage or a crawl space. A pipe that froze may not leak until it thaws, so a wet ceiling the next afternoon can trace back to the night before.

Every household member who might need it should know where the main shutoff valve is, usually where the water line enters the house, often in the basement. Closing it stops the flow while you wait for help. If it will not close, or it leaks, that valve is itself a repair.

### When the furnace stops in a Fort Wayne winter

Furnaces tend to fail on the coldest nights, after running almost nonstop for days. A house can lose several degrees an hour once the heat stops, depending on the insulation and the wind. Water pipes along outside walls can freeze if the cold lasts, so a no-heat call is worth making early.

A high-efficiency furnace makes water as it burns fuel and drains it through a small plastic line. That line can freeze in a cold basement or garage, or when it was installed without enough slope, and the furnace usually shuts itself down. A repair may get the furnace running that night, and a change to how the line is run may be the lasting fix.

### Before you call

Check three things. Is the thermostat set to heat and a few degrees above the room temperature, with fresh batteries if it takes them? Has a breaker tripped, or the switch near the furnace been flipped? Is the filter clogged? Hold it up to the light, and replace it if you cannot see through it. If the furnace starts and stops again, call us. Pressing reset over and over rarely fixes the cause.

### What usually fails in a furnace

Most no-heat calls on a gas furnace come down to a short list:

- **The igniter,** which lights the burners, wears out.
- **The flame sensor,** a thin rod that confirms the flame, gets coated and shuts the burners off after a few seconds.
- **The inducer motor,** a small fan that clears exhaust, and the **blower motor,** which pushes warm air through the ducts.
- **The control board,** the circuit board that runs the whole sequence. Many furnaces flash a numbered code on it, and the meaning differs by brand.
- **The limit switch,** a safety switch that shuts the burners off when the furnace overheats, often because of a clogged filter.

### Heat pumps

A heat pump heats by moving warmth from outdoors in, and cools the same way in reverse. In freezing weather it periodically melts frost off its outdoor coil, so some steam or dripping is often normal. In the coldest weather many heat pumps switch to electric backup heat, which costs more to run, so a jump in the electric bill is worth asking about.

### Air conditioning in humid heat

Indiana summers bring humid heat, and an air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air as well as cooling it. When one stops, a house often turns clammy before it turns hot. Most no-cooling calls come down to a weak capacitor, the part that gives a motor its starting jolt, a worn contactor, the switch that sends power to the outdoor unit, a failed fan motor, a clogged drain line or a refrigerant leak. An air conditioner does not use up its refrigerant, so a system that needs more has a leak, and adding refrigerant without finding the leak usually means doing it again.

If you see ice on the indoor coil or the thick copper line, turn the system off and let it thaw before calling. Do not chip it off.

### Repair or replace?

Age alone does not decide it. A furnace commonly lasts 15 to 20 years, and an air conditioner 12 to 15. A failed igniter, motor or capacitor is usually worth repairing at any age. A common rule of thumb is that when a repair costs about half the price of a new system and the system is near the end of its life, replacing it makes more sense. Systems built before about 2010 often use R-22, an older refrigerant whose production ended in 2020, so a leak repair on one can take a large share of the cost of a new system.

One failure usually ends the discussion: a cracked heat exchanger, the metal chamber where burner heat passes to your air. A crack can let combustion gases mix with the air you breathe, and a technician who finds one will usually recommend replacement. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call 911 before you call us.
