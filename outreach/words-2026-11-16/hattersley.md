# Commercial Plumbing in Fort Wayne, IN — A. Hattersley & Sons

Commercial plumbing is not a bigger version of the plumbing in a house. A hospital, school or plant has hundreds of fixtures, long pipe runs through several floors, rules that building inspectors check, and people using the building while the work goes on. Our union-trained plumbers handle that range, and we keep adopting newer technology to deliver innovative, cost-effective and sustainable plumbing systems.

### Water into the building

Domestic water systems are the potable, or drinkable, water lines that feed sinks, restrooms, kitchens and equipment. We install and upgrade them. In commercial buildings the trouble is often one of sizing: a line sized for the original use may not keep up after an owner adds fixtures or changes how a floor is used. Before an upgrade, it usually pays to check whether the existing line can supply the new layout.

### Drains, grease and stormwater

Sanitary waste and drainage systems carry used water out of the building. Stormwater systems carry rain off roofs and parking lots, and when roof drains or storm piping are undersized or partly blocked, the first sign is often standing water on a flat roof or in a basement after a heavy storm.

Our food service plumbing covers gas lines, grease interceptors and more. A grease interceptor is a tank that catches cooking fat before it can harden in the sewer line. Restaurants and cafeterias usually need one, local rules often set its size and how often it must be cleaned, and fat that gets past a full one is a common reason commercial kitchens have repeat drain backups.

### Natural gas

We install natural gas systems, which feed boilers, kitchen equipment and heaters. Gas piping is usually pressure-tested and inspected before it goes into service, and local code decides the details.

### Medical gas

Medical gas systems are the piping that carries oxygen and other gases to patient rooms and operating rooms. We install and certify them. Hospitals and surgery centers usually require this piping to be tested and certified before patients are connected to it, because a leak or a wrong gas can hurt a patient. Parkview Regional ONE, a $40 million orthopaedic surgery center on our project list, has eight operating suites and procedure rooms, and a building like that depends on this piping.

### Backflow and leak detection

A cross-connection is any place where drinking water could meet something else, such as a boiler, an irrigation line or a chemical tank. Backflow prevention uses a valve that stops that water from flowing backward into the drinking supply, and we do backflow prevention and cross-connection testing. Water utilities and local codes often require these valves to be tested on a schedule, often every year, and the test report is paperwork a building owner usually needs to keep.

We also do leak detection and system diagnostics. In a large building a leak often shows up far from its source, running along a pipe or through a ceiling, and finding the source before opening a wall usually costs less than guessing.

### Inspections and older buildings

Our installations and inspections are code-compliant. In a new or renovated building, plumbing is usually inspected twice: once with the pipes in place and the walls still open, and again at the end. Because walls are usually closed right after the first inspection, a failed one can hold up the trades that follow, so getting the pipework right the first time matters to the whole schedule. In older buildings, owners often find drain lines or supply pipes that have worn thin or filled with scale, and repeated repairs on the same run are often a reason to replace that run instead of patching it again.

### Plumbing in a building people are using

Monroe Primary School in South Bend was a complete heating, cooling and plumbing renovation of a historic two-story building, finished in 18 months at a cost of $4.5 million. It included two 125-ton chillers, which make cold water to cool the building, five boilers, and a new kitchen suite. In schools, hospitals and offices, plumbing work is often phased by wing or floor, with shutoffs timed for nights, weekends or summer breaks, so the people inside keep running water.

## Commercial HVAC — Commercial HVAC in Fort Wayne, IN — A. Hattersley & Sons

# Commercial HVAC in Fort Wayne, IN — A. Hattersley & Sons

In a commercial building, heating and cooling usually means a central plant rather than one furnace and one air conditioner: boilers that make hot water or steam, chillers that make cold water, and fans that move air through ducts. We install these systems for owners, general contractors and engineers, designed with safety, efficiency and long-term reliability in mind.

**Buttons:** Call (260) 341-0530 / Commercial Plumbing

### Cooling

Chilled water systems cool a building with cold water pumped to air handlers, which are large indoor units with a fan and a coil. They are usually found in larger buildings such as hospitals, schools and campuses, because water carries heat well and can be piped to many rooms. Packaged and split systems are smaller. A packaged system is one self-contained unit, often on the roof, and a split system has an indoor part and an outdoor part.

### Heating

Hot water and heating systems, and hydronic heating and cooling, use water carried through pipes to coils, radiators or unit heaters. We also work on high- and low-pressure steam. Many older buildings were built on steam. Converting to hot water is often done during a major renovation, because hot water is usually easier to control and the same piping can often be set up to carry cooling too.

### Moving and cleaning the air

Rooftop units are self-contained heating and cooling units on the roof. Variable air volume, or VAV, systems adjust how much air each room gets instead of running at full flow all day, which usually saves energy. Ventilation and exhaust systems bring in fresh air and move out stale, hot or smelly air, in kitchens, labs and restrooms. Make-up air systems replace the air that exhaust fans remove, because a building that is pulled too far under outside pressure can make doors hard to open and equipment hard to run. Dedicated outdoor air systems handle the fresh air separately from room heating and cooling, and energy recovery systems use the outgoing air to warm or cool the incoming air before it enters.

### Rooms that can't drift

Cleanroom and critical environment systems control temperature, humidity and air cleanliness, and often keep air moving from cleaner rooms toward dirtier ones. That matters in operating rooms and laboratories. Precision cooling for server and IT rooms matters because computers give off heat all the time, and a room that loses cooling can overheat equipment quickly. Humidity control matters because air that is too damp encourages mold and air that is too dry can damage materials and equipment.

### A school that stayed comfortable during a major renovation

Carroll High School in Fort Wayne was a $60 million renovation of the whole building, with 200,000 square feet of new space and 402,000 square feet remodeled. We added a boiler room and hot water and chilled water systems throughout, along with new science rooms, classrooms, gyms and administrative offices. The work was done during normal school hours, with big pushes in the summer. Students never went without air conditioning or heating, and the job finished on a tight schedule of two and a half years.

### Air conditioning in under 60 days

For the LaPorte Community School Corporation, we were hired as general contractor, the company that runs the whole job, on the HVAC renovation at Handley Elementary. The building was over 50 years old and had no air conditioning, and the time to finish was less than 60 days. The $1.7 million job took six air handling units, two rooftop units, 19 unit ventilators, which are the classroom heating and cooling units, two boilers, and three chillers with five remote condensers, the outdoor parts that release heat. We changed the steam to hot water, added cooling throughout, and put in a new heating and cooling pipe system along with new lighting, ceilings and offices. A schedule that short usually means planning the work around a summer break.

### Heating and cooling from the ground

Phase I of Indiana Tech's campus-wide geothermal loop has 120 tons of capacity, a ton being a measure of cooling power, from vertical wells 400 feet deep. It serves the renovated Wilfred Uytengsu, Sr. Center and the Zollner Engineering building, and it doubles as a learning tool for students in the energy engineering program. The ground holds a fairly steady temperature, so a geothermal loop can usually take in or give off heat more efficiently than outdoor air can.

### Maintenance before something fails

Our Proactive Predictive Maintenance program uses advanced data and technology to anticipate potential failures before they occur. Regular inspections and early intervention let us address minor issues before they grow. The aim is less downtime, lower maintenance costs, equipment that lasts longer, and maintenance scheduled around how the building is used.

In commercial equipment, the parts that often wear first are fan belts and bearings, coils that clog with dust, drain lines that back up, and refrigerant leaks. Catching them in a scheduled visit is usually cheaper than a failure on the hottest day of the year. When repairs on an aging chiller or boiler keep recurring, owners often ask for a comparison of repair costs against replacement over the equipment's remaining life before deciding.
