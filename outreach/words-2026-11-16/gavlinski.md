### What a Replacement Day Usually Looks Like

A replacement usually starts early. The crew covers the plants and the ground around the house, then strips the old shingles off in sections so the house is never open to the sky longer than it has to be. Once the old layer is gone, the wood underneath is checked, and anything soft gets replaced. Then a waterproof layer goes down, the new shingles go on, and the pieces around pipes, vents and chimneys get fitted.

Many houses are done in a day. A bigger or more complicated roof can take two or three, and a rain delay stretches it. One customer said the job was done a lot sooner than they were told.

### The Wood Under the Old Shingles

The shingles keep the rain off, but the boards or plywood under them hold the roof up. Nails in soft or rotted wood don't hold, and a leak that has been going on for years can hide there until the old roof comes off. That's why the first look at a roof is only an estimate of what is underneath. A fair quote tells you in advance how rotted wood will be handled and what it would cost per board, so a surprise on the day doesn't turn into an argument.

A job that finds a problem and handles it without drama is what customers remember.

### Asphalt Shingles or Metal

We install both. Asphalt shingles are what most houses have. They cost less up front, come in many colors, and in a climate like Central Illinois they usually last somewhere between 20 and 30 years, depending on the product and on how well the attic breathes. Metal roofing usually costs more at the start and often lasts much longer, which can make sense if you plan to stay.

Some shingles carry an impact rating for hail, and a few insurers give a discount for them. Ask your insurer before you pick, because the rules vary.

### Colors and the Look of the House

A new roof is the largest color on the house, so it deserves more than a glance at a small sample. A color that looks gray on a card can look brown on the roof in afternoon sun, and the same color reads differently against brick, siding or trees.

### Large Roofs With Lots of Corners

A roof with many peaks and steep sections takes longer than a plain one, and it costs more because of the extra cutting and fitting. Every corner where two slopes meet is a place where water can get in if the work is rushed.

### What Peoria Summers Do to a Roof

Central Illinois summers are hot and humid, and an attic that can't breathe bakes the shingles from below. Heat is one reason two roofs of the same age can be in very different shape. When a roof is replaced, it is worth asking how the attic will be vented, because the same shingles usually last longer over an attic that breathes. Storms matter too, and the repair page covers what hail and wind do.

You get a free estimate. What goes into it is the size and slope of the roof, the number of corners, how many layers are on now, the material and color you choose, and any rotted wood. ### Questions About Replacement

**Can new shingles go over the old ones?** Sometimes, depending on local rules, but it usually isn't a good idea. The old layer hides the wood underneath, and a second layer adds weight and can shorten the life of the new roof. Taking the old roof off lets the wood get checked.

**When is the best time of year to replace a roof?** Late spring to early fall is usually easiest, because shingles seal best in warm weather. A replacement can still be done in cooler months when there's a good weather window, and a roof that is leaking shouldn't wait for perfect weather.

**Can I stay home during a replacement?** Usually, but it is loud, and the pounding can shake dust loose inside the attic and sometimes knock things off shelves near the ceiling. Pets and young children often do better elsewhere for the day.

### Where Roof Leaks Usually Start

Most leaks begin at an opening, not in the middle of a shingle. The usual suspects are the rubber collar around a plumbing pipe, the place where the roof meets a chimney or wall, the corners where two slopes meet, and skylights. Water also travels. A stain on a bedroom ceiling can be several feet from where the water gets in, because it runs along the wood before it drips.

That is why a repair usually starts with finding the opening, not with putting a patch on the stain.

### Fixing the Area, Not Just the Spot

A quick fix is easy to see: one shingle replaced, a dab of sealant. A lasting repair deals with why the water got in, such as a collar around a pipe that was never secured properly, and that usually means redoing the whole area around it.

> Brian could have just added a shingle and nailed it down, but instead, he redid the whole area to make sure it was secure.
>
> — Hayley M.

### Rotted Wood and the Trim Along the Edge

A leak that runs for a long time soaks the wood under the shingles, and wet wood rots. When that happens, new shingles on top of soft boards only hide the problem. The same goes for the trim board along the roof edge, which gets wet because it sits right where water runs off. Fixing a roof properly means replacing the rotted pieces too.

> They quickly identified all of the roof rot, laid new decking, and a new roof on my garage.
>
> — Dan A.

### Maintenance Keeps Repairs Small

Roof maintenance is on our list too, and it is mostly about the edges. Gutters packed with leaves hold water against the roof edge, where it can back up under the first row of shingles. Limbs that rub the roof wear the surface away, and a small loose area is usually cheap to secure before the next windstorm finds it. A look at the roof once a year, and after any big storm, usually catches these while they are still small.

### After a Storm

Hail, strong wind and fallen limbs can all damage a roof. Hail leaves bruises that wear the gritty surface off shingles. Wind can lift or crease them, and a limb can punch a hole. From the ground you can often see missing shingles or debris, but most damage is only visible up close, and walking on a wet or damaged roof is not safe. Photos from the ground, taken before anything is cleaned up, are useful for an insurance claim. Then call someone to look.

Customers have also had us put an emergency cover on a damaged roof to stop a leak. We also offer a free damage assessment, and we coordinate directly with your insurance provider on the claim.

### Winter on a Damaged Roof

Central Illinois freezes and thaws all winter. Water that gets into a small crack freezes, expands and widens it, which is why an ignored leak often gets worse by spring. A repair made before the cold months is usually easier than one made in a freeze.

### Questions About Repairs

**Should I repair the roof or replace it?** It depends on how much of the roof is affected and how old it is. A repair usually makes sense when the damage is in one area and the rest of the roof is in good shape. When the roof is near the end of its life, or damage is spread across it, a replacement is often the better use of money. An inspection can sort that out.

**How soon after a storm should I call?** Soon. A leak that lasts a few days can soak the wood and the insulation, and a roof with missing shingles is open to the next rain. Take photos from the ground first, then call.

**Can a roof be repaired in cold weather?** Often a temporary fix can stop the leak, since shingles seal best in warm weather and a lasting repair is easier then. A roof that is leaking shouldn't wait for spring.
