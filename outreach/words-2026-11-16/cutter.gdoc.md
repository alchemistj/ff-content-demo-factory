### Concrete for Cedar Rapids Homes

We pour driveways, replace cracked and sinking ones, and add parking areas beside an existing driveway. We pour sidewalks and front steps, patios, garage slabs, and custom outdoor basketball courts. Patios can include extras like a curved edge or a seating wall.

### Why Concrete Cracks and Sinks Here

Cedar Rapids winters freeze and thaw again and again. Water finds a small crack, freezes, expands and widens it, and the next thaw lets in more. Road salt and de-icers often speed up the surface damage, especially on a slab that was never sealed.

Sinking is usually a different problem. In a wet spring, water can wash soil out from under a slab, and one corner settles. A driveway that tilts toward the house or holds puddles is usually telling you about the ground below it, not the concrete on top.

### The Ground Under the Concrete Matters Most

A slab is only as stable as what it sits on. A lasting driveway usually starts with the old slab removed, the soil underneath leveled and a layer of crushed rock packed down hard before any concrete arrives. Concrete poured on soft or wet soil often cracks again within a few seasons.

Slope matters as much as depth. Water should run away from your foundation and garage, often at about a quarter inch of drop for every foot, and it should not collect against either one. If your driveway or patio drains toward the house, ask how the new one will drain before you agree to anything.

### What a Driveway Replacement Looks Like

The old driveway comes out, whether it's cracked concrete or worn asphalt. The ground is leveled, the rock base goes down, and wood forms set the shape and the slope. Then the concrete is poured, smoothed and cut with control joints, which are shallow grooves that give the slab a planned place to crack instead of a random one.

Concrete keeps gaining strength for weeks after it's poured. A new slab is often ready for foot traffic in a day or two, and for cars after about a week, but the timing depends on the weather and the mix, so follow your installer's word on the day.

### Protecting Your Lawn and Existing Driveway

A loaded concrete truck is heavy enough to crack an older driveway or leave ruts in a lawn. How the concrete gets from the truck to the forms is a fair question for any contractor, and so is what goes down over the grass.

### Patios, Steps and Sidewalks

A patio needs the same base and drainage as a driveway, with a gentle slope away from the house. Steps need to be poured to a consistent height, because a step that is off by even half an inch is the one people trip on. Sidewalks in Cedar Rapids often meet the city's right of way near the street, so ask whoever pours yours whether a city permit is needed.

If a patio is a good fit for your yard, a seating wall, an edge in a different material or a curve can change how you use it.

### Composite or Wood?

Composite boards are a mix of wood and plastic, and they're made to resist weather and insects. They don't need staining every year, and many are made partly from recycled material. Wood costs less up front and has a classic look, but it usually needs sealing or staining every year or two to keep it from graying, splintering or rotting.

Composite costs more at the start. Over a deck's life it often costs less to own, because you aren't buying stain and giving up weekends. If you like the feel and look of real wood, we can build in wood too.

### Why We Build With Trex

Trex is a composite decking brand, and it's our first choice for composite. As Trex lists them, its entry-level boards carry a 25-year warranty against fading and staining, its middle tier carries 35 years and its top tier carries 50. Warranties like these cover the boards, not the framing under them or the labor, so ask who covers what.

We keep physical samples so you can see the colors and textures in person. A board looks different in the sun than on a screen.

### Which Boards Fit Your Household

A lower-traffic deck with no pets or small children can do well with an entry-level board, and its cosmetic wear takes longer to bother you. A busy household with kids, dogs or frequent cookouts will usually be happier with a top-tier board, because it scratches less.

### What Iowa Winters Do to a Deck

A deck moves with the seasons. Water gets into joints and fasteners, freezes and thaws, and slowly loosens things that were tight in July. Footings, the concrete piers that hold the posts, generally have to go below the frost line, often about three and a half feet in eastern Iowa, or the deck can heave in winter. The city sets the exact depth.

The most common place for water damage is where the deck attaches to the house, at the board that bolts it to the wall. If that connection isn't built and sealed right, water runs behind it and rots the wall and the deck frame from the inside. A deck that has started to sag or pull away from the house is usually telling you about this spot.

### Rebuild, Resurface or Start Over

A deck frame in good shape can often stay. If only the boards, the railing and the stairs are worn, resurfacing may be all you need, and it usually costs far less than starting over. If the frame is soft, rotted or sized wrong for what it carries, the next layer of boards just hides the problem, and a rebuild is the honest answer.

Old decks also hide surprises. Framing that doesn't suit new boards, or a window covered when the old deck was built, can change the plan once the old boards come off.

### Permits and Footings

Most decks need a building permit, and the city inspects the footings and framing before the boards go down. Ask whoever builds yours who handles the permit and when the inspections happen. A deck that skipped inspections can cause trouble when you sell the house.

### Railings, Stairs and Finishing Touches

Railings change how a deck looks and how open it feels. Common choices are composite rails in black or white, aluminum with round or square spindles, steel mesh, glass panels for an open view, and cable. A cocktail rail adds a wide flat cap that holds a drink, which is handy if you entertain.

Another finish is a picture frame border, a ring of boards laid around the edge of the deck and along each step. It hides the cut ends of the boards and gives the deck a finished edge.
