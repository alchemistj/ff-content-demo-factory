### When a New Roof Makes More Sense Than Another Patch

A repair fits a small problem on a sound roof. A roof that keeps leaking, or has large patches of missing or curling shingles, is often better replaced, and a roof past about 20 years may be due even without a storm. Cost depends on the size of the roof, the material and how complicated the job is, and the estimate is free.

### What We Find Under the Old Roof

Here is one roof we replaced. It leaked in several places. The shingles had been nailed over boards with gaps between them, so the nails had nothing solid to grip, and hundreds of nails had pushed back up through the shingles. The ridge vent, the vent along the peak that lets hot, humid attic air out, had been installed without cutting an opening in the wood beneath it, and the wood had rotted in several areas.

We put new wood panels over the whole roof, about 185 of them, and cut the opening so the ridge vent could let air out. The corners where the roof meets a wall had no step flashing, the overlapping metal pieces that seal that joint, so we installed it. A large natural stone chimney got custom aluminum flashing, cut to fit the stone, which took special equipment to grind the stone for a snug fit. The homeowner chose an impact-resistant Class 3 shingle, an entry-level rating for hail. While the roof was open the homeowner asked about new gutters, so we measured and quoted them, and they chose 6-inch gutters sized for over 5,000 square feet of roof.

Another roof had leaked near the chimney, and wind had lifted part of its older shingles. We replaced the water-damaged roof boards, added protection around the chimney and the other spots that leak, and put on the black shingles the homeowner picked, with black gutters to match.
