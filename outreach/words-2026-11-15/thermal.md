### What we repair

We fix air conditioning systems and the components inside them. That includes the parts that fail most often and the ones that take more skill: motors, capacitors, compressors, evaporators and sensors. Whatever the unit is doing, we trace it to the part that caused it and explain what it takes to put it right.

> Turned out to be rather simple fix ( a fan capacitor) which he repaired within 45 minutes. I am so impressed with the whole process and the friendly folks who helped us. — Bob W.

### Repair or replace

Here is the rule we use. Repair when the problem is a part: a fan capacitor, a motor, a sensor, or a refrigerant leak caught early. Price a new system next to the repair when the unit is old or beyond repair, when the compressor has failed, or when repairs keep coming. Either way, you see both numbers before you decide.

### Is your A/C leaking refrigerant?

Two signs point to a leak: cooling cycles that run longer or take longer to start, and ice on the evaporator coils. If you see either, call us. We will inspect the system and find where the refrigerant is going.

### Ductless splits

A ductless split system can boost your efficiency and maximize your space. We are trained and equipped to repair ductless compressors, evaporators and the other components. We install new ductless split systems and tune up existing ones as part of maintenance.

### Installing a new system

When your current air conditioner is old or beyond repair, we install a replacement. For a new home or building, we fit a system to your construction plans. The size has to match the space, so we size it before you buy: a technician visits, takes the measurements and gives you a quote.

### Chillers for businesses

If you run a building with a chiller, we inspect it, diagnose what is wrong, and repair its components. When a chiller has reached the end of its life, we replace it with a new one. That means your building keeps its cooling through the warm months.

### Your spring tune-up

We recommend an air conditioner inspection at least once a year, preferably in spring. Our summers can get dangerously hot for people and animals, and a tune-up before the heat arrives costs less than an emergency repair. Add a fall check on your heating system and you have the two inspections a year we recommend.

### Keeping your outdoor unit safe in winter

You do not need to do any work on your A/C in winter. If you want to protect it, put a tarp over the outdoor unit in fall and leave it on through winter, so snow and leaves stay out. Take the tarp off before you turn the condenser on again. Running the system with the tarp on can overheat the unit and damage it.
