# Writing the strategy page

How to write the words on a demo's strategy page (`/`): the "Why we built this site" paragraphs, the page cards and the report card. These are Josh's principles, from his review of the Lovable demos on 2026-10-26. Follow the spirit, not the letter. Principles 1 and 5 also apply to anything a prospect or client reads: business pages, outreach emails and texts.

## The approved subheading

Every strategy page hero uses this subheading, word for word:

`Your site shapes your Google ranking, what AI says about your business, and how easy you are to reach. Here’s a demo of what yours could be.`

If the business has no website, start it with "A website" instead of "Your site". Never say the site shapes their reviews. Reviews are earned on Google; the website's jobs are getting found and making it easy to reach out.

## Six principles

1. **Write in the owner's words, not web words.** Never footer, header, hero, H1, CTA, slug, schema, template or redirect. Say where it is on the page: "at the very bottom of the page", "the top of your homepage", "your menu", "the name in your browser tab". If the owner wouldn't say it to their spouse, rewrite it.

2. **Lead with the problem that costs the most calls.** Rank each card's problems in this order and put the costliest first:
   1. Does the top of the homepage say what they do and where, in the words people search? ("fence company", "plumber", plus the town.)
   2. Can someone on a phone reach them in one tap?
   3. Does each money service have its own page?
   4. Proof: reviews and real photos.
   5. Cosmetic: an old copyright year, typos, leftover sample text. These belong in the report card, not at the top of a card.

3. **Say what it costs, not just what's there.** "No headline" is an observation. "Google can't tell you're a plumber in Marshfield" is the cost. Give every problem one clause on why it loses calls or rankings.

4. **Check against the search phrase, not the general topic.** The test is whether the headline contains what a customer types, like "fence company Springfield MO". "Stronger, Safer Fencing Built for the Ozarks" mentions fencing and a region but fails the test: nobody searches "Ozarks". Slogans fail even when they sound strong.

5. **Only claim what you can back up.** No "most searched", "biggest" or "best" without data. State what is verifiably on their site, or reframe to something true ("some of your biggest home jobs").

6. **Every red problem has a visible green fix.** If a card says their homepage never says "fence company", our demo's homepage must say it, and the fix line names it ("A headline that says fence company in Springfield, MO, right at the top."). Anyone who taps "View page" sees the difference.
