# Josh's voice guide

For Fluid Frame blogs, outreach and client notes. Source: 72 posts Josh wrote by hand (2018 to 2023, `docs/voice/preferable-life/posts.md`) plus how he talks in 2026. This guide is about voice only, not topics. His faith and personal posts teach the voice; they don't become business content.

## 1. Voice in 5 lines
- A guy at the next table who has tried it, failed at it, and tells you straight.
- Direct and warm. He says the hard part, then says "I'm by your side."
- Action over theory. Try it, look at the numbers, adjust.
- Nerdy about marketing and data, but he talks like a person, not a deck.
- Laughs at himself first ("I am definitely the spreadsheet guy!").

## 2. Openings and closings

**How he opens:** a question to the reader, a blunt claim, or a one-word setup. Never a warm-up paragraph.
- "What are you most afraid of?"
- "So your life sucks? Good news: You're not alone."
- Also: "Creativity is a myth." / "Look:" / "Bad thoughts suck…"

**How he closes:** one clear next step, then a human sign-off. Short.
- "Two minutes of running is better than no minutes of running, wishing you would." (then: "Take the first step, and just go.")
- "If I can help out in any way, reach out in the comments. By your side. Josh"
- Also: "Hit me up if you have any questions." / "I'm rooting for ya all the way!" / "Don't drift, direct!!"

## 3. Rhythm
- **Sentences:** mostly short. Many are one line on their own. A long run-on shows up when he's fired up, then a short one lands the point ("Look at the suck and own it. You will be glad you did.").
- **Questions:** constant. He asks, then answers. "How's it going? What do you like? What sucks? What rocks?"
- **Lists:** numbered steps and short headers. Plain labels, not clever ones.
- **Humor:** quick and self-aimed. "lol", "Boom!", a one-word reaction. No sarcasm at the reader's expense.
- **Stories:** he proves a point with something that happened to him. "Let me tell you a (true) story…" Small and specific beats big and vague.
- **How personal:** very, in the old posts (job, health, family, what went wrong). In business writing keep the honesty and the "I did this and it cost me" move, drop the private details.

## 4. Words he reaches for, and ones he wouldn't use
**Reaches for:** launch, analyze, adapt, explore, drift vs. direct, "by your side," "come alongside," "hit me up," "boom," "crap," "sucks / rocks," "a week at a time," "the game," "data," "dial in."
He says "you gotta" and "ya." Exclamation marks and the odd ALL CAPS word for emphasis (E-V-E-R-Y-T-H-I-N-G, "TOUGH").

**Would never use** (zero times in 128k words): synergy, leverage, stakeholder, bandwidth, deliverables, circle back, touch base, best-in-class, scalable, robust, ROI, KPI. Also avoid "solutions," "elevate," "unlock," "game-changing partner," and the "we are passionate about" family.

## 5. Then vs. now (2026 wins)
| | 2018 to 2023 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | "Launch, Analyze, Adapt" (still a loose life formula) | "plan is a 4-letter word to me. I'm a launch guy." |
| Motto | "Launch, Analyze, Adapt" (the 2018 draft title) | "explore, analyze, adapt... data answers all" |
| Style | exclamation-heavy, SEO-blog padding ("killer," "dominate," "crush it"), long faith passages | plain, fast, dry. Lets a joke ("Lol") and an image do the work |
| Metaphor | mountain of dirt, spoon, garden | casino, addict: "spins those actions like a... gambling addict in a casino" |
| Business talk | how-to for strangers | owner talking to owner: "we know our stuff," without giving away "the keys to the kingdom" |

**Follow 2026:** shorter, less hype, more "here's what the data says." Keep from the old posts: the questions, the self-teasing, the honest story, the next step. Drop: "killer," "crush," "dominate," stacked exclamation points (!!!), and the long abstract paragraphs.

## 6. Do / don't for business writing
**Do**
- Start with the point or a question the reader is already asking.
- Say what we tried and what the data showed. Numbers over adjectives.
- Admit one thing that didn't work.
- Show we know our stuff without handing over the playbook.
- End with one small action and a person ("Reply here and I'll look at it").
- Allow one light joke. A "Lol" is fine in a note to a client he knows.

**Don't**
- Don't use agency-speak (see section 4) or promise outcomes we can't prove.
- Don't open with "In today's fast-paced digital landscape."
- Don't stack exclamation points or hype words.
- Don't pad. If a sentence adds nothing, cut it.
- Don't use faith language or private stories in client-facing copy.
- Don't write "plan" talk. Say what we'll launch and when we'll check it.

## 7. Before and after

**Blog intro**
- Generic: "In today's competitive landscape, a strong online presence is essential for growth-minded businesses seeking to elevate their brand."
- Josh: "Is your website bringing in calls? Be honest. Most roofers I talk to don't know. Here's how to find out in ten minutes."

**Outreach message**
- Generic: "We'd love to explore a mutually beneficial partnership to unlock synergies and drive ROI for your organization."
- Josh: "Hey, I built a quick mock-up of your site with your logo and photos on it. It'll look a lot better once it's really yours! Lol. Take a look and tell me what you think."

**Client note**
- Generic: "Per our strategic roadmap, we will be implementing a phased optimization initiative, with results to be evaluated at the next review."
- Josh: "We launched the new page Monday. Calls from it are up. Next week we look at the numbers, keep what works, and change what doesn't."
