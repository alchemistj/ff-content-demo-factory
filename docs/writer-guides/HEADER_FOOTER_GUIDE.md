# **Fluid Frame Demo Header & Footer Guide**

## **Purpose**

Write the global business chrome so every business-facing route feels like one coherent production website.  
Header and footer belong to the prospect's business—not Fluid Frame, the audit, or the demo.

## **Inputs**

Use:

> * approved route/page prescription  
> * confirmed business identity and NAP  
> * finished service pages  
> * homepage direction/final copy as it becomes available  
> * confirmed service areas, hours, email, credentials, and offers  
> * FLUID\_FRAME\_DEMO\_WRITING\_GUIDE.md

Do not invent a link, service, offer, or contact detail to make the navigation/footer feel fuller.

# **Header**

## **Brand**

Use the confirmed business name and available verified logo identity.  
Do not invent a slogan, monogram, badge, certification, or “trusted” claim.

## **Navigation**

Keep navigation focused on real built routes.  
Rules:

> * Logo and Home point to /home.  
> * The Strategy Overview at / is not normal business navigation.  
> * Include only routes that actually exist.  
> * Do not add fake service links for future architecture.  
> * Do not link ordinary navigation back to the prospect's old website.

The exact nav order should follow the customer journey and page prescription; it does not need to copy one prior demo.

## **Primary CTA**

Use the approved business action and verified contact hierarchy.  
Do not add:

> * Free Estimate  
> * 24/7  
> * Emergency Service  
> * Same-Day Service  
> * Financing  
> * Warranty

unless that exact concept is supported upstream.

# **Footer**

## **Job of the footer**

The footer should reinforce:

> * who the business is  
> * what it does  
> * where it serves  
> * how to contact it  
> * where the key pages are

It is a trust/navigation anchor, not a second homepage.

## **Useful content when confirmed**

The footer may include:

> * business name/logo  
> * 1–2 sentence business identity statement  
> * phone  
> * email  
> * address  
> * hours  
> * service links  
> * service-area summary  
> * brief verified credentials or ownership facts  
> * copyright/service-market context

Use only what the evidence supports.

## **Flexible structure**

Do not force a four-column footer.  
Use whatever grouping makes the actual information easy to scan.  
The writer defines information hierarchy and labels; the builder owns visual composition.

## **Do not sell the website**

Never use footer/header language such as:

> * Professional Website Experience  
> * Clear Service Information  
> * Clean Mobile-Friendly Experience  
> * Easy-To-Find Contact Options  
> * This Website Mockup  
> * This site was designed to

Those are Fluid Frame attributes, not business differentiators.

## **Reviews in global chrome**

The page review-floor rules do not apply to header/footer.  
Primary review proof belongs inside sales-page content where it can sit near the claim it proves.  
Do not invent star badges, ratings, or review counts in global chrome.  
If the approved design later uses a verified rating signal, it must preserve the true context and come from verified evidence.

## **Consistency**

Header/footer copy should use the same:

> * business name  
> * primary phone/contact hierarchy  
> * service names  
> * service-area terminology  
> * CTA language  
> * capitalization conventions

as the finished pages.  
Do not introduce a second naming system or alternate business voice in the footer.

## **Header/footer hard failures**

Revise if:

> * demo/audit/Fluid Frame commentary appears  
> * Logo/Home points to / instead of /home  
> * a nav/footer link points to a nonexistent route  
> * contact details conflict with confirmed NAP  
> * an unsupported service, offer, credential, or location appears  
> * website-quality language is used as a business differentiator  
> * the Strategy Overview appears as ordinary business navigation  
> * global chrome invents a review/rating badge  
> * footer copy bloats into another homepage instead of serving trust/navigation
