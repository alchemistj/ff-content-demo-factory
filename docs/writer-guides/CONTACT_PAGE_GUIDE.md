# **Fluid Frame Demo Contact Page Guide**

## **Purpose**

The contact page removes friction from the next step.  
It is not a second homepage and not another sales essay.  
A visitor who reaches /contact should quickly understand how to contact the business, what information is useful to provide, and any confirmed service-area or hours context that helps them act.

## **Tone**

Short, practical, confident, human.  
Do not spend paragraphs reselling the company.

## **Hero**

Keep the hero simple:

> * Contact-oriented eyebrow  
> * Clear H1  
> * One short sentence on the best next step  
> * Primary contact CTA  
> * Secondary CTA only when it adds a genuinely different useful path

Do not invent response-time, same-day, emergency, or callback promises.

## **Core content**

Use writer judgment, but an effective contact page usually needs only a few jobs:

### **Clear next step**

Tell the visitor whether to call, use the visual request form, or choose between those paths.

### **What to have ready**

When useful, give a short list such as:

> * service address  
> * service/problem type  
> * equipment/property type when relevant  
> * a short description of what is happening

Adapt this to the actual business rather than copying a universal list.

### **Service area / hours**

Use only confirmed facts.

### **Visual form support**

If the demo includes a visual-only contact form, write labels/supporting copy that does not pretend the form is connected to a CRM, email system, or real success workflow.  
Do not write fake success-state promises.

## **Reviews**

The contact page is primarily a utility page, not automatically a high-proof sales page.  
Follow the evidence grade supplied upstream if one is assigned.

> * Grade A: 3 reviews  
> * Grade B: 2 reviews  
> * Grade C: one suitable review when one exists, zero when none exists

Do not force a review onto the contact page merely to decorate it if the upstream evidence grade does not require one and it does not help the contact decision.  
If a review is used, the same rules apply:

> * no adjacent reviews  
> * no testimonial block  
> * within roughly 150 words of the claim it supports  
> * exact/faithful verified wording and attribution

## **FAQ**

Three to five practical questions is a useful target when FAQ helps.  
Good topics:

> * service area  
> * hours when confirmed  
> * what information to include  
> * call vs. message  
> * what kind of jobs the business handles

Do not invent response times or guarantees.

## **Length**

The page should normally remain intentionally lean: roughly **150–350 body words excluding FAQ**.  
Overwriting the contact page is a defect.

## **Contact-page hard failures**

Revise if:

> * the page becomes a second homepage  
> * it contains long “why choose us” reassurance sections  
> * it invents contact details, hours, response times, or service promises  
> * visual-form copy implies a real submission backend that does not exist  
> * the next action is unclear  
> * the page violates an assigned review grade or review fidelity rule
