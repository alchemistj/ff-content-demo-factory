# Fluid Frame Demo Contact Page Guide

The contact page removes friction from the next step. It is not a second homepage and not another sales essay.

A visitor who reaches it should quickly understand how to contact the business, what information is useful to provide, and any confirmed service-area or hours context that helps them act.

Read `FLUID_FRAME_DEMO_WRITING_GUIDE.md` and the contact examples before writing.

## Tone

Short, practical, confident, human. Do not spend paragraphs reselling the company.

## What to draw from the examples

Window Dudes `/contact/` and Greene Planet `/springfield/contact/` are both lean utility pages with a clear next step.

SRA’s Springfield contact example is **not** available in this set. Do not substitute SRA’s statewide `/contact/` and do not invent a Springfield contact page from it. See `examples/approved-copy/SOURCE_MANIFEST.md`.

## Hero

A contact-oriented opening, a clear H1, one short sentence on the best next step, and a primary contact CTA. Add a secondary CTA only when it is a genuinely different useful path.

Do not invent response-time, same-day, emergency, or callback promises.

## Core jobs

- Tell the visitor whether to call, use the visual request form, or choose between those paths
- When useful, a short list of what to have ready, adapted to this business
- Confirmed service area and hours only
- Form labels that do not pretend a visual-only form is connected to a CRM or a guaranteed success workflow

## Reviews and FAQ

The contact page is a utility page. Do not force quotations onto it merely to decorate it.

Use a quotation when it genuinely helps the contact decision and the evidence supports it. The same fidelity rules apply.

A few practical questions can help (area, hours, what to include, call vs. message). Do not invent response times or guarantees. Packet FAQ counts and word targets are advisory. Overwriting the contact page is a defect.
