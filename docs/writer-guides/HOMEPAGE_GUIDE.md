# **Fluid Frame Demo Homepage Guide**

## **Purpose**

The homepage is the business/category front door at /home.  
It should quickly tell a local customer:

> * who this business is  
> * what it does  
> * where it works  
> * why the customer should trust it  
> * which service path fits  
> * what to do next

It is broader than a service page and should not duplicate service-page depth.

## **Special input: finished service pages**

The homepage writer receives the completed service pages before writing.  
Use them as continuity context.  
The homepage should:

> * sound like the same business  
> * summarize those services without copying their paragraphs  
> * link/rout the visitor into them  
> * preserve the distinctions those pages already established

Do not reopen their strategy or rewrite them inside the homepage.

## **Search intent**

The homepage normally owns the broad company/category \+ primary market intent.  
Do not make it compete with a dedicated service page by turning the H1 into one narrow service keyword.  
Follow the approved prescription when an exception exists.

## **Hero**

Use the shared Fluid Frame shape:

> * useful eyebrow  
> * short, literal company/category \+ location H1 \+ Company Name  
> * 2–3 short sentences of business-specific trust/differentiation  
> * primary \+ secondary CTA with a real commitment gradient

The hero should orient the visitor in seconds.  
Use the strongest broad proof, not every credential at once.

# **Hero → First Section**

The hero shape is the page's true opening. There is no separate headerless paragraph between the hero and the first body section — the page moves directly from hero into the first H2.

That first H2's opening paragraph:

* Includes the primary keyword in the first sentence  
* Runs 40–60 words  
* Plainly answers what the service is and where it's offered

This is only the *lead-in* paragraph, not the entire section. It sets up the section's point — the section itself continues with whatever additional content that topic needs (process detail, differentiators, proof points, etc.) before the next H2 begins. The 40–60 word count applies to that first paragraph only, never to the section as a whole.

## **What the body should accomplish**

Use writer judgment on section order and format, but make sure the homepage covers the jobs that matter.  
Typical jobs include:

### **Business identity**

Establish who the company is, what it does, who it helps, and where it serves.

### **Services**

Show the confirmed service set in a scan-friendly way.  
The built service pages should be easy to find and clearly named.  
Other confirmed services may be mentioned without inventing fake routes.

### **Differentiation**

Give specific reasons to choose the business based on real evidence.  
Do not use website-quality claims such as:

> * professional website experience  
> * clean mobile experience  
> * clear service information  
> * simple next steps

Those describe Fluid Frame's work, not the business.

### **Service area**

Use only confirmed geography.

### **Human/business story**

Use ownership, team, history, mission, or experience only when verified and genuinely useful to a customer.

### **Final action**

Make the path to call/contact obvious.

## **Reviews: distributed company proof**

Follow the page evidence grade assigned upstream.

### **Grade A**

3 reviews: one lead \+ two supporting.

### **Grade B**

2 reviews: one lead \+ one supporting.

### **Grade C**

Use one suitable review when one exists; zero only when no suitable review exists.  
If suitable anchors exist and the homepage uses zero reviews, it fails.

### **What reviews should prove on a homepage**

Homepage reviews often work best when they establish broad confidence:

> * trust  
> * workmanship  
> * responsiveness  
> * communication  
> * pricing fairness  
> * repeat-customer behavior  
> * breadth of work

A service-specific review may also work well when it introduces a built service page.

### **Placement**

> * Start from the prescription's recommended first review.  
> * No two reviews adjacent.  
> * No dedicated testimonial block.  
> * Keep each review within roughly 150 words of the nearby claim it supports.  
> * Do not force every review to talk about the same service.

## **Avoid homepage/service duplication**

A homepage service summary should create interest and route the visitor.  
A service page should answer the deeper service-specific questions.  
If a homepage section starts explaining the same decision at the same depth as a service page, shorten it and link onward.

## **FAQ**

Five to seven useful buyer questions is a good target when the evidence and customer journey support it.  
Use questions that help visitors choose a service path, understand coverage, or decide how to contact the business.  
Do not create blog-style educational filler.

## **Length**

Roughly **350–550 body words excluding FAQ** remains a useful homepage range for this demo format.  
The goal is breadth with authority, not encyclopedic depth.  
Do not add generic paragraphs merely to make the homepage longer.

## **Images**

The writer may identify useful approved prospect image candidates for below-fold sections, especially when an image demonstrates a real service or business story.  
Do not prescribe layout and do not select a prospect-hosted image as the hero.

## **Homepage hard failures**

Revise if:

> * demo/audit/Fluid Frame language appears  
> * H1 owns a service-page keyword instead of the homepage intent  
> * business identity, service, geography, offer, or proof is unsupported  
> * the page fails to route clearly into the prescribed service pages and contact path  
> * the homepage copies large chunks of service-page content  
> * differentiators describe the website instead of the business  
> * suitable review anchors exist and no reviews are used  
> * review grade floor, spacing, proximity, or fidelity fails  
> * the page reads like a generic local-service template after removing the business name/city
